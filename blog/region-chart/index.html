      How the region fits together, and where your piece plugs in

      One map of how The Upskilling Labs of Greater Washington runs today, what’s planned, and four places where value leaks. For the core contributors who will fix them.

      The Wayfinding pod, Nov 7, 2026. A six-minute read.

      Why you’re getting a map

      You hold a piece of the region: a pod, a project, a kit, a session, a corner of the app, a seat on the steering committee. Your piece makes sense from where you sit. From anywhere else it’s hard to see how it connects to the rest, and that goes for the people working right beside you.

      So we drew all of it on one page, the parts and what passes between them. Use it to find your piece, to see what feeds it and what it feeds, and to spot the gaps nobody owns yet.

      The region on one map

      Boxes are parts of the region. Lines are what moves between them, in four kinds, and the buttons let you look at one at a time: people, work and code, knowledge, and money and support. Dashed means planned. Red dotted lines end in a circle where people leave.

      The label on each line is our best reading. If you run the thing a line touches, you know more than the map does, and the last section shows how to fix it. On a phone, scroll sideways or open the map on its own page.

      What each kind of line says today

      People first. The blue path runs from newcomer to session to member to Cycle to alumni. Two lines already run past the end of it, and nobody had drawn them: alumni who stay on teach the sessions, and they take a stuck team’s question. Mentors come in after a team has used up its own resources and its pod’s, never before. The plan makes that path deliberate, and adds one more line, to employers, who haven’t been asked yet.

      Work and code. National builds and maintains OLOS. The region runs a fork of it, which the steering committee and the regional association keep current. What the Cycle builds goes into the fork, and what holds up is offered upstream, where the Board decides what reaches the core. That makes two bars, ours and national’s, and the map draws them as two arrows. Kits and modules are planned to travel the same road, under the same ladder of contributor, reviewer, committer and maintainer.

      Knowledge. National gives the practice and the Cycle’s design. The region gives the Cycle problems worth its time, and the Cycle gives back finds, paradoxes and what each team learned. Sessions hand members a tool or a way of working. The planned teach-back line is the one to watch: each participant teaches one public workshop on a skill they used, which is how what a team learned reaches the next room.

      Money and support. Grants, contracts and partnerships go through national, not through us. What the region has in hand is hours and rooms, meaning volunteer time and library space. The plan is sustaining regional members who give to Greater Washington, held in the region’s own account inside national. It’s dashed because terms aren’t agreed, and standing still comes from what you contribute.

      The big outline is the open question here. The Upskilling Labs holds both levels for now, and a steering committee is working toward a chartered regional organization. Where the line between regional and national roles falls is being written this quarter, and a longer-term structure is being co-designed during this Cycle.

      Four leaks, as open questions

      Each red line is a place where value leaves the region. Each one is also a question that somebody has to own.

      People come once and don’t return. We can’t say who or why, because we don’t yet measure who we serve. That’s a gap in the app as much as in the program. Counting who comes by area, in totals that can’t point at a person, is the first promise the app can keep.

      At the vote, a project that doesn’t win takes its team with it, and the people who gathered around it are left without one. Teams form after the second vote. Whether people could start forming teams in parallel, before it, is still open.

      After a Cycle, you leave with a public record of what you built and no obvious next door. No between-cycles experience is mapped, and employers haven’t been asked to read the record. Is the next door a role, a program or a path from one to the other? Somebody has to say.

      No earlier Cycle has a published account of what it found. Our nights and weekends went into the thing that runs, and the write-up was nobody’s job. It gates more than the others, because every line of funding asks for the record, and so will the next region.

      The dashed lines are open to build

      Every dashed line on the map is a plan that someone could pick up.

      Neighborhood workshops would let a small team host a session at their own library from a kit. A kit holds the agenda, the materials, notes for whoever leads, and the room setup, so a stranger can run it. Salons are evenings built around one question, with people who’ve done a Cycle talking to people who haven’t. Meetups are bigger events co-hosted with libraries, nonprofits, civic tech groups and employers. Summits are national’s to run, and the plan is real DC-style ones with the Cycle’s showcase on the program.

      Learning modules would be written once and taught by any trainer. A small group is designing the mentor program now: a bench, a way to ask, and a way to say yes. The app, the kits and the modules all take open contribution, and each one needs maintainers who hold a high bar, since what gets merged is what the next region inherits.

      Correct it, or claim a line

      The map is a data file. Every box and line lives in one JSON file in the pod’s repo, so correcting a label or adding a missing line is a pull request.

      If you own something and the map has it wrong, the map is wrong. If a dashed line is yours to build, say so in an issue and we’ll mark it.
