      One person’s way through The Labs, left to right

      The region chart shows the parts. This map follows one participant from first hearing of us to after a Cycle, with what people told us at every step.

      The Wayfinding pod, Nov 8, 2026. A five-minute read.

      Why follow one person

      The Wayfinding pod asks how people find their way around The Labs, and where they lose it. Our region chart answers from above: the parts of the region and what moves between them. This map answers from the ground. It follows one participant, step by step, and asks what you do at each step, where it happens, who’s with you, and what people who’ve been there told us.

      Read it as your own route. Find the step you’re on, and look at what comes next.

      The journey on one map

      The blue line is the journey, left to right. Dashed means planned and not running yet. Red rings are where people leave. The arcs above the line are people skipping ahead or coming back. The shaded columns are the Cycle.

      The strip across the top shows the shape of the work. Each diamond opens up, with many options on the table, then narrows to a choice the group makes together: problem situations narrow at Vote 1, research narrows to the paradox, frames narrow at Vote 2, and prototypes narrow to what you show. Before the Cycle, the dashed funnel is the many ways people find us, all ending at the same door. After it, the work fans out again into wherever people take it.

      The bottom row quotes the pod’s statements: A is the alumni survey of the first three Cycles, O is organizers. Where it says nothing heard yet, we haven’t asked anyone who was there.

      What the map shows

      Most people start at step 6. Today the Cycle is the front door, so the arc from hearing of us jumps straight to it, past the sessions, the profile and the prep. Organizers said it plainly: the Cycle is the point of entry. The plan is for the Cycle to be something you build up to, and steps 3 to 5 are where that would happen. One of them doesn’t exist yet.

      The complaints pile up inside the Cycle. Nine of the seventeen quotes sit in steps 6 to 8, and nearly all of them ask the same thing: what am I meant to do now? Joining a pod, then a team, is where people lose the thread. That is the pod’s problem, stated by the people who lived it. Put the quotes under the strip and they line up with the narrow points. People get lost where the group closes on a choice, and they have to find their place in what was chosen.

      Three steps have no voice at all. Nobody has told us how they first heard of us, what a profile did for them, or what the showcase felt like. And the survey only reached people who stayed, so the red rings come from what others saw of people leaving, never from anyone who left.

      The arcs on the right are the hopeful part. Alumni come back to teach a session or take a stuck team’s question. That loop runs today without anyone running it.

      Open questions for the next draft

      What does a profile get you before your first Cycle? Right now, mostly an account. If it helped you plan your upskilling, step 3 would stop being a form and start being a reason to come back.

      What is step 5? Short prep before a Cycle is planned and unbuilt. It is also the likeliest fix for the quotes in step 6.

      What happens at step 10 for people who don’t stay? Projects that outlast a Cycle and employers who read the record are both planned, and neither has an owner yet.

      Who did we lose? Every red ring needs a voice from someone who left. Talking to them is the next round of interviews.
