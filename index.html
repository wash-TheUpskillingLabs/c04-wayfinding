The Upskilling Labs of Greater Washington

Wayfinding

How do people find their way between the parts of The Labs, and what do they need to see to choose the route that fits them?

Why this problem

The Upskilling Labs started in DC Public Library branches in 2025. In under a year, run entirely on volunteer hours, about a thousand people have come through a pilot and three Build Cycles.

When people tell us what got in their way, it's rarely what happened in the room. It's the spaces between: how to get started, what to do this week, how to join a team, where to go when the Cycle ends. Much of that comes from tools and platforms we built in a hurry, while running Cycles at the same time.

This fall, for the first time, we're running a Cycle on The Labs itself, so we can fix the gaps instead of putting out fires every day.

What Wayfinding works on

The moments between the parts of The Labs: the first time someone walks in, getting ready for a Cycle, the weeks between sessions, joining a team, the end of a Cycle and whatever comes after.

It isn't just us. Programs everywhere lose most people at the edges.

The research points at three things people need to see when they reach one of those moments: where they are and what's next, that they're ready for it, and who's going with them.

What people at The Labs told us

Asked what mattered most, almost all of them named the people they worked with.

Twelve isn't many, and they're the ones who stayed. People who got lost at the door didn't answer. They're who we want to hear from next.

What we're building

This Cycle, The Upskilling Labs of Greater Washington is working on The Labs itself, in three Research Pods on our own copy of OLOS, the open platform The Labs runs on. Scaffolding works on what happens inside each part. Front Door works on where it all lives. Wayfinding works on the routes between them. On Nov 14, projects form inside the pods, each led by a builder in residence.

On Dec 8 the three pods show what we built. Then we offer it to the national project, and the national Board decides what meets the standard for the core every region runs on. We've committed to getting at least three projects into the core. We're aiming for all of them, so the next region starts where we finished.

We're building toward a place where anyone can see where they are, choose where to go next, and find the people going there with them. You can start anywhere: a Slack thread, a GitHub issue, a calendar invite, a link a friend sent. Wherever you start, it brings you into the room with your pod, your team, your branch. And what one person learns at a branch library on a Tuesday night is something every other community can use by Wednesday.

That doesn't exist yet. Help us build it.

Help us find out

We've read 54 programs that tried something like this and mapped 50 organizations around the problem. What we're missing is people who've lived it here. You don't need to code, and an hour helps. Pick one:

  • Tell us about the last time you didn't know what came next at The Labs, and what you did.
  • If you came once and didn't come back, tell us what happened. That's the story we have least of.
  • List every tool you touched at The Labs, in order, from first hearing about it to your last contribution.

Comment on Start here. A maintainer will reply by the next Monday with one thing to find out. What you learn goes into the record with your name on the contributors list.

Go further: build with us

Every project in this pod is led by a builder in residence. We're looking for three more.

A builder in residence gives 4 to 6 hours a week through Dec 8, mostly nights and weekends, with a coding agent doing the heavy lifting. Until Nov 14 you help run the research. Then you lead one project with a team you recruit, ship a working change to how The Labs runs, and present it on Dec 8 for the national core.

If you've been waiting for a reason to build something real with people who'll actually use it, this is it.
