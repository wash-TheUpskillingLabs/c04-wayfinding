      People don’t get lost in the room. They get lost on the way to the next one.

      Anyone should be able to see where they are and choose where to go next. We’re rebuilding The Labs so they can, from the first workshop to whatever comes after. Follow along, contribute, or lead a project.

      What we believe

      Anyone should be able to see where they are, choose where to go next, and find the people going there with them.

      When that’s true, people stop waiting to be told what’s next. They form teams around problems they care about, learn by building, and carry what they learned somewhere new. What one person figures out at a branch library on a Tuesday night, another community can use by Wednesday.

      That’s the kind of agency The Labs exists to build. Right now, the routes between our own parts get in its way.

      Next stopWhere people get lost

      Where people get lost

      The Upskilling Labs started in DC Public Library branches in 2025. In under a year, run entirely on volunteer hours, about a thousand people have come through a pilot and three Build Cycles.

      When people tell us what got in their way, it’s rarely what happened in the room. It’s the spaces between: how to get started, what to do this week, how to join a team, where to go when the Cycle ends. Much of that comes from tools and platforms we built in a hurry, while running Cycles at the same time.

      It isn’t just us. Programs everywhere lose most people at the edges.

      What people at The Labs told us

      Asked what mattered most, almost all of them named the people they worked with.

      Twelve isn’t many, and they’re the ones who stayed. People who got lost at the door didn’t answer. They’re who we want to hear from next.

      How we’re working

      We use frame creation, a method from the designer Kees Dorst. Hard problems stay hard because everyone looks at them the same way, so we study the problem properly before we try to solve it, and we do it in the open.

      1. Read what’s been tried. 54 programs and 50 organizations so far, from Google Summer of Code to 4-H.
      2. Hear from people who lived it. Especially the ones who came once and didn’t come back.
      3. Name the paradox. On Nov 7, the two true things that pull against each other.
      4. Try new frames. Quick prototypes, each put in front of three real people.
      5. Build what holds up. Working changes to how The Labs runs, shipped by Dec 8.

      Every source, interview note and prototype goes into the pod’s public repo, so anyone can check the work or pick it up.

      Next stopWhat we’re building

      What we’re building

      This fall, for the first time, The Upskilling Labs of Greater Washington is running a Cycle on The Labs itself, so we can fix the gaps instead of putting out fires every day. Three Research Pods are working on our own copy of OLOS, the open platform The Labs runs on. Scaffolding works on what happens inside each part. Front Door works on where it all lives. Wayfinding works on the routes between them: getting started, what comes next, and where people go when a Cycle ends.

      You’ll be able to start anywhere, a Slack thread, a GitHub issue, a calendar invite, a link a friend sent, and land in the right room with your pod, your team and your branch.

      On Dec 8 the three pods show what we built. Then we offer it to the national project, and the national Board decides what meets the standard for the core every region runs on. We’ve committed to getting at least three projects into the core. We’re aiming for all of them, so the next region starts where we finished.

      None of it exists yet. That’s the part you can help with.

      Follow the pod

      The lightest way in. Everything we find goes into the pod’s repo on GitHub and onto the blog: the research, the paradox on Nov 7, the projects that form on Nov 14, and what ships on Dec 8. Follow it and you’ll see each step as it happens.

      Follow on GitHub

      On the repo page, choose Watch, then Custom, then Issues and Pull requests. It’s free; you need a GitHub account. Already in The Labs’ Slack? Say hello in #c04-all.

      Next stopBecome a contributor

      Become a contributor

      We’ve read 54 programs that tried something like this and mapped 50 organizations around the problem. What we’re missing is people who’ve lived it here. You don’t need to code. Pick one:

      • Tell us about the last time you didn’t know what came next at The Labs, and what you did.
      • If you came once and didn’t come back, tell us what happened. That’s the story we have least of.
      • List every tool you touched at The Labs, in order, from first hearing about it to your last contribution.

      Comment on Start here. A maintainer will reply by the next Monday with one thing to find out. What you learn goes into the record with your name on the contributors list.

      Comment on Start here

      New to GitHub? Say so in your comment. We’ll walk you through it.

      Next stopLead a project

      Lead a project

      On Nov 14, projects form inside the pod, each led by a builder in residence. We’re looking for three.

      A builder in residence gives 4 to 6 hours a week through Dec 8, mostly nights and weekends, with a coding agent doing the heavy lifting. Until Nov 14 you help run the research. Then you lead one project with a team you recruit, ship a working change to how The Labs runs, and present it on Dec 8 for the national core.

      What you leave with: a team you recruited, a change The Labs actually runs on, a public record of how you got there, and a shot at the national core. It’s the kind of work you talk about in an interview, not a certificate you attach to an application.

      If you’ve been waiting for a reason to build something real with people who’ll actually use it, this is it.
