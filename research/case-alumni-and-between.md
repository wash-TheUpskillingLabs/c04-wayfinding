# Case: AmeriCorps and Teach For America alumni, what a service year leaves behind eight years on

**Kind:** paradigmatic (two of the largest and best-measured US alumni populations), with a failure strand (fade and attrition)
**Bears on:** post-cycle, activation-mobilization, facilitators
**Product part:** post-between | facilitators
**Logged:** 2026-11-06 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
Both AmeriCorps and Teach For America (TFA) run a fixed-term service program (one year; two years) and then keep an "alumni" identity open indefinitely. AmeriCorps commissioned a comparison-group longitudinal study of alumni eight years after enrollment; TFA's alumni are the subject of a large independent cohort survey on how long they stay in teaching and of TFA's own voluntary alumni surveys. Both are chosen here because they have numbers; Recurse Center ("Never Graduate") and Y Combinator's alumni network are noted only briefly for lack of independent data.

## 2. Where it came from
AmeriCorps: the Corporation for National and Community Service's *Still Serving* study (Abt Associates, 2008) and its press release via Campus Compact. TFA: Donaldson and Johnson's 2011 survey of three TFA cohorts (Phi Delta Kappan via Education Week), Gary Rubinstein's critique of TFA's alumni statistics (NEPC, 2015), FutureEd's 2019 summary of TFA alumni figures, a RAND principal survey as reported by K-12 Dive (2018), and a 2025 Education Week interview with TFA's outgoing CEO. YC: YC's own founder-directory post (2023).

## 3. When
AmeriCorps members enrolled 1999–2000; follow-ups 2003 and 2007; report May 2008. TFA cohorts 2000–2002 surveyed 2007, published 2011; alumni figures 2015, 2019, 2025.

## 4. What it says

**AmeriCorps: eight years later**
- Design: over 1,700 AmeriCorps State and National members from 108 programs plus 475 NCCC members from three campuses, enrolled 1999–2000, with comparison groups of people who had been interested in serving but did not enroll; outcomes measured in 2003 and 2007. [AmeriCorps Evidence Exchange, Still Serving](https://americorps.gov/evidence-exchange/Still-Serving%3A-Measuring-the-Eight-Year-Impact-of-AmeriCorps-on-Alumni)
- Civic engagement: State/National alumni were more active in community affairs (effect size 0.19, p<0.05; participation in meetings and events 69% vs 63%); NCCC's effect on the same measure was not significant. [Still Serving executive summary](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080916011939/http://cns.gov/pdf/08_0513_longstudy_executive.pdf)
- Volunteering: no effect for State/National alumni (effect size 0.07, not significant); a positive effect for NCCC (64% vs 51%, p<0.01). [Still Serving executive summary](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080916011939/http://cns.gov/pdf/08_0513_longstudy_executive.pdf)
- Public-service employment: State/National alumni 46% vs 33% (p<0.01); NCCC not significant; for minority and disadvantaged alumni the gap was 44–46% vs 26%. About 60% of all alumni worked in government or nonprofit jobs, with no comparison figure. [Still Serving executive summary](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080916011939/http://cns.gov/pdf/08_0513_longstudy_executive.pdf); [Campus Compact press release](https://www.compact.org/news/rigorous-longitudinal-study-of-americorps-finds-significant-impacts-eight-years-later)
- Fade: the report says "about half of the impacts observed in 2004 persist into 2007," some weaker; by one count 9 of 16 positive 2004 effects remained. Not significant in 2007 for State/National: importance of neighborhood participation, civic obligations, volunteering, social trust, service-oriented careers. No effect on educational attainment. Voting is reported as slightly negative in the text and not significant in the exhibit. [Still Serving executive summary](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080916011939/http://cns.gov/pdf/08_0513_longstudy_executive.pdf)
- The sponsoring agency's own release summarizes the same study as showing alumni are "significantly more civically engaged" and that 60% hold public-service jobs. [Campus Compact press release](https://www.compact.org/news/rigorous-longitudinal-study-of-americorps-finds-significant-impacts-eight-years-later)
- The study measures what alumni *do* in life, not whether they stay engaged with AmeriCorps itself; no AmeriCorps alumni-network participation rate was found. Unverified.

**TFA: who stays, and how it's counted**
- Donaldson and Johnson surveyed 2,029 members of the 2000–2002 cohorts in 2007 (62% response): 60.5% taught beyond two years; 35.5% beyond four; 27.8% were still teaching after five. 43.6% stayed in their original low-income placement school past the two-year obligation; by the fifth year 14.8% were still in that school. Among leavers, the most common top reason (34.9%) was a non-K-12 job; about 18% cited school-based factors such as poor administrative leadership. [Donaldson & Johnson, Education Week/Kappan, Oct. 2011](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/opinion-tfa-teachers-how-long-do-they-teach-why-do-they-leave/2011/10)
- TFA's own long-running claim is that about two-thirds of alumni are "in education." Rubinstein (TFA 1991 alum) notes the figure had not moved in roughly ten years, that it comes from a voluntary alumni survey, that the question asks whether one's job "impacts" education, and that the 10–15% who leave during the two-year commitment never enter the alumni denominator. [Rubinstein via NEPC, Sept. 2015](https://nepc.colorado.edu/node/7547)
- FutureEd (2019), citing TFA: more than 50,000 alumni; over 14,000 in classrooms; 1,260 principals; six sitting state education chiefs. No survey method or response rate given. [FutureEd, Staying power](https://www.future-ed.org/staying-power-tfa-alumni-in-public-education/)
- TFA's outgoing CEO (2025): a network of "more than 70,000" (the same interview also says 66,000), up from 14,000 in 2005; "almost 17,000" alumni lead classrooms, schools, or systems; 12 state commissioners; about 10% of corps members are second-generation and almost 7% of the 2024 corps came through TFA's own high-school/college Ignite pipeline. No alumni engagement rate is given. [Education Week, Hess interview with Villanueva Beard, Apr. 2025](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/opinion-teach-for-americas-outgoing-ceo-reflects-on-her-tenure/2025/04)
- RAND's 2017 survey of 1,100 principals found principals who were themselves TFA alumni were *less* satisfied with current corps members than other principals; researchers attributed this to first-hand experience. [K-12 Dive on RAND RR2192, Jan. 2018](https://www.k12dive.com/news/study-teach-for-america-alumni-more-critical-of-current-corps-members/515089)

**Y Combinator (brief, thinly documented)**
- YC publishes a public Founder Directory and describes its community as "approaching 10,000 founders"; the internal alumni platform (Bookface) is mentioned but not described, and no engagement data is published. [YC blog, The YC founder directory, Jan. 2023](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/the-yc-founder-directory)

**What the case assumes that The Labs can't**
- A paid, full-time, fixed-length service term with a stipend or salary; a federal agency or a $300M nonprofit to fund a longitudinal study; and an "alumni" status defined by completing a term rather than by any ongoing activity.

## Failure modes seen
- **Half the effect fades:** about half of AmeriCorps' four-year impacts were gone or weaker by year eight; volunteering showed no effect for the main program. [Still Serving executive summary](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080916011939/http://cns.gov/pdf/08_0513_longstudy_executive.pdf)
- **Cliff after the obligation:** 56% of TFA teachers left their placement school at the end of the two-year term; 72% were out of teaching by year five. [Donaldson & Johnson 2011](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/opinion-tfa-teachers-how-long-do-they-teach-why-do-they-leave/2011/10)
- **The headline number is a survey artifact:** the "two-thirds in education" figure rests on a voluntary survey with a loose definition and excludes early leavers. [Rubinstein via NEPC 2015](https://nepc.colorado.edu/node/7547)
- **Alumni as critics:** TFA-alum principals rated current corps members lower than non-alum principals did. [K-12 Dive on RAND 2018](https://www.k12dive.com/news/study-teach-for-america-alumni-more-critical-of-current-corps-members/515089)
- **Effects land unevenly:** the AmeriCorps public-service-career effect was concentrated among minority and disadvantaged alumni; NCCC and State/National showed different patterns on nearly every measure. [Still Serving executive summary](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080916011939/http://cns.gov/pdf/08_0513_longstudy_executive.pdf)

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- Both organizations measure alumni by what they do *elsewhere* (public-service jobs, teaching), not by whether they come back. What would The Labs count as a post-cycle success: a maintainer, a facilitator, or a person who took the method to their own workplace and never returned?
- TFA's network grew from 14,000 to 70,000 while the retention curve stayed the same shape. Does an open alumni status need any activity to keep, and if not, what does the count mean?
- AmeriCorps' effects were strongest for people with the least prior access. Who in a branch's catchment is the equivalent, and does the between-cycle experience reach them or the people who already had routes?
- The two-year obligation produced a visible cliff at exactly two years. Does a twelve-week Cycle produce a cliff at week twelve, and what did TFA's "second-generation" pipeline (10% of corps) do that an alumni network did not?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
AmeriCorps' comparison group self-selected out of serving, so residual differences could drive the gaps; outcomes are self-reported; one cohort. TFA's numbers after 2011 come from TFA itself. No source here measures how many alumni of either program take part in alumni programming. **Rival explanation:** the public-service employment gap for AmeriCorps alumni may be a credentialing and network effect of the service year on the job market (a line on a résumé, a referral) rather than a change in civic disposition, which would also explain why attitudinal measures faded while employment did not. AmeriCorps' Office of Research and Evaluation, Abt Associates, and TFA's research team could confirm.
