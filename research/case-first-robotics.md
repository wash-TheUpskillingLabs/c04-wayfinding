# Case: FIRST Robotics Competition, a global season clock delivered by local volunteer-run teams

**Kind:** paradigmatic (a shared calendar with local delivery), with documented failure modes
**Bears on:** cycle, post-cycle, facilitators, activation-mobilization, pre-cycle
**Product part:** cycle | post-between | facilitators | kits
**Logged:** 2026-11-06 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
FIRST Robotics Competition (FRC) is a high-school robotics program in which every team in the world receives the same game at a January Kickoff, builds a robot, and competes at regional or district events in March and April. Teams are local, school- or community-based, and run by volunteer adult mentors; the off-season (roughly May to December) is unofficial and left to teams and third-party hosts.

## 2. Where it came from
FIRST (For Inspiration and Recognition of Science and Technology) is a US nonprofit. This file draws on FIRST's own mentor guide, annual report, impact page and off-season event policy; a 2018 FIRST announcement retiring the fixed six-week build; a 2013 ASEE study of mentoring on 11 teams; a 2011 WPI student report on rookie teams; a 2023 Minnesota legislative white paper by the state's FRC organizers; and threads on Chief Delphi, the main FRC community forum (not peer reviewed, but the place where mentors describe why teams fold).

## 3. When
- FRC 2024 season: 3,468 teams; 2020 season was the first without an enforced six-week build.
- Sources: ASEE paper 2013; WPI report October 2011; Chief Delphi threads 2011, 2013, 2016, 2023, 2024; FIRST Stop Build Day announcement November 13, 2018; Minnesota white paper March 2023; FIRST annual report FY2024–25 (July 1, 2024 to June 30, 2025).

## 4. What it says

**The clock**
- Kickoff is in January; district and regional events run March to April; the FIRST Championship closes the season. The mentor guide's sample build timeline runs "Weeks 1 through 8+", and build season now runs from Kickoff to a team's first event. [FRC Mentor Guide](https://www.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/resources/frc-mentor-guide.pdf?hsLang=en)
- Until 2019 a fixed "Stop Build Day" in February enforced a six-week build season. FIRST announced on November 13, 2018 that it would be retired from 2020, giving teams a year to plan; reasons given were flexibility around holidays, school breaks and weather, the cost of the second robot that about half of teams built to keep practicing after bagging, and letting teams balance robotics with work, family and school. FIRST's reply to commenters said mentor workload should be considered and that stretching six weeks of work over eight or more might not serve members well. [FIRST, Stop Build Day in 2019 and 2020](https://firstinspires.org/node/15976)
- The 2020 Kickoff was January 4, 2020; 3,898 teams registered; the season was suspended on March 12, 2020 for COVID-19. [Wikipedia, Infinite Recharge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_Recharge)
- Team counts by season: 3,660 (2018), 3,802 (2019), 3,914 (2020), 3,079 (2021, no official events), 3,225 (2022), 3,304 (2023), 3,468 (2024, of which 3,141 veteran and 327 rookie teams, 28 countries, 172 official events). [Wikiwand mirror of Wikipedia, FRC](https://wikiwand.com/en/FIRST_Robotics_Competition)
- FIRST reports 93,000+ FRC students in 35 countries in the 2025 season, and across all programs 493,000+ volunteer, mentor and educator roles and 39 million+ hours in 2024–25. The annual report gives no data on team or mentor retention. [FIRST Annual Report 2025](https://www.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/about/report/annual_report_2025.pdf); [FIRST FRC impact page](https://www.firstinspires.org/robotics/frc/impact)

**Local delivery: mentors**
- A 2013 study of 11 FRC teams (67 interviews) found mentor involvement ranged from an "apprentice" pole (mentors decide, students watch) to an "autonomous" pole (students do, mentors step back); all rookie teams sat at the heavy-involvement end, and the authors warned heavy involvement may breed dependence while too little sets inexperienced students up to fail. [Dolenc and Tai, ASEE 2013](https://peer.asee.org/taking-different-paths-a-comparative-study-of-mentoring-models-among-robotics-competition-teams.pdf)
- FIRST's mentor guide sets no hours figure but tells teams to plan for transitioning mentors so the team survives a lead coach stepping down, to compare graduation with recruitment, and to "Watch for signs of serious stress among team members." It lists two sustainability trainings. [FRC Mentor Guide](https://www.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/resources/frc-mentor-guide.pdf?hsLang=en)
- A 2013 mentor described a build-season schedule of weekday evenings plus Saturdays (about 16 hours/week available) and "50-something hours" in the final weekend before bagging, with about as many mentors as students; the team moved to subteams with set days so mentors did not have to attend every session. [Chief Delphi, 2013](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/forums/showthread.php?p=1272353)
- A 2016 mentor thread on team sustainability lists mentors carrying the team alone alongside full-time jobs, paperwork, chasing money, student drop-off during routine tasks, and little time to teach during build; it asks for more off-season opportunities and a smaller training robot. [Chief Delphi, 2016](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/forums/showthread.php?p=1588944)
- An August 2024 thread reports a team next to a national lab that asked more than 13 professionals to a meeting; 5 tried volunteering, the longest lasting 3 weeks at 6 hours/week. One reply said most teams that folded or never formed did so because no one would lead them; another described a nonprofit-started team that closed when its lead mentor left for paid work. [Chief Delphi, 2024](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/the-mentor-problem/470179)
- Minnesota's FRC organizers reported mentor and volunteer numbers 24% below 2019 at the pandemic peak and still 16% below in 2022, team funding down 50% during COVID, and costs up 30–35% since 2019. [Minnesota FRC white paper, 2023](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2023/jobs/20230320/Jobs_20230320_2024-25-FIRST-Robotics-Legislative-White-Paper.pdf)

**Teams folding**
- Return data compiled by a forum member for 2011: about 86.8% of 2010 rookie teams returned (37 of 281 did not), against an overall return rate of 91.5%. The thread started from a rookie-grant question: sponsorship is not guaranteed year to year. [Chief Delphi, Return Numbers, 2011](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/return-numbers/113207)
- A 2011 WPI report says teams that do not succeed in competition are less likely to return, that many rookies finish the year but do not come back, and gives no rate. It counts 2,073+ FRC teams in 2011 versus about 1,300 in 2007. [Giovacchini and Heslinga, WPI 2011](https://digitalwpi.wpi.edu/downloads/j38607278?locale=de)
- Team 1551 announced in May 2023 it would cease for lack of students: one recruit and three returning members against a stated minimum of eight, despite having money, supplies, mentors and school support. It set a condition for reinstatement (eight committed students attending a month of weekly training in September). [Chief Delphi, Team 1551, 2023](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/team-1551-cessation/436853)
- A 2016 reply to a histogram of years-active for dropped-out teams argued that funding usually comes from a single source, that mentor time causes burnout, and that teams with stronger school relationships are more stable; it put a reasonable team at about $10k/year. [Chief Delphi, 2016 histogram thread](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/forums/showthread.php?p=1588591)

**What the off-season does**
- FIRST defines an off-season event as a gathering using FIRST content with a fee or sponsorship; hosts are outside bodies (partners, schools, teams' nonprofits), who must file a notice and handle insurance and youth protection. The 2026 off-season period runs April 25 to December 31. [FIRST, Off-Season Events](https://firstinspires.org/resource-library/frc/off-season-events)
- The mentor guide says in May–September some teams break and others meet year-round, for recruiting, fundraising, training, outreach and organizing the build space; teams often meet once or twice a week in summer and fall. [FRC Mentor Guide](https://www.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/resources/frc-mentor-guide.pdf?hsLang=en)
- A Texas summer invitational, run by a veteran team's coach with volunteers, exists to give teams that cannot travel a nearby event, to let pre-rookie teams start on robots loaned by veterans, and to build confidence before January. [FIRST in Texas](https://firstintexas.org/the-value-of-off-season-events-interview-with-allen-gregory-iv)

## Failure modes seen
- **Cliff when the one leader leaves.** A nonprofit-started team closed after its lead mentor took a paid job; forum consensus that most folds are "no one willing to lead." [Chief Delphi, 2024](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/the-mentor-problem/470179)
- **Peak-driven burnout.** 50-hour final weekends and nightly meetings in the six-week build; FIRST itself cited mentor workload when retiring the fixed deadline. [Chief Delphi, 2013](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/forums/showthread.php?p=1272353); [FIRST, 2018](https://firstinspires.org/node/15976)
- **Rookie attrition.** About 13% of 2010 rookies did not return in 2011, versus about 8.5% overall. [Chief Delphi, 2011](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/return-numbers/113207)
- **Single-source funding.** Rookie grants and one sponsor; costs up 30–35% and funding down 50% in Minnesota after COVID. [Minnesota white paper](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2023/jobs/20230320/Jobs_20230320_2024-25-FIRST-Robotics-Legislative-White-Paper.pdf)
- **Participants do not show up even when everything else is in place.** Team 1551 had money, mentors and support but not eight students. [Chief Delphi, 2023](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/team-1551-cessation/436853)
- **No one measures retention.** FIRST's annual report counts students, events and hours, not returning teams or mentors. [FIRST Annual Report 2025](https://www.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/about/report/annual_report_2025.pdf)

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- FIRST hands the off-season to third parties and leaves the steady middle to each team. Who owns the between-Cycle period in The Labs, and is it a kit or a gap?
- FIRST relaxed the fixed six-week deadline partly for mentor workload. What does the twelve-week Cycle assume about facilitator hours, and what happens on a lab where there is one facilitator?
- Rookie teams start at the heavy-mentor end. What is the equivalent of the "apprentice to autonomous" shift for a lab, and who notices it?
- The program's conditions for a team to exist (eight students, a lead, money, a space) are explicit on a forum but not in the program. What are a lab's minimum conditions, and where are they written?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- Most evidence on folding and burnout is from forum posts by mentors, not from FIRST data; FIRST publishes no team- or mentor-retention series we could find. The 2011 return figures are a forum member's compilation of an unnamed dataset.
- The ASEE study covers 11 teams, 9 of them 4-H sponsored, during build and competition only; it says nothing about off-season.
- FIRST assumes what The Labs cannot: a school calendar, minors as the participants (with youth-protection rules), a school or sponsor paying roughly $10k/year, and a competition that gives the peak its deadline.
- Wikipedia's main FRC page and FIRST's "what is FRC" page could not be opened (permission not granted); team counts come from a Wikiwand mirror of Wikipedia.
- **Rival explanation:** teams may fold less from the calendar's intensity than from the economics of a $10k/year, sponsor-dependent, school-tied program; the six-week peak might be what draws mentors in, and the Stop Build change may have been about cost and equity more than burnout, as FIRST's own text says.
- Who could confirm: FIRST headquarters (team registration data by year); state Program Delivery Partners such as FIRST in Texas or Minnesota; the ASEE authors.
