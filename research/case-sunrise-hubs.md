# Case: Sunrise Movement hubs, fast distributed growth through self-starting local hubs, then internal crisis and restructuring (2020–2023)

**Kind:** failure (extreme growth followed by contraction)
**Bears on:** pre-cycle, cycle, facilitators, activation-mobilization, post-cycle
**Logged:** 2026-11-05 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
A youth climate organization founded in 2017 whose local "hubs" could be started by volunteers with national training and online resources; it grew from about 15 to 150+ hubs within months of a November 2018 sit-in, reached a reported 400+ hubs, then went through member losses, a staff cut from about 100 to about 65, and a rewrite of its structure ("Sunrise 2.0").

## 2. Where it came from
Investigative reporting (BuzzFeed News, Grist), a hub founder's published essay (Forge), Sunrise's own public movement updates (April and May 2022), a Leading Change Network session write-up, campus reporting on one hub's collapse and revival (Brown Daily Herald), and a former national team member's published reflection (via Climate Advocacy Lab).

## 3. When
- Founded 2017; Pelosi sit-in November 2018.
- Internal conflicts reported 2019–2021; March 2021 internal manifesto.
- Restructuring and staff buyouts March–May 2022; new structure aimed at public campaigns from January 2023.
- Sources dated March 2021 to November 2023 (one campus article's date is uncertain, see below).

## 4. What it says
**Growth and the hub model**
- After the November 2018 sit-in at Nancy Pelosi's office, Sunrise grew from about 15 hubs to at least 150 within months, with about 20 paid staff and around 13,000 volunteers; its budget rose from about $850,000 (2018) to over $4 million a year later. ([BuzzFeed News via Yahoo, 12 Aug 2021](https://www.yahoo.com/news/sunrise-movements-members-color-group-142027288.html))
- A hub founder writing in 2021 reported more than 400 hubs since 2017. New members started with "Sunrise 101" (history, theory of change, Green New Deal plan); "Sunrise School" ran weekly volunteer-led courses, one drawing nearly 4,000 attendees. Large hubs had teams and clear paths in; small hubs sometimes put new members straight into leadership; her hub handed off lead roles about every six months via "deputy" leads. Hubs could draw on paid regional organizers, training networks and online resources. ([Nikayla Jefferson, Forge, 4 Mar 2021](https://forgeorganizing.org/?p=4512))
- The same essay states the movement had "chosen size over depth, and quantity over quality of hubs." ([Forge, 2021](https://forgeorganizing.org/?p=4512))

**Internal crisis**
- Members of color reported feeling tokenized over about three years; a BIPOC caucus sent demands in August 2019; fellows were paid about $400/month in 2019; in September 2020 founders acknowledged tokenization; a Black caucus of about 40 hub leaders and staff called for directors to step down. A March 2021 internal manifesto said the group was losing more active members than it gained, especially Black members; some local hubs stalled. ([BuzzFeed News via Yahoo, 2021](https://www.yahoo.com/news/sunrise-movements-members-color-group-142027288.html))
- The 2021 hub-founder essay also describes white members dominating some hub leadership and women and members of color pushed into visible roles too early, and barriers of time, money and English proficiency. ([Forge, 2021](https://forgeorganizing.org/?p=4512))

**Restructuring (2022)**
- Sunrise's April 2022 update said it had turned inward and its capacity for organizing support had shrunk; it was cutting national staff from about 100 to about 65 by 2023 (29 took buyouts in March 2022), capping its 2022 budget at $15 million against a hoped-for $20 million, and planned to ratify a new "DNA" so hubs could organize from summer 2022 and launch public campaigns in January 2023. ([Sunrise, 7 Apr 2022](https://www.sunrisemovement.org/?p=21910))
- The May 2022 update described member input sessions for hub members on the draft "Sunrise 2.0" framework and a national restructure giving each staffer a defined role, team and manager. ([Sunrise, 6 May 2022](https://www.sunrisemovement.org/?p=21962))
- A December 2022 Leading Change Network session described structural limits: bringing local volunteers into national decisions, building beyond the core, and deciding how to govern democratically. ([Leading Change Network, 6 Dec 2022](https://leadingchangenetwork.org/lessons-explorations-challenges-in-structure-from-the-sunrise-movement/))

**External context**
- Grist (June 2022) reported Earth Day 2022 protests drawing hundreds rather than thousands, and attributed the climate movement's decline to COVID moving protest online, a Democratic White House, repeated legislative failure (Build Back Better cut and stalled in Dec 2021), and competing crises. ([Grist, Shannon Osaka, 16 Jun 2022](https://grist.org/protest/where-have-all-the-climate-activists-gone))
- A former national team member wrote in November 2023 that a volunteer "Movement Democracy Project" formed in spring 2021 to push for transparency, that internal politics depended on who you knew, and that the 2018–2020 energy would not return without a new strategy. ([Lake Liao via Climate Advocacy Lab, 12 Nov 2023](https://www.climateadvocacylab.org/resource/reflections-what-went-wrong-sunrise-movement/))

**One hub's lifecycle (Brown/RISD)**
- The Brown/RISD hub split from the Providence hub in 2019, drew 20–50 students to general meetings at its peak, tried a shift to "non-hierarchical organization" in spring 2020, lost momentum when the pandemic sent students home, and was last operational in late 2020 after key leaders graduated without replacements. ([Brown Daily Herald, Will Kubzansky, 2022](https://www.browndailyherald.com/article/2022/03/former-organizers-reflect-on-brownrisd-sunrise-dissolution))
- A later article reports a relaunch after a course project on reviving the chapter, with four task forces and a nine-student executive board, and cites more than 400 hubs nationwide. ([Brown Daily Herald, Sam Levine, date uncertain](https://www.browndailyherald.com/article/2022/10/browns-sunrise-chapter-sees-new-dawn))

## Failure modes seen
- **Size over depth.** A hub founder wrote that the movement prioritized number of hubs over their quality; small hubs lacked resources for newcomers. ([Forge, 2021](https://forgeorganizing.org/?p=4512))
- **Newcomer straight to leader.** Small hubs sometimes put new members into leadership immediately. ([Forge, 2021](https://forgeorganizing.org/?p=4512))
- **Losing more than gaining.** An internal 2021 manifesto said active-member losses exceeded gains, especially among Black members. ([BuzzFeed News, 2021](https://www.yahoo.com/news/sunrise-movements-members-color-group-142027288.html))
- **Graduation cliff.** A campus hub ended when leaders graduated without successors. ([Brown Daily Herald, 2022](https://www.browndailyherald.com/article/2022/03/former-organizers-reflect-on-brownrisd-sunrise-dissolution))
- **Hub turns inward.** In 2022 the national organization said its support for organizing had shrunk while it restructured. ([Sunrise, Apr 2022](https://www.sunrisemovement.org/?p=21910))
- **Who-you-know governance.** Volunteers organized to demand transparency; access was described as depending on personal connections. ([Liao, 2023](https://www.climateadvocacylab.org/resource/reflections-what-went-wrong-sunrise-movement/))

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- What did "Sunrise 101" and "Sunrise School" give a newcomer, and what did a newcomer in a small hub get that a newcomer in a large hub did not?
- How were hub leads handed off (the six-month deputy pattern), and what happened in hubs where no one was ready to take over?
- After the peak moments (2018 sit-in, 2019 strikes), what were people in hubs asked to do week to week, and who decided?
- Which parts of the hub crisis came from the national layer (pay, governance, staff cuts) and which from local conditions (campus calendars, pandemic)?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- No public time series of active hub counts; "400+" is a claim repeated in a 2021 essay and a campus article, not an audited figure.
- Internal documents reported by BuzzFeed were leaked and partial; Sunrise disputed some claims (e.g., that Black participation stayed steady for at least 18 months).
- The URL of the Brown "new dawn" article reads 2022/10, but the text refers to a spring 2024 course, so its date is uncertain.
- **Rival explanation:** the decline may mainly reflect the external cycle (end of the Trump era, COVID, the Build Back Better loss, students graduating) that hit all climate groups, as Grist reports, rather than Sunrise's hub structure or internal conflicts.
- Who could confirm: Sunrise's current national team; Leading Change Network (session video); hub leads in DC.
