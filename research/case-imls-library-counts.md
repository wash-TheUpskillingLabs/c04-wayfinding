# Case: IMLS and the Public Libraries Survey, a national system that counts programs and attendance

**Kind:** paradigmatic (and extreme: an annual census since 1988, interrupted in 2025)
**Bears on:** pre-cycle, cycle, facilitators
**Field claims:** 2, 3
**Evidence:** complicates 2 (the main federal record of public libraries counts programs held and people attending, not what happened to them, and library programming has been funded and reported on that basis for decades; [IMLS FY2023 brief](https://www.imls.gov/sites/default/files/2026-05/FY2023-Research-Brief-508_05132026.pdf), [Becker 2015](https://libraryjournal.com/story/outcomes-impacts-and-indicators)); supports 3 in part (IMLS itself restricted outcome measurement to cases where attribution is feasible; [IMLS 2017](https://www.imls.gov/sites/default/files/outputsoutcomesbuildingcapacity.pdf))
**Logged:** 2026-11-05 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
The Institute of Museum and Library Services (IMLS) runs the Public Libraries Survey (PLS), an annual census of U.S. public libraries that reports visits, circulation, number of programs and program attendance. Separately, states report on federal Grants to States (LSTA) money through a State Program Report.

## 2. Where it came from
IMLS is the federal agency that funds libraries and museums. Sources: IMLS research briefs and news releases; an IMLS training deck on outputs and outcomes (2017); a 2015 *Library Journal* essay on library indicators by a University of Washington researcher; IMLS's Measuring Success initiative page; news and ALA releases on the 2025 executive order and litigation.

## 3. When
- PLS conducted annually since 1988; Measuring Success launched March 2011; outcome-aligned state reporting piloted from FY2015.
- Executive Order 14238 (March 14, 2025) led to staff leave on March 31, 2025; a preliminary injunction in May 2025 ordered staff and some grants restored.
- Sources dated 2014–2026.

## 4. What it says
- The PLS collects data from about 9,000 library systems covering more than 17,000 outlets; IMLS reported over 118 million people attended library programs in FY2017. [IMLS news, Nov 30, 2020](https://imls.gov/news/over-118-million-people-attended-library-programs-annually)
- In FY2023 the median library offered 202 programs (85 rural, 1,065 city) with median attendance of 3,087. The brief does not define how attendance is counted. [IMLS FY2023 Research Brief, May 2026](https://www.imls.gov/sites/default/files/2026-05/FY2023-Research-Brief-508_05132026.pdf)
- Reporting on FY2011 data, *Library Journal* noted program attendance had risen more than 30% since 2004 (89 million attendees at 3.8 million programs) and that the survey lacked electronic-use data, which an IMLS official called a significant gap. [Library Journal, June 2014](https://www.libraryjournal.com/story/latest-imls-public-libraries-survey-sees-continued-rise-in-program-attendance-nationwide)
- A 2015 essay on library indicators placed the PLS as the source of input, activity and output indicators; outcome indicators had to come from other tools such as the Impact Survey (2011: 200+ libraries, about 30,000 users). The author argued libraries often pick a survey before deciding what they want to know. [Becker, *Library Journal*, Sept 2015](https://libraryjournal.com/story/outcomes-impacts-and-indicators)
- IMLS's 2017 training defined outputs as reportable for all activities and outcomes as measured only "when appropriate," i.e. where attribution to the activity is feasible; it aligned state report survey questions with PLA's Project Outcome. [IMLS, Outputs, Outcomes, Impacts, Apr 2017](https://www.imls.gov/sites/default/files/outputsoutcomesbuildingcapacity.pdf)
- Measuring Success, launched March 2011, involved over 70 volunteers from 54 state library agencies to plan and evaluate grant-funded activity. [IMLS Measuring Success](https://www.imls.gov/research-evaluation/program-evaluation/grants-state-library-agency/measuring-success-initiative)
- On March 31, 2025, IMLS's entire staff of about 70 was placed on administrative leave following a March 2025 executive order; the union said most grants would likely be terminated without staff to administer them. [OPB/NPR, Mar 31, 2025](https://www.opb.org/article/2025/03/31/imls-staff-put-on-administrative-leave/)
- In *Rhode Island v. Trump*, 21 state attorneys general won a preliminary injunction ordering terminated staff and grants restored for those states; the administration appealed. [ALA, May 21, 2025](https://www.ala.org/news/2025/05/ala-welcomes-imls-staff-return-and-select-grant-reinstatements-cautiously) The FY2023 PLS brief was published in May 2026. [IMLS FY2023 brief](https://www.imls.gov/sites/default/files/2026-05/FY2023-Research-Brief-508_05132026.pdf)

## Failure modes seen
- **Counts stand in for change:** the national record shows how many programs and attendees, with no measure of what attendees gained. [Becker 2015](https://libraryjournal.com/story/outcomes-impacts-and-indicators)
- **Undefined units:** the FY2023 brief does not define a visit or an attendee, so totals are as reported by each library. [IMLS FY2023 brief](https://www.imls.gov/sites/default/files/2026-05/FY2023-Research-Brief-508_05132026.pdf)
- **Blind spot for new channels:** the survey lacked electronic-use data as digital use grew. [Library Journal 2014](https://www.libraryjournal.com/story/latest-imls-public-libraries-survey-sees-continued-rise-in-program-attendance-nationwide)
- **The record-keeper is fragile:** the agency holding the national record lost its staff for weeks in 2025. [OPB/NPR 2025](https://www.opb.org/article/2025/03/31/imls-staff-put-on-administrative-leave/)

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- We did not find a published critique arguing the PLS should measure outcomes; the outputs-vs-outcomes framing comes from evaluators and IMLS's own training, not from an evaluation showing harm from counting.
- **Rival explanation:** counts may persist not because the field lacks interest in outcomes but because a national census of 9,000 systems can only collect what every library can report cheaply; IMLS's "when appropriate" rule points that way.
- We did not verify the current (late 2026) status of IMLS litigation or staffing.
- Could confirm: state library data coordinators; IMLS Office of Research and Evaluation; COSLA.
