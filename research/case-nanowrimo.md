# Case: National Novel Writing Month, a fixed month run through volunteer local liaisons, and its 2025 shutdown

**Kind:** failure (shutdown), also paradigmatic for calendar-plus-local-volunteer delivery
**Bears on:** activation-mobilization, facilitators, post-cycle, pre-cycle, interfaces
**Product part:** cycle | post-between | facilitators | channels
**Logged:** 2026-11-06 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
NaNoWriMo asked people to write 50,000 words in the 30 days of November, counted on a central website, with local write-ins run by volunteer Municipal Liaisons (MLs) in hundreds of regions and a lighter "Camp NaNoWriMo" in April and July between Novembers. The nonprofit announced its closure on March 31, 2025.

## 2. Where it came from
Started by Chris Baty in 1999; a 501(c)(3) from 2006. This file draws on the Wikipedia article (for the timeline), the community-maintained Wikiwrimo wiki (for the ML role), a January 2024 board message reposted on Goodreads, a published author's April 2024 account of the ML dispute, TechCrunch's April 2025 shutdown report, a March 2025 Little Village (Iowa City) feature on local regions, and an October 2025 Goodreads post on a successor challenge.

## 3. When
- Event: 1999–2024 (last November run 2024). ML role from 2002/2003. Camp from 2011. Forum scandal late 2023; ML agreement dispute and removal of ML access spring 2024; AI statement August 2024; shutdown announced March 31, 2025.
- Sources: board message January 23, 2024; author account April 10, 2024; Little Village March 11, 2025 (updated March 31); TechCrunch April 1, 2025; Goodreads post October 25, 2025.

## 4. What it says

**The clock and its scale**
- Began in July 1999 with 21 participants; moved to November in 2000 (140 signed up, 29 finished); about 5,000 in 2001 and 14,000 in 2002 after press coverage; 431,626 participants across 633 regions in 2015; over 400,000 in 2022. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Novel_Writing_Month)
- 2022: 412,295 participants in 671 regions, supported by several hundred volunteer MLs. [Little Village](https://littlevillagemag.com/nanowrimo-no-more/)
- Camp NaNoWriMo launched in 2011 (July and August), moved to June and August in 2012, then to April and July from 2013 on. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Novel_Writing_Month)

**Local delivery: Municipal Liaisons**
- MLs were volunteer regional coordinators. Duties: moderate the regional forum, send regional emails and pep talks, run at least one write-in a week plus a kickoff and a "Thank God It's Over" party, write their own 50k, hand out HQ-supplied stickers. Applicants had to be prior winners and 18+; MLs kept the role as long as they did the duties. Counts: 450+ in 2010, 600+ in 2011, 926 MLs over 633 regions in 2015; most regions had one to three. [Wikiwrimo, ML](https://www.wikiwrimo.org/wiki/ML)
- The ML program began in 2003; volunteers moderated forums, sent pep talks, organized fundraisers and called regional meetings. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Novel_Writing_Month)
- Application windows were moved from late August to June–July in 2012 so international MLs could receive kits in time; unofficial helpers ("minions") and in 2019 a self-organized group of people who missed the deadline ("un-MLs") also existed. [Wikiwrimo, ML](https://www.wikiwrimo.org/wiki/ML)

**The crisis**
- After allegations in late 2023 that a long-standing forum moderator had groomed minors through the Young Writers Program, the board shut the forums; they never reopened. [Epic Indie Substack](https://epicindie.substack.com/p/news-nano-wrimo-is-no-more); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Novel_Writing_Month)
- On January 23, 2024 the board said it was reviewing business practices under an interim director, rescoping roles, revising the ML agreement, drafting contracts for non-ML volunteers, adding identity and background checks, and planning ML focus groups for February. [Board message, January 2024](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/24442301-a-message-from-the-nanowrimo-board-of-directors?tab=book)
- A published author's April 2024 account: the draft ML agreement made MLs liable for events and participant conduct, required all communication through the official site in English, and barred local Facebook or Discord groups; MLs resigned, and the organization then removed all MLs' access, restoring it only to those who signed. [Raven Oak, April 2024](https://ravenoak.net/?p=11208)
- Little Village reports the same: critics said the agreement shifted liability onto volunteers, numerous MLs resigned, and the organization removed every ML from its system. [Little Village](https://littlevillagemag.com/nanowrimo-no-more/)
- In August 2024 the organization said condemning AI had "classist and ableist undertones"; two board authors resigned and sponsors left. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Novel_Writing_Month); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/01/nanowrimo-shut-down-after-ai-content-moderation-scandals)

**The shutdown**
- Announced the evening of March 31, 2025, in a video by the interim executive director, citing longstanding financial problems; the organization said the AI and moderation controversies did not directly cause it. TechCrunch quotes the director that too many members "let themselves believe the service to be provided was free." [TechCrunch, April 1, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/01/nanowrimo-shut-down-after-ai-content-moderation-scandals)
- Little Village reports a budget deficit in four of the previous six years, a staff reportedly down to three, and a stated "downward trend in participation." [Little Village](https://littlevillagemag.com/nanowrimo-no-more/)

**What happened locally afterward**
- Iowa had seven regions. One rebranded with a volunteer board, a Facebook page and a Discord, ran an August 2024 "Inkathon" with self-set goals instead of 50,000 words, and a November 1, 2024 "Write All Night" in which 148 participants wrote 404,500 words in 50 hours. [Little Village](https://littlevillagemag.com/nanowrimo-no-more/)
- A commercial successor, Novel November (ProWritingAid), runs a free 30-day challenge with October prep, word-count integrations and a donation per finisher; it describes itself as "very intentionally not NaNoWriMo 2.0" and has no local groups yet. [Goodreads, October 2025](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/26198750-nanowrimo-to-novel-november-a-new-chapter-for-the-write-in)

**What the structure assumed (as stated by sources, not our inference)**
- That hundreds of volunteers would run weekly in-person events under central rules, with the central site as the only sanctioned channel. [Raven Oak](https://ravenoak.net/?p=11208); [Wikiwrimo](https://www.wikiwrimo.org/wiki/ML)
- That a free service for 400,000 people could be funded by donations and sponsors; the director's closing statement frames the gap between "free" and cost as the core problem. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/01/nanowrimo-shut-down-after-ai-content-moderation-scandals)

## Failure modes seen
- **Central liability pushed onto volunteers.** The 2024 ML agreement made volunteers liable for events; many resigned and all were then cut off. [Little Village](https://littlevillagemag.com/nanowrimo-no-more/); [Raven Oak](https://ravenoak.net/?p=11208)
- **Single-channel rule breaks the local layer.** Banning local Discord/Facebook groups and non-English communication collided with how regions actually ran. [Raven Oak](https://ravenoak.net/?p=11208)
- **Safety failure in the all-ages space.** Forums closed after grooming allegations and never reopened. [Epic Indie](https://epicindie.substack.com/p/news-nano-wrimo-is-no-more); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Novel_Writing_Month)
- **Free at the edge, unfunded at the center.** Deficits in four of six years with a staff of three. [Little Village](https://littlevillagemag.com/nanowrimo-no-more/)
- **The local groups outlive the hub.** Iowa regions continued under new names with their own channels. [Little Village](https://littlevillagemag.com/nanowrimo-no-more/)

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- NaNoWriMo's between-peaks offer was two lighter camps; a region's steady middle was whatever its ML chose to run. What does The Labs expect a lab to run between Cycles, and who decides?
- When the hub failed, regions with their own channels survived. Which parts of a lab's life should live in OLOS and which in channels the lab owns?
- The ML role required a prior win, a weekly event, and signing an agreement. What is the equivalent threshold for a facilitator, and what happens to the lab when the one facilitator declines to sign?
- Participation was counted at the center (words logged) and delivery happened at the edge (write-ins). What does OLOS count, and does it see the write-in?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- The ML dispute is documented by a participant-author and a local magazine, not by the organization; the organization's own shutdown video was not opened (YouTube). Financial figures (deficits, staff of three) are as reported by Little Village.
- Wikipedia's dating of the forum scandal is inconsistent (2023 vs "2024 allegations").
- NaNoWriMo assumed what The Labs cannot: a mass online audience that needed no local delivery to participate at all, minors in the community, and a product (a word count) that the center could verify.
- **Rival explanation:** the shutdown may owe less to the volunteer structure than to a decade of thin funding plus two reputational shocks (moderation, AI) that drove away sponsors; the ML structure arguably worked for twenty years and the regions' survival after the hub suggests the local layer was not the weak part.
- Who could confirm: former NaNoWriMo board members; former MLs (not to be named); successor organizers; the Wikiwrimo maintainers.
