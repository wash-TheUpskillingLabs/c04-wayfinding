# Case: Obama for America → Organizing for America → Organizing for Action, keeping a campaign's volunteer base active after election peaks

**Kind:** failure (with a paradigmatic success inside it: the campaign's own volunteer model)
**Bears on:** activation-mobilization, post-cycle, facilitators, interfaces
**Logged:** 2026-11-05 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
The volunteer and email infrastructure of Barack Obama's 2008 and 2012 presidential campaigns, kept alive after each election as an issue-advocacy organization, first inside the Democratic National Committee (DNC) and then as a separate nonprofit, until it was folded into a redistricting group in 2018–19.

## 2. Where it came from
Political press (NPR, The Nation, Fox News), a 2010 report by journalist Ari Melber for techPresident, a book by Elizabeth McKenna and Hahrie Han on the campaign's volunteer model, a review of that book in Boston Review, an excerpt of Micah Sifry's 2017 New Republic article, and Wikipedia's timeline (used only for facts it attributes to named outlets).

## 3. When
- 2008 campaign; Organizing for America run under the DNC after the 2008 win (exact launch date not confirmed here).
- 2009–10: health-care advocacy year.
- 18 Jan 2013: relaunched as Organizing for Action, a 501(c)(4).
- May 2014: staff halved (per Wikipedia citing Washington Post).
- Feb 2017: relaunch after a period of inactivity (per Wikipedia citing NBC News).
- 20 Dec 2018: announced it would fold into the National Democratic Redistricting Committee; merger reported Feb 2019.
- Sources dated 2010–2018 (book 2015).

## 4. What it says
**The peak (2008 and 2012 campaigns)**
- By Election Day 2008 the campaign reportedly held about 13 million email addresses, 3 million donors and 2 million active members of its MyBO platform, which let supporters set up local groups, events and fundraising pages; MyBO had over 800,000 registered users by mid-2008. ([Sifry, New Republic, 9 Feb 2017, excerpt](https://seclists.org/interesting-people/2017/Feb/46))
- In July 2008 a senior campaign policy adviser convened a "Movement 2.0" call on how to sustain the movement if Obama won. ([Sifry excerpt](https://seclists.org/interesting-people/2017/Feb/46))
- McKenna and Han's *Groundbreakers* (Oxford University Press, 2015) argues the campaign's field strength came from unpaid local volunteers who took responsibility for organizing their own neighborhoods months or years before Election Day, based on 70+ interviews, about 100 internal documents and participant observation. ([Harvard Kennedy School publication page](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/groundbreakers-how-obamas-22-million-volunteers-transformed-campaigning-america))
- A review of the book describes the model as volunteers taking on more responsibility over time and leading neighborhood teams, with staff connecting teams rather than managing them hierarchically. ([Boston Review, c. 2015](https://www.bostonreview.net/?p=121799))

**After 2008: from campaign to advocacy inside the party**
- In its first year OFA had about 13 million members on its list and paid staff in all 50 states. Asks included calling members of Congress, office visits, local events and petition drives, generally framed positively around broad goals ("healthcare reform") rather than specific demands; some volunteers found that frustrating. ([The Nation, Ari Melber, 14 Jan 2010](https://www.thenation.com/article/archive/looking-back-one-year-organizing-america))
- Melber's report counted, for 2009: 200,000 cumulative volunteer hours, 37,000 local events, 65,000 people at lobbying events, about 250,000 letters to the editor. It also found most congressional staff interviewed said OFA was not changing votes, that lobbying mostly targeted offices already supportive, and that OFA worked almost every month as a single-issue (health care) group. It sorted members into "super activists," critical participants, supportive bystanders and former members. ([HuffPost, Ari Melber, early 2010](https://m.huffpost.com/us/entry/423188))
- NPR (March 2010) reported critics saying OFA never rebuilt the two-way, bottom-up relationship of the campaign, that enthusiasm fell as quick change did not come, and that deal-making over health care clashed with the campaign's outsider image. ([NPR, 16 Mar 2010](https://www.npr.org/2010/03/16/124613714/can-obamas-grass-roots-sway-midterm-elections))

**After 2012: a separate nonprofit, then fade, revival, merger**
- Organizing for Action launched 18 Jan 2013 as a 501(c)(4). In 2013 donors giving or raising $500,000 could join an advisory board, drawing "pay-to-play" criticism. In May 2014 it halved its staff. By mid-2015 it was reported to hold about 30 million emails, 3 million donors and 2 million active participants. ([Wikipedia, citing Washington Post, NYT and others](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organizing_for_Action))
- The Boston Review reviewer found OFA's concrete accomplishments hard to identify despite tens of millions of dollars raised. ([Boston Review, c. 2015](https://www.bostonreview.net/?p=121799))
- Fox News reported that OFA would have nearly closed in 2016 if Hillary Clinton had won; Trump's election revived it; on 20 Dec 2018 Obama announced it would merge into the National Democratic Redistricting Committee. The article reports nothing on what would happen to its volunteers or list. ([Fox News, 21 Dec 2018](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/obama-aligned-organizing-for-action-group-to-fold-in))
- Wikipedia reports a Feb 2017 relaunch with 14 field organizers to defend the Affordable Care Act, after a long period of inactivity. ([Wikipedia, citing NBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organizing_for_Action))

**Hahrie Han's case-relevant findings**
- In *Groundbreakers*, McKenna and Han locate the campaign's lasting value in two effects: a field model that built community power while organizing for an election, and a transformative effect on the people who took part. ([HKS publication page](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/groundbreakers-how-obamas-22-million-volunteers-transformed-campaigning-america))
- In Han's broader framing, "mobilizing" maximizes numbers through quick, easy asks, while "organizing" develops people's agency and leadership through sequenced, relational asks; she notes the most active groups do both. ([Hahrie Han, "What is Organizing?", Medium, undated](https://medium.com/@hahriehan/what-is-organizing-1508746331d1))

## Failure modes seen
- **Organizing structure becomes a mailing list.** The campaign's two-way, team-based relationship was, per critics, not rebuilt after 2008; OFA's 2009 asks were list-driven calls and letters. ([NPR, 2010](https://www.npr.org/2010/03/16/124613714/can-obamas-grass-roots-sway-midterm-elections); [The Nation, 2010](https://www.thenation.com/article/archive/looking-back-one-year-organizing-america))
- **Activity without traction.** High 2009 volunteer counts coincided with congressional staff saying votes were not moving. ([Melber, 2010](https://m.huffpost.com/us/entry/423188))
- **The goal belongs to someone else.** OFA's agenda was set by the White House and party, with volunteers asked to back broad positive goals rather than set demands. ([The Nation, 2010](https://www.thenation.com/article/archive/looking-back-one-year-organizing-america))
- **Dormant between threats.** Wikipedia (citing NBC) describes a long inactive period before 2017; Fox reports it nearly closed in 2016. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organizing_for_Action); [Fox News, 2018](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/obama-aligned-organizing-for-action-group-to-fold-in))
- **The list outlives the purpose.** A list of tens of millions was folded into a redistricting group in 2018–19, with no reported plan for its volunteers. ([Fox News, 2018](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/obama-aligned-organizing-for-action-group-to-fold-in))

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- What did volunteers who led neighborhood teams in 2008 actually get asked to do in 2009, and how did that differ from what they had been doing?
- Who sets the goal between peaks: the people who were active at the peak, or the central organization? What happened to participation when that changed?
- When a network keeps a huge contact list but stops asking people to lead, what kind of "active" is left (Melber's super activists vs. supportive bystanders)?
- What signals did OFA have that it was dormant, and who noticed?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- No public data on how many 2008 neighborhood team leaders stayed active in 2009–2012 or after 2013.
- The Sifry article was read only as a partial repost (first section); its later sections on OFA inside the DNC are not used here.
- Several facts (2014 staff cut, 2015 list size, 2017 relaunch) come via Wikipedia's citations, not from the original articles.
- **Rival explanation:** a presidential campaign's volunteer energy may rest on the candidate and the election deadline, so any successor organization would have faded regardless of structure; the decline may reflect the normal end of an electoral moment rather than OFA's design choices.
- Who could confirm: Elizabeth McKenna and Hahrie Han (interview data); Ari Melber's techPresident report; Micah Sifry (full article and his book *The Big Disconnect*).
