# Case: Indivisible, a free guide that seeded thousands of self-starting local groups, and a national office that tried to support them between moments

**Kind:** maximum variation (bottom-up spread from a document, versus OFA's top-down list) with documented national–local failure
**Bears on:** pre-cycle, activation-mobilization, facilitators, post-cycle, interfaces
**Logged:** 2026-11-05 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
A movement that began in December 2016 as a free online guide to pressuring members of Congress, after which thousands of local groups formed on their own and registered on a map; a national nonprofit (Indivisible Project) later built staff, trainings and campaigns to support and coordinate them, and in 2025–26 helped coordinate the "No Kings" mass protests.

## 2. Where it came from
Academic fieldwork by Theda Skocpol and colleagues at Harvard (a 2019 working paper with Leah Gose; a 2021 American Prospect article with Caroline Tervo), Indivisible's own training page, a No Kings / Commons Library description of the 2025 "One Million Rising" training campaign, and Wikipedia's dated summary (used only for facts attributed to named outlets).

## 3. When
- Guide published on Google Docs 14 Dec 2016; national organization incorporated early 2017.
- Strongest coordination 2017 (Affordable Care Act repeal fight); loosening through 2018–2020.
- No Kings protests June 2025, October 2025, March 2026; One Million Rising trainings from mid-2025.
- Indivisible lists a "2027 Indivisible Guide" launch on 19 Nov 2026.
- Sources dated 2019–2026.

## 4. What it says
**How groups formed**
- The guide, a 23-page handbook, was first published on Google Docs on 14 Dec 2016 and spread virally; by 4 Feb 2017 more than 3,800 local groups calling themselves Indivisibles had registered support. ([Wikipedia, citing The New Yorker and Indivisible's site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indivisible_(organization)))
- Gose and Skocpol found local groups formed after the November 2016 election mainly through friendships and social media, often started by two to five people who did not know each other beforehand. The guide's early tactical advice mattered more to formation than the national organization's later formal support; much of the effect came before Indivisible incorporated and hired staff. ([Gose & Skocpol, "Resist, Persist, and Transform," c. 2019](https://thedaskocpol.scholars.harvard.edu/resource/resistpersistandtransform3-21-19appdf))
- In their samples, participants were about 90% women and 90% white, median age 55 (eight-county sample) and 61 (Pennsylvania). Groups were found in 52 of 100 North Carolina counties and 52 of 67 Pennsylvania counties. ([Gose & Skocpol, c. 2019](https://thedaskocpol.scholars.harvard.edu/resource/resistpersistandtransform3-21-19appdf))

**How groups persisted**
- Most groups formed in late 2016–early 2017 were still operating in 2019, typically meeting about monthly and dividing work through subcommittees. Leadership turnover was common and groups recruited replacement leaders. Among Pennsylvania leaders surveyed in 2019, one reported an original group had closed and two reported mergers. ([Gose & Skocpol, c. 2019](https://thedaskocpol.scholars.harvard.edu/resource/resistpersistandtransform3-21-19appdf))
- Reported difficulties: volunteer burnout, leaders pulling back, interpersonal feuds, suspended activity, and trouble sustaining in-person meetings. Some groups effectively took over local Democratic Party leadership; one respondent said, "We have become the local Democratic Party." ([Gose & Skocpol, c. 2019](https://thedaskocpol.scholars.harvard.edu/resource/resistpersistandtransform3-21-19appdf))

**The national–local relationship**
- Skocpol and Tervo report national revenue of $6.5M (2017), $14.7M (2018) and $14.9M (2019), staff growing from about 40 (fall 2017) to 75 positions (Aug 2019), mostly in Washington; little money reached local groups; field organizers were few, turned over quickly and each covered many groups. ([Skocpol & Tervo, American Prospect, 4 Feb 2021](https://prospect.org/2021/02/04/resistance-disconnect-indivisible-national-local-activists/))
- The national map had about 6,000 entries by late 2017, of which researchers estimated 2,000–3,000 were real operating groups; Indivisible claimed about 4,312 affiliates in 2020, while the authors found thousands fewer surviving. By the late 2010s national staff kept ongoing contact with only hundreds of groups, and national emails drew responses from a tiny share. ([Skocpol & Tervo, 2021](https://prospect.org/2021/02/04/resistance-disconnect-indivisible-national-local-activists/))
- National pressure campaigns drew little local response; a 2018 effort to form state-level associations largely stalled; in March 2019, 18% of surveyed members favored a presidential primary endorsement and 48% opposed. Local groups chose their own issues and often used other organizations' resources. ([Skocpol & Tervo, 2021](https://prospect.org/2021/02/04/resistance-disconnect-indivisible-national-local-activists/))

**Mobilization peaks in 2025–26**
- Indivisible and partners coordinated No Kings protests in over 2,000 communities in June 2025; CBS estimates cited by Wikipedia put October 2025 at about 7 million participants and March 2026 at about 8 million across about 3,300 locations. ([Wikipedia, citing ABC News and CBS News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indivisible_(organization)))
- One Million Rising, launched after June 2025, aims to train one million people through national coordinating calls and host-run local gatherings using a toolkit (checklists, sample agendas, facilitation tips), with sessions linking participants to the next national campaign. ([Commons Social Change Library / No Kings, Oct 2025](https://commonslibrary.org/organising-your-local-community-one-million-risings-campaign-against-authoritarianism/))
- Indivisible's training page lists a recurring national call series (Oct–Dec 2026) and a "2027 Indivisible Guide" launch on 19 Nov 2026, plus a separate "Guide for New Groups." ([Indivisible, Trainings & Calls, viewed 2026-11-05](https://indivisible.org/training))

## Failure modes seen
- **Map inflation.** The registration map counted thousands of entries that were not operating groups. ([Skocpol & Tervo, 2021](https://prospect.org/2021/02/04/resistance-disconnect-indivisible-national-local-activists/))
- **Hub grows, edges don't.** National revenue and staff tripled or more between 2017 and 2019 while little money or authority reached local groups. ([Skocpol & Tervo, 2021](https://prospect.org/2021/02/04/resistance-disconnect-indivisible-national-local-activists/))
- **Email asks into silence.** National campaign emails drew responses from a tiny share of groups, and some groups dropped the affiliation. ([Skocpol & Tervo, 2021](https://prospect.org/2021/02/04/resistance-disconnect-indivisible-national-local-activists/))
- **Missing middle layer.** A 2018 attempt at state associations largely stalled. ([Skocpol & Tervo, 2021](https://prospect.org/2021/02/04/resistance-disconnect-indivisible-national-local-activists/))
- **Burnout and feuds locally.** Groups reported burnout, leaders pulling back and interpersonal conflict. ([Gose & Skocpol, c. 2019](https://thedaskocpol.scholars.harvard.edu/resource/resistpersistandtransform3-21-19appdf))
- **Narrow base.** Participants were overwhelmingly older, white, college-educated women. ([Gose & Skocpol, c. 2019](https://thedaskocpol.scholars.harvard.edu/resource/resistpersistandtransform3-21-19appdf))

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- What did a 23-page guide give people that let them start a group without a staff organizer, and what did they still lack afterwards?
- Between national moments, what kept local groups meeting (monthly meetings, subcommittees, local issues, local party roles), and how much of it came from the national office?
- How does a national hub know which local groups are real and active, and what happens to newcomers routed to a group that no longer exists?
- After a peak like No Kings, what route exists for someone who marched once, and who hosts it?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- Skocpol's samples are regional (Pennsylvania, North Carolina and selected counties) and described by the authors as non-representative; national survival rates are unknown.
- Skocpol and Tervo argue for redistributing resources; the factual numbers are separable from that argument, but the framing is theirs.
- No independent data yet on whether 2025–26 One Million Rising participants joined or formed lasting local groups.
- **Rival explanation:** local groups may have persisted or faded mainly because of the national political calendar (Trump's first term, the 2018 and 2020 elections, and his return in 2025) rather than anything Indivisible national did or did not provide.
- Who could confirm: Theda Skocpol's research group at Harvard; Indivisible's group-support team; local Indivisible group leaders (including DC-area groups).
