# Case: P2PU Learning Circles, peer study groups for online courses run inside public libraries

**Kind:** paradigmatic (closest structural relative: library-hosted, non-expert facilitators, volunteer pathway added later)
**Bears on:** pre-cycle, cycle, post-cycle, facilitators, interfaces
**Logged:** 2026-11-05 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
Peer 2 Peer University (P2PU) designed "learning circles": a small group (about 8 people) meets weekly, usually in a library, for 6 to 8 weeks to work through a free online course together, with a facilitator who manages logistics rather than teaching content.

## 2. Where it came from
P2PU is a nonprofit founded around 2010 that pivoted to learning circles in 2015, piloting them with Chicago Public Library (CPL) and in Nairobi. This file draws on P2PU's own blog posts (2017–2023), a University of Washington TASCHA survey report (2018), WebJunction and KQED coverage of the Chicago pilot (2016), and a Siegel Family Endowment Q&A (2021).

## 3. When
- 2015–2016: Chicago pilot, Knight News Challenge funding ([WebJunction, 2016](https://www.webjunction.org/news/webjunction/recap-learning-circles.html)).
- 2017–2020: IMLS grant with Kansas City Public Library to spread the model nationally ([P2PU, 2019](https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/1943/feed_items/2488971)).
- 2022: institutional membership program for libraries launched ([Siegel Family Endowment, 2021](https://siegelendowment.org/?p=1455)).
- Sources dated 2016–2023.

## 4. What it says

**Pre (arriving, choosing a circle)**
- Learners browse circles near them on P2PU's site and sign up; facilitators see sign-ups and can send messages and weekly reminders from a dashboard ([Learning Circles docs](https://learning-circles.readthedocs.io/en/stable/intro.html)).
- In Chicago, 90% of attendees heard about circles through the library, and 65% had never taken an online course ([KQED, 2016](https://www.kqed.org/mindshift/44784/online-learning-why-libraries-could-be-the-key-to-moocs-success)).
- CPL staff chose courses with employability in mind; Introduction to Public Speaking (Coursera) was the most popular ([WebJunction, 2016](https://www.webjunction.org/news/webjunction/recap-learning-circles.html)).
- In 2018 interviews with eleven libraries, P2PU found attendance tended to be low when facilitators picked topics from their own interests or anecdote, and that learners with a vague interest returned weekly far less often than those with a specific need ([P2PU, 2018](https://medium.com/p2pu/learning-about-learning-circles-56dc25320f38)).

**During**
- A P2PU staff member put Chicago retention at roughly 45–55%, against an estimated 5–10% completion for free online courses taken alone ([WebJunction, 2016](https://www.webjunction.org/news/webjunction/recap-learning-circles.html); [KQED, 2016](https://www.kqed.org/mindshift/44784/online-learning-why-libraries-could-be-the-key-to-moocs-success)). These figures are a staff estimate, not a published dataset.
- Facilitators found groups of four to nine worked; larger turnouts were split ([KQED, 2016](https://www.kqed.org/mindshift/44784/online-learning-why-libraries-could-be-the-key-to-moocs-success)).
- A TASCHA survey of 144 learners who signed up via P2PU's site found at least three-quarters said they met their goals; it credited the library, social and emotional support, group discussion and facilitator interpersonal skill ([TASCHA report record, 2018](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/researchworks/items/bac0a618-d298-4afc-bc60-ff33e26b2d9d/full); [TASCHA news](https://tascha.uw.edu/new-tascha-report-summarizes-data-from-p2pu-survey)). The news post says respondents were in the US, Canada and Kenya; the repository abstract says US and Kenya.
- Survey respondents attributed success roughly one-third to personal drive, with facilitator, peer support and idea exchange splitting most of the rest; subject interest was under 10% ([P2PU, 2018](https://medium.com/p2pu/learning-about-learning-circles-56dc25320f38)).

**Post (after a circle)**
- 90% of Chicago participants said they wanted to take another course ([WebJunction, 2016](https://www.webjunction.org/news/webjunction/recap-learning-circles.html)). No source found reports how many actually did.
- From 2021, a pilot with College Unbound and Providence Public Library let learners earn college credit through circles in public speaking, cybersecurity and anti-racism, framed as a low-stakes way to try a degree ([Siegel Family Endowment, 2021](https://siegelendowment.org/?p=1455)).

**Facilitators**
- Facilitators did not need subject expertise; P2PU supplied a free toolkit (facilitator guide, promotion materials, "recipe cards" for group discussion) ([WebJunction, 2016](https://www.webjunction.org/news/webjunction/recap-learning-circles.html)).
- Most facilitators were librarians, and many new ones were onboarded by colleagues rather than by P2PU ([P2PU, 2019](https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/1943/feed_items/2488971)).
- Some librarians found the shift from instructor to facilitator hard, were uneasy without deep content knowledge, or worried about time and the unpredictability of learner-led groups; peer onboarding and co-facilitating a first circle worked best; large virtual trainings were less effective than in-person support for some staff ([P2PU, 2018](https://medium.com/p2pu/learning-about-learning-circles-56dc25320f38)).
- By August 2019: 178+ circles, 12 partner libraries, 1,500+ patrons in 10 states, about 8 learners per circle. Facilitators drifted toward topics they already knew. Two partner libraries stopped, mainly because thinly staffed branches could not spare time ([P2PU, 2019](https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/1943/feed_items/2488971)).
- In response P2PU planned a facilitator resource page, an online course underpinning workshops, an organizer page for onboarding colleagues, and volunteer (non-librarian) facilitator pathways with Carnegie Library of Pittsburgh ([P2PU, 2019](https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/1943/feed_items/2488971)). A 2023 newsletter mentions a surge of volunteer-facilitated circles in Pittsburgh, no numbers ([P2PU, Nov 2023](https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/1943/feed_items/9034545)).

**Funding and platform**
- P2PU was almost entirely grant-funded (Knight, Open Society, IMLS, Siegel, Dollar General and others); in 2018 it set a target of $90k–150k/year unrestricted revenue (30–50% of operating costs) and ruled out paywalls and freemium ([P2PU, 2018](https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/1943/feed_items/2458337)).
- It then moved to paid library membership plus consulting; some partners went from being paid by P2PU to paying P2PU; some could not afford membership ([Siegel Family Endowment, 2021](https://siegelendowment.org/?p=1455)).
- Free courses that libraries relied on disappeared or went behind paywalls, pushing some libraries to proprietary databases such as Lynda.com ([P2PU, 2019](https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/1943/feed_items/2488971)).
- In November 2023 P2PU reported two departures from a small team and was hiring a part-time community coordinator on a three-month contract ([P2PU, Nov 2023](https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/1943/feed_items/9034545)).

## Failure modes seen
- **Facilitator-interest drift:** Facilitators gravitated to topics they already knew, and circles chosen this way drew low attendance ([P2PU, 2019](https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/1943/feed_items/2488971); [P2PU, 2018](https://medium.com/p2pu/learning-about-learning-circles-56dc25320f38)).
- **Host capacity exit:** Two of 12 partner libraries stopped running circles because small branches lacked staff time ([P2PU, 2019](https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/1943/feed_items/2488971)).
- **Content floor disappears:** The free courses the model depended on vanished or were paywalled ([P2PU, 2019](https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/1943/feed_items/2488971)).
- **Grant dependency:** The model was built on grants and had to be refinanced through fees that some partners could not pay ([Siegel Family Endowment, 2021](https://siegelendowment.org/?p=1455)).
- **Intent without a next step:** 90% said they wanted another course; no published follow-through number ([WebJunction, 2016](https://www.webjunction.org/news/webjunction/recap-learning-circles.html)).

## 5. What it tells us about the problem

- If the host's staff time is what runs out first, where does the facilitation load sit when the host is a branch library?
- What evidence would show whether people who say they'll come back actually come back?
- What happens to a peer-learning route when the content it rides on is withdrawn by a third party?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- The 45–55% retention figure is a staff estimate reported in two articles; no method or denominator is published. The TASCHA survey covers people who signed up online and answered, so it likely over-represents engaged learners.
- No published count of facilitators trained versus facilitators still active.
- **Rival explanation:** Higher retention than solo MOOCs may reflect who walks into a library program (self-motivated, local, already library users) rather than the circle format itself; the 2017 ethnography described both learners and facilitators as highly self-motivated ([P2PU, 2017](https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/1943/feed_items/2231405)).
- Could confirm: P2PU staff; Chicago, Kansas City, Pittsburgh or Providence library program coordinators; P2PU's impact report (announced for late 2023, not located).
