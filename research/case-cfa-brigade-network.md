# Case: Code for America Brigade Network, a national hub supporting local volunteer civic-tech chapters, then letting them go

**Kind:** failure
**Bears on:** facilitators, activation-mobilization, post-cycle, interfaces
**Logged:** 2026-11-05 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
A network of local volunteer "Brigades" (civic technologists meeting at regular project nights) that Code for America (CfA) supported with money handling, brand, tools and convenings from about 2012 until it ended the program in 2023.

## 2. Where it came from
Code for America is a San Francisco-based nonprofit that builds technology with governments. This file draws on CfA's own announcement and reflection posts, the CfA Network forum thread where Brigade leaders responded, trade-press reporting (StateScoop, GovTech, Technical.ly), and the Open Collective pages of the successor umbrella group and the fiscal host many Brigades moved to.

## 3. When
- Brigade program: offered local tools since 2012 per GovTech (2016); ended 2023.
- First funding crisis reported July 2016.
- Ending announced 1 Feb 2023; contractual support ended June 2023; grace period through 31 Dec 2023.
- Follow-on: the fiscal host many Brigades chose announced its own dissolution in early 2024.
- Sources dated 2016–2024.

## 4. What it says
**Before the ending: an earlier crisis (2016)**
- In 2016 the Brigade program hit a funding shortfall: expenses had grown ahead of funding that did not close, and a major grant's timing made it worse. ([GovTech, 2016](https://govtech.com/civic/this-week-in-civic-tech-code-for-america-brigade-faces-funding-crisis.html))
- The same report describes tension between local chapters and national management, several local leaders stepping down, leaders reporting burnout from balancing volunteer work with their lives, and at least one large Brigade (Code for Philly) weighing independence. ([GovTech, 2016](https://govtech.com/civic/this-week-in-civic-tech-code-for-america-brigade-faces-funding-crisis.html))

**The ending (2023)**
- On 1 Feb 2023 CfA told Brigades on its network forum that it would stop handling Brigade finances, redefine the relationship (possibly as partnership or licensing), and pilot a digital-equity effort in up to three states. Reasons given: less funding for a decentralized network, CfA's shift to direct service work, volunteer fatigue, post-pandemic declines in volunteering, and privacy/security concerns about volunteers working with governments. Three network staff positions were eliminated. ([CfA Network Discourse, 1 Feb 2023](https://discourse.codeforamerica.org/t/2023-network-announcements-and-reflections/1640))
- CfA's public reflection (10 Mar 2023) said it was "not best suited to serve as a central supporting organization" for a volunteer network and saw itself as better placed as a partner to local organizations. It also said some Brigades had already gone independent successfully, which informed the decision. ([CfA, 10 Mar 2023](https://codeforamerica.org/news/reflections-on-the-brigade-networks-next-chapter/))
- StateScoop reported the decision affected about 60 volunteer chapters; the Brigade program's 2022 budget exceeded $3 million with 14 full-time staff; chapters with "Code for" names were told to rebrand. Additional reasons reported: costs growing faster than fundraising, and short-term volunteer projects that struggled to create lasting impact. ([StateScoop, 17 May 2023](https://statescoop.com/code-for-america-former-brigades-regroup/))

**What Brigades did next**
- Forum replies show Brigades comparing fiscal hosts (an 8% fee at Open Collective Foundation, a 9% fee at another sponsor, a two-administrator minimum), worrying the national convening would end and small Brigades would weaken, and one leader saying a one-person Brigade would likely close. ([CfA Network Discourse, 2023](https://discourse.codeforamerica.org/t/2023-network-announcements-and-reflections/1640))
- A former CfA Brigade program manager organized a smaller umbrella, the Alliance of Civic Technologists, launching with Brigades in 16 cities, a Discord server, no fiscal oversight and no annual conference; some local groups called CfA's decision a "death knell." ([StateScoop, 2023](https://statescoop.com/code-for-america-former-brigades-regroup/))
- Code for DC (founded 2012) had already gone on hiatus in June 2022, restarted in January 2023, lost many earlier projects and its ties to other Brigades during the hiatus, moved to Open Collective Foundation as fiscal sponsor, and renamed itself Civic Tech DC in October 2023 by member vote. It reported 30–40 attendees per meetup since August 2023. ([Technical.ly, 2 Nov 2023](https://technical.ly/civic-news/code-for-dc-rebrand-civic-tech-dc))

**The second shock (2024)**
- Open Collective Foundation, the fiscal host many ex-Brigades moved to, announced it would dissolve by the end of 2024; its fiscal sponsorship ended 30 Sep 2024, and it says "many collectives" had to cease operations while others formed their own 501(c)(3)s or moved hosts. No reasons are given on its page. ([OCF board update, Oct 2024](https://opencollective.com/foundation/updates/an-update-from-open-collective-foundations-board-of-directors))
- The Alliance of Civic Technologists itself could no longer accept donations from 15 Mar 2024 and was weighing a new sponsor or its own nonprofit. ([ACT update on Open Collective, 15 Mar 2024](https://opencollective.com/act-fund/updates/act-ocf-update))

## Failure modes seen
- **Hub cost outruns hub funding.** The national support layer twice ran short of money (2016 shortfall; 2023 decision citing costs growing faster than fundraising). ([GovTech, 2016](https://govtech.com/civic/this-week-in-civic-tech-code-for-america-brigade-faces-funding-crisis.html); [StateScoop, 2023](https://statescoop.com/code-for-america-former-brigades-regroup/))
- **Mission drift at the hub.** CfA's direct government-service work grew and the volunteer network no longer fit its main line of work. ([CfA, 2023](https://codeforamerica.org/news/reflections-on-the-brigade-networks-next-chapter/))
- **Borrowed infrastructure as a single point of failure.** Brigades depended on the hub for money handling, name and tools; after the split many moved to one fiscal host that then dissolved. ([OCF, 2024](https://opencollective.com/foundation/updates/an-update-from-open-collective-foundations-board-of-directors))
- **Single-organizer chapters.** A forum reply expected a one-person Brigade to close once support ended. ([CfA Network Discourse, 2023](https://discourse.codeforamerica.org/t/2023-network-announcements-and-reflections/1640))
- **Lost memory during a pause.** Code for DC's 2022 hiatus cost it most earlier projects and its links to other Brigades. ([Technical.ly, 2023](https://technical.ly/civic-news/code-for-dc-rebrand-civic-tech-dc))
- **Leader burnout.** Local leaders stepped down in 2016 citing difficulty balancing volunteer work and life. ([GovTech, 2016](https://govtech.com/civic/this-week-in-civic-tech-code-for-america-brigade-faces-funding-crisis.html))

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- What exactly did local Brigades get from the national layer that they could not get alone (money handling, name, tools, convenings), and which of those did they actually miss after 2023?
- When a chapter pauses (Code for DC, 2022), where does the knowledge of its projects and relationships live, and who can pick it back up?
- How many local units in a network depend on one or two people, and what happens to the people who were attending them when that person stops?
- What does a volunteer who joined for a short project expect to happen after it ends, and did the "short-term projects" critique describe volunteers' experience or the hub's funders' expectations?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- We do not have Brigade-level attendance or retention data, nor a count of how many of the ~60 Brigades still operate in 2026.
- The 2023 "reasons" are CfA's own account; Brigade leaders' private views appear only in a public forum thread and press quotes.
- **Rival explanation:** the network may have ended less because a central hub is the wrong model and more because of one organization's funding and strategy choices (a large 2022 gift to CfA's safety-net work is reported by StateScoop); a differently funded hub might have held.
- Who could confirm: the Alliance of Civic Technologists; Civic Tech DC organizers; CfA's network team (former); researchers of civic tech (e.g., the Civic Tech Field Guide).
