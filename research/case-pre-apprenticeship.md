# Case: US pre-apprenticeship, a defined on-ramp whose exit into the main program is the weak link

**Kind:** maximum variation (a federally defined pre-program with thousands of local versions and thin evidence)
**Bears on:** pre-cycle, post-cycle, facilitators
**Product part:** pre-cycle | kits
**Logged:** 2026-11-06 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
"Pre-apprenticeship" is the US Department of Labor's name for a short program (typically about 12 weeks) meant to prepare people to enter and succeed in a Registered Apprenticeship. DOL defines it and publishes a quality framework but does not register or certify it; what happens at the exit — whether participants actually enter an apprenticeship — is the main thing evaluators have tried to measure.

## 2. Where it came from
US DOL Employment and Training Administration (TEN 13-12, 2012; TEN 23-23, 2024; TEN 05-26, 2026). Evidence draws on the Abt Associates outcomes study of the American Apprenticeship Initiative (AAI) for DOL (2022), a Jobs for the Future (JFF) study of youth transitions (2026), an AIR state-strategies report (2026), the Urban Institute's description of DOL's current evaluation portfolio (2023), and a New America analysis of the 2026 guidance.

## 3. When
Definition issued November 30, 2012; AAI grants 2015–2020 with outcomes reported August 2022; JFF and AIR reports May–June 2026; DOL's Urban Institute/Mathematica evaluation runs 2022–2027.

## 4. What it says

**The definition**
- TEN 13-12 (Nov. 30, 2012) defines pre-apprenticeship as a program or set of strategies designed to prepare people to enter and succeed in Registered Apprenticeship, requiring a documented partnership with at least one Registered Apprenticeship program. Its six quality elements: industry-approved curriculum; strategies to increase apprenticeship access for under-represented people; access to support services; promoting apprenticeship to employers; hands-on training that does not replace paid workers; and "facilitated entry and/or articulation" into apprenticeship where possible. ETA does not register or certify pre-apprenticeship programs. The framework came from listening sessions and an advisory committee, not from outcome evaluations. [CLASP copy of TEN 13-12](https://www.clasp.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/01_Defining20a20Quality20Pre-Apprenticeship20Program20and20Related20Tools20and20Resources2028TEGL2013-1229-2.pdf); the original DOL notice is now marked rescinded [DOL TEN 13-12](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/advisories/training-and-employment-notice-no-13-12)
- The 2026 replacement (TEN 05-26) keeps a five-part framework — direct pathways into Registered Apprenticeship, industry-driven curriculum, work-based learning, broad partnerships, outcomes tracking — and, per New America, drops the 2024 guidance's discussion of recruiting and serving marginalized communities and says less about supportive services. New America's line: pre-apprenticeship "should not become a default prerequisite." Programs "vary wildly in design and quality." [New America, What to know about DOL's new pre-apprenticeship guidance](https://www.newamerica.org/insights/what-to-know-about-dols-new-pre-apprenticeship-guidance/)

**Evidence on entry into apprenticeship**
- AAI (46 grantees, $175M): 6,282 pre-apprentices in program records; programs averaged about three months; about 81% completed; about 63% of completers went on to a Registered Apprenticeship, which was the most common next step in every occupation except computer/IT. Over half of grantees reported there were not enough apprenticeship positions for all pre-apprentices. 90% of pre-apprentices were women, people of color, or veterans, versus 61% of apprentices. Only about 17% of AAI apprentices had come through a pre-apprenticeship. [Abt/DOL, AAI outcomes final report, Aug. 2022](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/ETA/publications/ETAOP2022-35_AAI_Outcomes_Final_Report_508_9-2022.pdf)
- JFF youth study (2026): in administrative data on 801 pre-apprenticeship completers, 64 (8%) went on to a Registered Apprenticeship; in a 13-person youth survey, 2 entered one within six months. Other outcomes were more common: further education (31%), non-apprenticeship work (23%), and out of work and school (23%). The authors call immediate transitions "relatively rare" and say the data do not support causal claims. Barriers named: too few apprenticeship slots, pressure to take any job now, transport and scheduling, caregiving, limited understanding of apprenticeship's long-term value, and some sites with unsupportive workplaces. [JFF, Understanding youth movement from pre-apprenticeship to Registered Apprenticeship, key learnings](https://www.jff.org/idea/understanding-youth-movement-from-pre-apprenticeship-to-registered-apprenticeship/key-learnings/)
- AIR state survey (2026): 41 of 55 state/territory offices responded, reporting 2,209 pre-apprenticeship programs; 73% of states track entry into apprenticeship and 85% track completion, but data methods are inconsistent and some cover only grant-funded programs. South Carolina requires at least 50% of completers to enter a Registered Apprenticeship for full certification; a 2023 state audit found fewer pre-apprentices transitioning than expected and named inconsistent design, limited employer connections, and outcome windows too short to measure. Pennsylvania's early registration collected so little that it lacks information on connections to apprenticeship. [AIR, Strengthening the apprenticeship pipeline, May 2026](https://air.org/sites/default/files/2026-05/Pre-Apprenticeship-Case-Study-May2026_1.pdf)
- DOL's current evaluation (Urban Institute with Mathematica and SPR, 2022–2027) includes an "evaluability assessment" of pre-apprenticeship impact — i.e., whether a rigorous impact study is even feasible — and states there is "little rigorous evidence" on pre-apprenticeship impact. No findings yet. [Urban Institute for DOL CEO, Registered Apprenticeship initiatives evaluation one-pager, Aug. 2023](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/OASP/evaluation/one-pagers/Registered-Apprenticeship-Initiatives-Evaluation_OnePager.pdf)

**What the case assumes that The Labs can't**
- Paid staff and grant funding; employers who hold the exit slots; a credential at the end; and in the youth versions, a school calendar and minors.

## Failure modes seen
- **Exit slots controlled by someone else:** over half of AAI grantees said there were not enough apprenticeship positions for their pre-apprentices; JFF found the same in regions with few participating employers. [Abt/DOL 2022](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/ETA/publications/ETAOP2022-35_AAI_Outcomes_Final_Report_508_9-2022.pdf); [JFF 2026](https://www.jff.org/idea/understanding-youth-movement-from-pre-apprenticeship-to-registered-apprenticeship/key-learnings/)
- **On-ramp becomes a gate:** New America warns the guidance risks making pre-apprenticeship a default prerequisite and the only youth entry route. [New America 2026](https://www.newamerica.org/insights/what-to-know-about-dols-new-pre-apprenticeship-guidance/)
- **Nobody counts the crossing:** states and programs track completion more than entry, with inconsistent methods, so transition rates are often unknown. [AIR 2026](https://air.org/sites/default/files/2026-05/Pre-Apprenticeship-Case-Study-May2026_1.pdf)
- **Definition without certification:** DOL defines quality but does not certify programs, so "pre-apprenticeship" covers thousands of unlike programs. [TEN 13-12 via CLASP](https://www.clasp.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/01_Defining20a20Quality20Pre-Apprenticeship20Program20and20Related20Tools20and20Resources2028TEGL2013-1229-2.pdf)
- **Compressed or virtual on-ramps:** youth and practitioners told JFF that virtual, compressed programs needed more in-person, hands-on time at a slower pace. [JFF 2026](https://www.jff.org/idea/understanding-youth-movement-from-pre-apprenticeship-to-registered-apprenticeship/key-learnings/)

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- In pre-apprenticeship the thing the on-ramp leads to (an apprenticeship slot) is scarce and owned by employers. Is a Cycle seat scarce, and if not, what is the pre-cycle preparing people *for* that could run out?
- Transition rates range from 63% (AAI) to 8% (JFF youth sample) depending on who runs the program and who is counted. What would The Labs count as "entered the Cycle," and from which denominator?
- A 2012 definition with six elements and no certification produced 2,000+ unlike programs. If kits are modular and any lab can run them, what keeps "pre-cycle" from meaning something different at every branch?
- South Carolina ties certification to a 50% entry rate. Would any threshold like that make sense for a volunteer-run kit, and what would it do to who gets admitted?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
No study here is causal; AAI and JFF are outcomes tracking with no comparison group, and DOL itself says rigorous evidence is thin. The 63% AAI transition figure is for grant-funded programs with built-in apprenticeship partners, so it is an upper bound. **Rival explanation:** high transition rates where they occur may reflect employers selecting pre-apprentices they had already decided to hire (AAI notes apprentices who had done a pre-apprenticeship most often did it with the same employer), so the program certifies a hire rather than creating one. DOL's Chief Evaluation Office, the Urban Institute evaluation team, and state apprenticeship offices with audit data (South Carolina) could confirm.
