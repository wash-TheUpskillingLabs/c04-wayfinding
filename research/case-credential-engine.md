# Case: Credential Engine, counting and registering every US credential to make them comparable

**Kind:** extreme (the largest attempt to make the credential landscape legible)
**Bears on:** pre-cycle, post-cycle, interfaces
**Field claims:** 2, 3
**Evidence:** supports 3; complicates 2 (the count grew from about 1.08 million in 2022 to 1.85 million in 2025, but the reports carry no national outcomes data and observers said listing what exists is less useful than showing results; [CC Daily, 2025](https://www.ccdaily.com/2025/12/credential-count-grows-in-the-u-s/), [EdSurge, 2022](https://edsurge.com/news/2022-12-08-a-nonprofit-spent-five-years-counting-a-million-credentials-what-does-it-all-add-up-to))
**Logged:** 2026-11-05 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
Credential Engine is a US nonprofit that publishes periodic "Counting Credentials" reports and runs the Credential Registry, a public database where providers describe credentials in a common language (the Credential Transparency Description Language, CTDL) so others can find and compare them.

## 2. Where it came from
- Credential Engine publishes the counts and runs the registry with partner states.
- Sources: Credential Engine's 2025 executive summary and live registry statistics; news coverage in Community College Daily (2025) and EdSurge (2022); a 2021 commentary on the earlier count.

## 3. When
- First count 2018; further reports 2019, 2021, 2022, 2025 (dates as given in the sources below).
- 2025 report: December 2025. Registry statistics: live page, read November 2026.

## 4. What it says
- The 2021 report counted 967,734 credentials, about three times the first (2018) count, with badge and course-certificate growth largely because platforms reported more. ([Boston, 2021](https://wallyboston.com/the-counting-of-u-s-postsecondary-and-secondary-credentials/))
- The 2022 report counted 1,076,358 credentials and 59,692 providers. ([EdSurge, December 2022](https://edsurge.com/news/2022-12-08-a-nonprofit-spent-five-years-counting-a-million-credentials-what-does-it-all-add-up-to))
- The 2025 report counts 1,850,034 unique credentials from 134,491 providers, with $2.34 trillion spent each year on delivering and earning credentials. Badges are 1,022,028 of the total; degrees 264,099; certificates 486,352. ([Credential Engine, 2025](https://credentialengine.org/all-resources/2025-counting-credentials/))
- Part of the badge growth came from better reporting by major badge platforms, not only new offerings. ([CC Daily, December 2025](https://www.ccdaily.com/2025/12/credential-count-grows-in-the-u-s/))
- The 2025 summary has no national employment or earnings outcomes; outcome data appears only for some states (e.g., Ohio adding workforce outcomes for about 800 occupations). ([Credential Engine, 2025](https://credentialengine.org/all-resources/2025-counting-credentials/))
- The live Credential Registry lists 134,463 credentials, 33,829 published organizations and 91,281 learning opportunities. ([Credential Engine Registry data, read Nov 2026](https://apps.credentialengine.org/services/reports/CredentialRegistryData)) By our arithmetic this is roughly 7% of the 1.85 million counted (different dates; not a like-for-like measure).
- State partners have published thousands each: e.g., Florida over 10,000, Georgia over 7,000, Alabama over 1,530. ([Credential Engine, 2025](https://credentialengine.org/all-resources/2025-counting-credentials/))
- The board chair told EdSurge the systems are not designed to say which program is best. Workforce-sector observers said listing is a first step, that results matter more, and that employers may keep using old hiring methods. A Texas Workforce Commission official said the first year was about quantity and wanted to move to quality. ([EdSurge, 2022](https://edsurge.com/news/2022-12-08-a-nonprofit-spent-five-years-counting-a-million-credentials-what-does-it-all-add-up-to))
- A commentator cited an estimate that only around 1,000 of the credentials meaningfully improve economic mobility. ([Boston, 2021](https://wallyboston.com/the-counting-of-u-s-postsecondary-and-secondary-credentials/))

## Failure modes seen
- **Visibility without valuation:** The registry is a catalog, not a ranking, and does not show which credentials pay off ([EdSurge, 2022](https://edsurge.com/news/2022-12-08-a-nonprofit-spent-five-years-counting-a-million-credentials-what-does-it-all-add-up-to)).
- **More light, more noise:** Each count is larger than the last (about 0.97M in 2021 to 1.85M in 2025), so transparency made the choice set bigger ([Boston, 2021](https://wallyboston.com/the-counting-of-u-s-postsecondary-and-secondary-credentials/); [Credential Engine, 2025](https://credentialengine.org/all-resources/2025-counting-credentials/)).
- **Partial registry:** About 134,000 credentials are in the registry against 1.85 million counted ([Registry data](https://apps.credentialengine.org/services/reports/CredentialRegistryData); [Credential Engine, 2025](https://credentialengine.org/all-resources/2025-counting-credentials/)).
- **Count driven by reporting:** Badge growth partly reflects better reporting by platforms, not a change in the world ([CC Daily, 2025](https://www.ccdaily.com/2025/12/credential-count-grows-in-the-u-s/)).
- **No behavior change measured:** No source measures whether learners or employers chose differently because of the registry ([EdSurge, 2022](https://edsurge.com/news/2022-12-08-a-nonprofit-spent-five-years-counting-a-million-credentials-what-does-it-all-add-up-to)).

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- The registry figure and the national count come from different dates and may count different things, so the ~7% ratio is approximate.
- Most coverage is from favorable trade press; the critical voices are quoted in one 2022 article and one 2021 blog.
- **Rival explanation:** The registry's job may be infrastructure for state tools (e.g., New Jersey's comparison tool, Alabama's registry), so its effect would show up in those tools, not in the registry itself. Low direct use would then be expected, not a failure.
- Could confirm: Credential Engine, partner state agencies (Texas, New Jersey, Alabama), and researchers on non-degree credential quality (e.g., New America).
