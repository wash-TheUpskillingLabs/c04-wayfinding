# UX case (counterexample): Snapchat 2018 redesign, a celebrated app that moved everything and lost its users' bearings
**Kind:** wayfinding
**Recognition:** Chosen as the counterexample because the failure is documented in the company's own SEC filing and earnings statements, not only in complaints: Snap attributed a quarter-on-quarter fall in daily active users to the redesign and then reversed parts of it. Sources below.
**Logged:** 2026-11-06

## What it is and who made it
On 29 November 2017 Snap announced a redesign of Snapchat that separated "social" from "media": friends' chats and stories on the left of the camera, ordered dynamically by how often you communicate; publishers, creators and community content on the right, algorithmically personalised with human curation of promoted items; the app still opened to the camera. Snap's stated reason was that mixing friends' posts with professional content caused problems including misinformation and pressure to perform ([Snap Newsroom, Introducing the new Snapchat](https://newsroom.snap.com/introducing-the-new-snapchat)). The broad rollout came in February 2018 ([Snap Inc. Form 10-Q, Q2 2018](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001564408/000156459018020094/snap-10q_20180630.htm)).

## What it does well (specific, observable design decisions)
- The stated intent was a clear spatial rule: people on one side, media on the other, camera in the middle ([Snap Newsroom, 2017](https://newsroom.snap.com/introducing-the-new-snapchat)).
- The rationale was published on launch day, with reasons rather than slogans ([Snap Newsroom, 2017](https://newsroom.snap.com/introducing-the-new-snapchat)).

## Where it fails or was criticized (sourced)
- **The company's own attribution.** The 10-Q states: "we rolled out a redesign of the Snapchat application, which negatively impacted our DAUs" (February 2018 rollout) ([Snap 10-Q, Q2 2018](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001564408/000156459018020094/snap-10q_20180630.htm)).
- **Users could not find their friends' content.** After the backlash, Snap rolled back several changes: snaps and chats returned to chronological order, friends' stories moved back to the right, and a left swipe again showed friends' posts ([MacRumors, 7 Aug 2018](https://macrumors.com/2018/08/07/snapchat-redesign-millions-of-lost-users)).
- **Organised objection.** More than 1.2 million people signed a Change.org petition asking Snap to reverse the redesign ([MacRumors, 2018](https://macrumors.com/2018/08/07/snapchat-redesign-millions-of-lost-users)).
- The CEO said the DAU drop was primarily driven by lower usage frequency caused by disruption from the redesign, and that about six months after rollout the biggest frustrations had been addressed ([MacRumors, 2018](https://macrumors.com/2018/08/07/snapchat-redesign-millions-of-lost-users)).

## Measured effects, if any (sourced)
- DAUs fell from 191 million in Q1 2018 to 188 million in Q2 2018, about 2%, while monthly actives kept growing ([MacRumors, 2018](https://macrumors.com/2018/08/07/snapchat-redesign-millions-of-lost-users)); the 10-Q gives 188 million for Q2 2018, up 8% year on year, and states the 2% quarter-on-quarter decline ([Snap 10-Q, Q2 2018](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001564408/000156459018020094/snap-10q_20180630.htm)).
- Rival explanation worth holding: the filing attributes the decline to the redesign, but year-on-year growth continued and monthly actives rose, so the loss is in frequency, not in reach; competing products are not separated out in the sources opened.

## What we can borrow without copying (patterns, not visuals)
- A clean conceptual map ("people left, media right") is not the same as a usable one if it moves what people had already learned to find.
- Changing order (chronological to algorithmic) is itself a wayfinding change; it removes a cue users navigate by.
- When a redesign ships, publish the reasons and watch frequency of return, not only totals.
