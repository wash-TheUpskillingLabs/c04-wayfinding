# Case: Mozilla Hive Learning Networks and Open Badges, making learning visible across a city's organizations

**Kind:** failure / transition (sponsor exit; portable-credential infrastructure retired)
**Bears on:** interfaces, post-cycle, facilitators, activation-mobilization
**Logged:** 2026-11-05 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
Hive Learning Networks were city networks of museums, libraries, schools and youth programs, funded largely by the MacArthur Foundation and stewarded by Mozilla, running "connected learning" programs for teens. Open Badges was a Mozilla-launched open standard (2011) for digital credentials, with a Mozilla "Backpack" where learners stored badges from many issuers.

## 2. Where it came from
Mozilla Foundation with the MacArthur Foundation's Digital Media and Learning initiative and local funders. This file draws on Mozilla blog and wiki posts (2014–2019), Chicago Learning Exchange (2017), Next City reporting (2014), an AACE review (2019) and an Open Recognition critique (2017).

## 3. When
- Hive Chicago stewarded by DePaul University 2009–2013, Mozilla 2013–2017, Chicago Learning Exchange from January 2018 ([Chicago Learning Exchange, 2017](https://chicagolx.opalstacked.com/news/same-hive-new-steward)).
- Mozilla stewardship of Hive NYC from 2011 ([Mozilla wiki, 2014](https://wiki.mozilla.org/Webmaker/HiveNYC)).
- Mozilla announced the end of its Hive and Clubs stewardship in November 2017 ([Mozilla, 2017](https://blog.mozilla.org/foundation-archive/mozilla-science/mozilla-learning-updates-transitions-sunsets/)).
- Mozilla Backpack retired August 2019; users could migrate to Badgr ([Mozilla, 2019](https://medium.com/read-write-participate/the-mozilla-backpack-is-transitioning-to-badgr-c080e68d5603)).

## 4. What it says

**Structure (network shape)**
- Hive had three tiers: one-off events (pop-ups, Maker Parties); local "learning communities" that could use the Hive name and borrow tools; and full city "learning networks" requiring at least one full-time staff member, a $150K+ annual budget and a grant program seeding at least $15K, with admission reviewed by MacArthur, Mozilla and an outside panel ([Mozilla, 2014](https://blog.mozilla.org/foundation-archive/mozilla-science/three-tiers-hive/)).
- Pop-ups were designed for three levels of participation: sampling, light engagement with everything, and focusing on one activity ([Mozilla, 2014](https://blog.mozilla.org/foundation-archive/mozilla-science/three-tiers-hive/)).
- Hive NYC (c. 2014) had 55 member organizations, reported 20,000+ youth engaged, and a pooled donor fund at The New York Community Trust; a Mozilla-run HQ provided convening, funding, professional development and documentation ([Mozilla wiki, 2014](https://wiki.mozilla.org/Webmaker/HiveNYC)).

**Making learning visible across places (badges)**
- Chicago Summer of Learning (2013) used Open Badges so summer activities across about 100 organizations could follow youth into school; about 150,000 badges were issued ([Next City, 2014](https://nextcity.org/urbanist-news/connected-learning-chicago-tests-digital-badges-to-track-education)).
- Only about half of earned badges were claimed into digital backpacks; some programs used printed claim codes that staff suspected were lost. About a quarter of participants were under 13, and federal children's privacy rules limited sharing and required parental oversight ([Next City, 2014](https://nextcity.org/urbanist-news/connected-learning-chicago-tests-digital-badges-to-track-education)).
- A 2017 critique argued the Backpack let people collect and display badges but not issue them, and that issuing required running a server, leaving recognition in institutions' hands; it cited a 2014 review of 30 badge projects finding existing ecosystems constrained recognition practice ([Open Recognition, 2017](https://medium.com/openrecognition/open-recognition-and-its-enemies-1-the-genesis-of-open-badges-2a4872c2b234)).

**Sponsor exit**
- In November 2017 Mozilla said it would end management of Hive (including Gigabit Hive), Mozilla Clubs and grassroots web literacy programs over the coming year, to focus on internet-health fights; no successor stewards were named in that post ([Mozilla, 2017](https://blog.mozilla.org/foundation-archive/mozilla-science/mozilla-learning-updates-transitions-sunsets/)).
- Chicago Learning Exchange said the move followed the end of large multi-year MacArthur and National Science Foundation grants; Hive Chicago became one program of the new local nonprofit, keeping meetups, RFP grants, membership and working groups, with funding secured into 2019 ([Chicago Learning Exchange, 2017](https://chicagolx.opalstacked.com/news/same-hive-new-steward)).
- Mozilla's current page lists only Chicago (Chicago Learning Exchange) and Hive NYC as continuing ([Mozilla Foundation](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/artifacts/hive-learning-networks)).

**Credential infrastructure exit**
- In August 2019 Mozilla retired the Backpack, moving Open Badges stewardship to IMS Global and offering users an emailed link to migrate to Concentric Sky's Badgr; migration was optional and the post did not say what happened to unmigrated badges ([Mozilla, 2019](https://medium.com/read-write-participate/the-mozilla-backpack-is-transitioning-to-badgr-c080e68d5603); [AACE Review, 2019](https://aace.org/review/goodbye-backpack-hello-badgr-open-badge-platforms/)).
- By then the market had split across many platforms (Badgr, Credly, Badgecraft, Accredible, Open Badge Factory and others) ([AACE Review, 2019](https://aace.org/review/goodbye-backpack-hello-badgr-open-badge-platforms/)).

## Failure modes seen
- **Earned but never claimed:** Roughly half of Chicago's 2013 summer badges never reached a learner's backpack ([Next City, 2014](https://nextcity.org/urbanist-news/connected-learning-chicago-tests-digital-badges-to-track-education)).
- **Record outlives its host:** The central place learners kept badges was retired, with migration left to each user ([Mozilla, 2019](https://medium.com/read-write-participate/the-mozilla-backpack-is-transitioning-to-badgr-c080e68d5603)).
- **Sponsor priorities move:** Mozilla exited city networks when its strategy and grant funding changed ([Mozilla, 2017](https://blog.mozilla.org/foundation-archive/mozilla-science/mozilla-learning-updates-transitions-sunsets/); [Chicago Learning Exchange, 2017](https://chicagolx.opalstacked.com/news/same-hive-new-steward)).
- **Recognition held by issuers only:** Learners could display but not issue, per a critic ([Open Recognition, 2017](https://medium.com/openrecognition/open-recognition-and-its-enemies-1-the-genesis-of-open-badges-2a4872c2b234)).
- **High bar for the top tier:** Full network status required paid staff and a $150K+ budget, so most places stayed at events or informal communities ([Mozilla, 2014](https://blog.mozilla.org/foundation-archive/mozilla-science/three-tiers-hive/)).

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- When a learner's record of what they've done lives on a platform, what happens to it if the platform or its steward goes away?
- Of the people who earn a recognition, how many ever see it, keep it, or show it to anyone, and who would measure that?
- Which parts of a network survived the sponsor's exit (Chicago, New York) and what did those places already have locally?
- Who can issue recognition: only the organization, or also peers and the learner?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- No independent evaluation of Hive outcomes was opened; numbers are Mozilla's own. The Next City badge figures come from program staff. The Open Recognition piece is advocacy.
- Fate of other Hives (Pittsburgh, Toronto, Bay Area, etc.) after 2017 not verified.
- **Rival explanation:** Badge loss may say more about printed claim codes, under-13 privacy limits and a short summer program than about portable credentials as such; and Hive's contraction may reflect the end of a fixed MacArthur funding cycle rather than any weakness in the network design.
- Could confirm: Chicago Learning Exchange; Hive NYC; MacArthur Foundation grant records; 1EdTech (formerly IMS Global) on Open Badges adoption.
