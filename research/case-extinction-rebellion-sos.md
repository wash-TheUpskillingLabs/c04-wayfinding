# Case: Extinction Rebellion UK, a self-organising system of circles and local groups built for mass "rebellions," and the cycle of peak and exhaustion

**Kind:** extreme (mass-mobilization-first design) with documented failure modes
**Bears on:** activation-mobilization, pre-cycle, facilitators, interfaces, post-cycle
**Logged:** 2026-11-05 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
A UK climate movement (founded 2018) with autonomous local groups, a national "Hive" of circles, no central membership list, and a "self-organising system" (SOS) of mandated roles; it organized its energy around periodic mass actions, paused disruptive tactics in January 2023 to build numbers for "The Big One" (April 2023), and has repeatedly reported problems onboarding and integrating new people.

## 2. Where it came from
XR UK's own published strategy and movement update, journalism (Novara Media, Prospect, Inside Climate News), and an assessment by a former XR organizer in Waging Nonviolence.

## 3. When
- Founded 2018; April 2019 "Easter Rebellion"; October 2019 "International Rebellion."
- February 2020: shift to a more decentralized structure; 2020 "Systems Realignment Project."
- 31 Dec 2022 / 1 Jan 2023: "We Quit" announcement.
- 21–24 Apr 2023: The Big One. 28 May 2023: strategy "Here Comes Everyone."
- Sources dated October 2020 to mid-2023.

## 4. What it says
**Structure**
- XR UK describes itself as decentralized and non-hierarchical: autonomous local groups, community groups (e.g., XR Youth), and the UK "Rebel Hive" of working groups often called the national team. It aims for consensus, organizes in teams called circles under a self-organising system, has no central membership list, and activists do not formally join. ([Novara Media, Diyora Shadijanova, 21 Apr 2023](https://novaramedia.com/2023/04/21/is-extinction-rebellions-structure-fit-for-purpose/))
- XR's 2020 Systems Realignment Project flagged problems onboarding new members and communicating between circles; later reforms added "integrator" and "gardener" roles to guide new members and "rebellion buses" touring local groups. Only 3% of volunteers receive expenses. ([Novara Media, 2023](https://novaramedia.com/2023/04/21/is-extinction-rebellions-structure-fit-for-purpose/))

**Exhaustion after peaks (2019–2020)**
- By 2020 the central national team had lost capacity partly because XR ran out of money to pay core volunteers; the October 2019 rebellion drew less attention than earlier ones; a local activist warned that the same few people kept getting arrested with few ready to take over; in February 2020 XR adopted a more decentralized structure with national direction set through consultation with regional and local groups. ([Prospect, Eleanor Salter, 9 Oct 2020](https://prospectmagazine.co.uk/politics/40670/a-hostile-government-dwindling-funds-and-activist-exhaustion-whats-next-for-extinction-rebellion))

**The 2023 pivot and The Big One**
- XR UK announced on 31 Dec 2022 it would pause disruptive tactics at least through spring 2023 and aim for 100,000 people at Parliament on 21 April, pledging to "prioritize attendance over arrest and relationships over roadblocks." ([Inside Climate News, Kristoffer Tigue, 6 Jan 2023](https://insideclimatenews.org/todaysclimate/we-quit-extinction-rebellion-vows-to-halt-disruptive-protests-so-whats-next/))
- A former XR organizer estimated 60,000–90,000 at the peak march, with the 100,000 target plausibly met counting turnover across four days, and about 200 supporting organizations; first-time protesters, parents and disabled people found it easier to take part. The problem he identifies was impact: almost no press coverage and no institutional response. He describes XR's tendency toward six-month cycles of mass action followed by exhaustion, which the new strategy aims to break. ([Waging Nonviolence, Douglas Rogers, 8 Jun 2023](https://wagingnonviolence.org/2023/06/extinction-rebellion-uk-big-one/))
- XR UK's own summer 2023 update claimed over 100,000 on the streets and 200+ participating organizations and gave no figures for new joiners; it announced rejigging groups and mandates and overhauling a financial structure dating from its early days. ([XR UK, summer 2023](https://extinctionrebellion.uk/?p=29007))

**XR's own diagnosis (2023–24 strategy)**
- The strategy says the constitution and SOS are not well enough understood inside or outside the movement; that XR cannot easily see how many people are fully mobilized or how that changes with different activities; and that "data processes will be improved to better recruit, integrate and onboard new rebels." Plans include reviewing all circle and role mandates, SOS training and coaching for coordinators, electing local group coordinators at least every six months, publishing all mandates, a sustained Hive–local dialogue, and digital onboarding pathways via social media and email. ([XR UK Rebel Toolkit, 2023–24 Strategy, "Growing Stronger"](https://rebeltoolkit.extinctionrebellion.uk/books/xruk-2023-24-strategy/page/growing-stronger-extinction-rebellion-uks-systems))

## Failure modes seen
- **Peak, then exhaustion.** A former organizer describes six-month cycles of mass action followed by exhaustion. ([Waging Nonviolence, 2023](https://wagingnonviolence.org/2023/06/extinction-rebellion-uk-big-one/))
- **Same few carry the load.** In 2020 a local activist said the same people kept being arrested, with few ready to take over. ([Prospect, 2020](https://prospectmagazine.co.uk/politics/40670/a-hostile-government-dwindling-funds-and-activist-exhaustion-whats-next-for-extinction-rebellion))
- **Unpaid core runs out.** The national team lost capacity when money to support core volunteers ran out. ([Prospect, 2020](https://prospectmagazine.co.uk/politics/40670/a-hostile-government-dwindling-funds-and-activist-exhaustion-whats-next-for-extinction-rebellion))
- **No list, no view of who is active.** With no membership list, XR says it cannot easily see how many people are mobilized. ([XR UK strategy](https://rebeltoolkit.extinctionrebellion.uk/books/xruk-2023-24-strategy/page/growing-stronger-extinction-rebellion-uks-systems); [Novara, 2023](https://novaramedia.com/2023/04/21/is-extinction-rebellions-structure-fit-for-purpose/))
- **System nobody understands.** XR's own strategy says its SOS is not well understood internally. ([XR UK strategy](https://rebeltoolkit.extinctionrebellion.uk/books/xruk-2023-24-strategy/page/growing-stronger-extinction-rebellion-uks-systems))
- **Informal hierarchy inside "structureless" design.** Local activists felt unable to shape national decisions and older, white, middle-class voices often had the final word. ([Novara, 2023](https://novaramedia.com/2023/04/21/is-extinction-rebellions-structure-fit-for-purpose/))
- **Big turnout, no conversion data.** Neither XR nor the assessments report how many Big One attendees joined local groups afterward. ([XR UK update, 2023](https://extinctionrebellion.uk/?p=29007); [Waging Nonviolence, 2023](https://wagingnonviolence.org/2023/06/extinction-rebellion-uk-big-one/))

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- After someone attends a mass action for the first time, what is the next thing they are offered, by whom, and through which channel?
- What did "integrator" and "gardener" roles actually do for a newcomer, and did they last?
- How does a network with no membership list know who is active between peaks, and who decides what "active" means?
- What happens to the people who carry each peak (arrestees, coordinators) in the months after it?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- No public figures on local group counts over time, new joiners after The Big One, or retention.
- Turnout figures for The Big One differ between XR (100,000+) and an independent estimate (60,000–90,000 at peak); the independent assessor is a former XR organizer.
- Strategy documents state intentions; whether the plans were carried out is not documented here.
- **Rival explanation:** XR's decline after 2019 may be driven mostly by external pressure (new UK public-order laws, police and court costs, loss of media novelty) rather than its internal self-organising design.
- Who could confirm: XR UK's integration/onboarding circles; Douglas Rogers (Raveller); researchers of UK climate movements.
