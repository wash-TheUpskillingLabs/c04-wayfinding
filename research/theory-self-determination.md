# Theory: Self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan)

**Bears on:** pre-cycle, cycle, post-cycle, facilitators, activation-mobilization
**Product part:** pre-cycle | post-between | facilitators | kits
**Logged:** 2026-11-06 (desk research, sources below)

Self-determination theory (SDT) is a theory of human motivation built over roughly fifty years by Edward Deci and Richard Ryan and their collaborators. It claims that people have three basic psychological needs (autonomy, competence, relatedness) and that motivation is not one thing but a continuum running from "doing it because I am made to" to "doing it because it is who I am." Its most-used piece for a wayfinding question is the internalization continuum: how a reason for doing something that started outside a person comes to be held as their own.

## Works

### Ryan & Deci, *Self-Determination Theory and the Facilitation of Intrinsic Motivation, Social Development, and Well-Being* (2000)
- **Link:** American Psychologist, 55(1), 68–78, DOI 10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68 — [bibliographic record](https://bibbase.org/network/publication/ryan-deci-selfdeterminationtheoryandthefacilitationofintrinsicmotivationsocialdevelopmentandwellbeing-2000) (citation verified; abstract not on that page).
- **Core idea:** Humans are, by default, curious and active, but that default can be supported or thwarted by social context. Three needs (autonomy, competence, relatedness) determine whether motivation grows or withers. Intrinsic motivation is the clearest case; extrinsic motivation can also become self-endorsed if the needs are met.
- **Vocabulary worth borrowing:** *autonomy* (acting with volition, ownership); *competence* (feeling effective); *relatedness* (reciprocal care with others); *need-supportive context* (a setting that feeds the three needs).
- **Touches:** Describes the conditions under which a person persists at an activity nobody is paying them for, which is the condition of most people at The Labs at every threshold.
- **Critiques and limits:** See the critiques section under Vansteenkiste et al. below. The 2000 paper is a position statement, not a test.

### Vansteenkiste, Niemiec & Soenens, *The development of the five mini-theories of self-determination theory* (2010)
- **Link:** [PDF on selfdeterminationtheory.org](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/2010_VansteenkisteNiemiecSoenens_The_development_of_the_five_mini-theories.pdf), in *Advances in Motivation and Achievement*, vol. 16A, pp. 105–165, Emerald.
- **Core idea:** A historical overview of SDT's sub-theories. The one that matters here is organismic integration theory, which lays out the internalization continuum: *external regulation* (reward/punishment; lasts only while the contingency does) → *introjected regulation* (pride, guilt, shame; partial internalization, internal pressure) → *identified regulation* (the person endorses the value; near-complete internalization) → *integrated regulation* (the value is synthesized into the self). *Amotivation* sits outside the continuum: no intention to act, from feeling incapable, seeing no contingency, or not valuing the activity. The authors stress that even fully internalized extrinsic motivation is still not intrinsic motivation, since the activity is done for a separable outcome.
- **Vocabulary worth borrowing:** *external / introjected / identified / integrated regulation* (the four rungs); *amotivation* (no intention at all); *internalization* (taking an outside reason inward).
- **Touches:** Describes, in motivational terms, what has to change inside a person between "came to a workshop because a friend dragged them" and "maintains a project because it matters to them." Also describes the introjected middle (guilt, pride) that many volunteer programs run on.
- **Critiques and limits:** The authors themselves acknowledge (a) the cultural-relativist critique that autonomy is a Western notion (Markus & Kitayama), which they contest by redefining autonomy as volition rather than independence; (b) that much organismic-integration work rests on self-report questionnaires rather than experiments; (c) that meta-analyses on rewards reached different conclusions (see theory-progress-and-commitment.md, Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999); (d) measurement limits in their own scales.

### Howard, Bureau, Guay, Chong & Ryan, *Student Motivation and Associated Outcomes: A Meta-Analysis From Self-Determination Theory* (2021)
- **Link:** [Perspectives on Psychological Science, 16(6), 1300–1323](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/8w8z7/student-motivation-and-associated-outcomes-a-meta-analysis-from-self-determination-theory), DOI 10.1177/1745691620966789.
- **Core idea:** Across 344 samples and 223,209 participants, each rung of the continuum lines up with different outcomes. Intrinsic motivation tracks success and well-being; identified regulation is strongly linked to persistence; introjected regulation is linked to persistence and performance goals but also to ill-being; external regulation is unrelated to performance or persistence and linked to lower well-being; amotivation tracks poor outcomes.
- **Vocabulary worth borrowing:** *persistence* as an outcome distinct from performance; *ill-being* alongside introjection.
- **Touches:** Describes which kinds of reason for showing up predict staying. The finding that identified regulation, not only intrinsic motivation, predicts persistence bears on post-cycle and activation phases, where the activity may not be fun but may be valued.
- **Critiques and limits:** Associations, not causal effects. The population is students in schools and universities, with the school calendar, grades and compulsory attendance that The Labs cannot assume.

### Zheng, Yao, Zhang, Li & Xing, *Does Basic Psychological Need Satisfaction Matter to College Students' Sustained Volunteering?* (2021)
- **Link:** [IJERPH, 18(24), 13229](https://mdpi.com/1660-4601/18/24/13229), DOI 10.3390/ijerph182413229.
- **Core idea:** A mixed-methods study of 803 Chinese college student volunteers (survey) plus 33 interviews. Competence and relatedness satisfaction predicted sustained volunteering (length, frequency, intent to continue); autonomy did not reach significance in the regressions. In interviews, competence was the most-mentioned reason for staying, then relatedness, then autonomy. A latent-profile analysis found that the profile high on all three needs (about 24% of the sample) and a profile low on autonomy but high on competence and relatedness (about 7%) reported the most sustained volunteering.
- **Vocabulary worth borrowing:** *sustained volunteering* (operationalized as length, frequency, intent); *need-satisfaction profile* (people cluster, they don't all need the same thing).
- **Touches:** One of the few direct tests of SDT on volunteers rather than students or employees. Describes feeling effective and feeling connected as what kept these volunteers, which bears on facilitators and on the between-cycle middle.
- **Critiques and limits:** Cross-sectional, self-report, one Chinese university, unbalanced gender, modest reliabilities (0.64 on two scales). The authors say the roles of the needs may differ by activity type and culture. The paper's abstract and results sections report slightly different percentages for the interview counts (45.58% vs 47.25% for competence), which is a small internal inconsistency.

## Critiques and replication status (summary)
- **Cultural universality** is contested. SDT's answer is that autonomy means volition, not independence, and the Vansteenkiste et al. chapter engages this directly; it remains the standing objection.
- **The rewards/undermining literature** has been fought over for thirty years (Cameron & Pierce 1994 vs Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999; see theory-progress-and-commitment.md). The undermining effect replicates for expected, tangible, performance-contingent rewards and does not for unexpected rewards or verbal feedback.
- **Method:** Most SDT evidence is correlational and self-reported; the large meta-analysis (Howard et al. 2021) is explicit that it reports associations.
- **Volunteer-specific evidence is thin.** The one direct volunteer study found competence and relatedness mattered more than autonomy, which cuts against SDT's usual emphasis.
- Not verified in this round: Ryan & Deci's 2017 Guilford book (cited widely; not opened), and SDT studies on adult volunteers outside China.

## What this body of work assumes that The Labs cannot
Most evidence comes from schools, universities and workplaces: a calendar, grades or pay, mandatory attendance, paid teachers. The volunteer study comes from a university volunteering program with institutional structure. None of it assumes a drop-in library audience with no obligation to return.

## Questions for the pod
- Where on the internalization continuum does each threshold sit for a typical person (first workshop; signing up for a Cycle; week 6; after the vote; maintaining)? Is the Cycle designed for identified regulation or does it run on introjection (guilt, not letting the team down)?
- The volunteer study found competence and relatedness, not autonomy, predicted staying. If that held at The Labs, what would it mean for a wayfinding product that leads with "choose your own route"?
- Which need does each interface (web, Slack, SMS, agent) actually feed? Can a text message feed relatedness, or only remind?
- What would it take to measure which regulation type facilitators are running on, and whether it shifts over a Cycle?
