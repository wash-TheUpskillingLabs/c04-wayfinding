## Patterns across the field (facts, not advice)

1. **The dominant unit of success is a credential or a wage, and the dominant delivery partner is the community college.** Lumina (75% credentials of value by 2040), Gates (10M credentials), Microsoft (20M credentials), OpenAI (10M certifications), JFF (centers "anchored by community colleges"), America Achieves (11 community colleges in WNC) all count this way. None of the pages opened names a volunteer-run or library-based network as a delivery partner for AI upskilling. The one exception is the Microsoft–PLA partnership (Oct 2026), which treats the library as a channel for Microsoft content and a staff community of practice.

2. **Two federal channels that funded first-threshold work were cut in 2025 while a third, AI-labelled channel opened.** Digital Equity Act grants (NDIA's $25.7M navigator program) were terminated May 9, 2025; IMLS was targeted for closure, lost roughly half its staff, and reinstated grants only after a Nov 21, 2025 injunction. In the same period the AI Action Plan (Jul 23, 2025) assigned DOL "AI literacy" and NSF launched TechAccess ($168M–$224M, 2026) with a required public inventory of AI resources per state. The first-threshold money moved from "digital inclusion" labels to "AI readiness" labels.

3. **Wayfinding is being built at the state level by coordination bodies that are told not to deliver.** NSF hubs must maintain a public resource inventory and act as backbone organizations; JFF proposes state career-navigation platforms through a Digital Transformation Fund; America Achieves works through governors' Good Jobs Funds. In each, the person navigating is imagined as a user of a state inventory, and the local program is an entry in it.

4. **Peer programs document their front door as the bottleneck more than their exit.** Year Up says its assessment process limited who it could serve and is building cheaper entry tools with AIR; Per Scholas publishes an alumni loop (Career Accelerator, 2,650+ in 2025) but no data on who is turned away; Opportunity@Work finds only 15% of STARs call themselves STARs; JFF finds AI-literacy curricula written at grade 11. Selection, self-identification and reading level recur as threshold facts.

5. **Volunteer civic-tech infrastructure at national scale collapsed and was not rebuilt.** Code for America's Brigade program ($3M+/year, 14 staff, about 60 chapters in 2022) ended in 2023 citing low post-pandemic engagement and privacy/security concerns about volunteers working with governments; its successor, ACT, has raised about $6,900 total and provides a Discord and letters of intent. No national player in this map currently funds volunteer onboarding as such.

6. **Skills-first hiring is widely pledged and partially practiced.** Opportunity@Work's coalition opened 273,779 jobs to STARs in 2024 and one in three aware employers removed a degree requirement from at least one posting; Burning Glass Institute's "pronouncements to practice" work and its 30-role shared skills language with Fortune 500 employers show the gap and the attempt to close it with a common vocabulary that a community program does not yet speak.
