# Case: Opportunity@Work, naming "STARs" and the "paper ceiling" to make non-degree workers visible

**Kind:** paradigmatic
**Bears on:** post-cycle, interfaces, activation-mobilization
**Field claims:** 2, 4
**Evidence:** supports 2 (employers screen by degree and miss skills: [Blair, Debroy & Heck](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28991)); complicates 4 (awareness and posting changes rose, but measured mobility for STARs nationally stayed near 40% and slipped to 39% in the 2026 edition: [SOPC 2025](https://www.opportunityatwork.org/impact/sopc2); [SOPC 2026](https://www.opportunityatwork.org/impact/sopc)).
**Logged:** 2026-11-05 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
Opportunity@Work, a US nonprofit, coined "STARs" (Skilled Through Alternative Routes) for workers with skills but no bachelor's degree, and "paper ceiling" for the barrier that keeps them out of higher-wage jobs. It runs a public campaign (Tear the Paper Ceiling) and publishes an annual *State of the Paper Ceiling* report.

## 2. Where it came from
- Opportunity@Work's own reports and press releases ([SOPC 2025 page](https://www.opportunityatwork.org/impact/sopc2); [SOPC 2026 page](https://www.opportunityatwork.org/impact/sopc); [press release](https://www.opportunityatwork.org/about/press-release/new-research-uncovers-untapped-pathways-to-opportunity-for-millions-of-u-s-workers)).
- Congressional testimony by Papia Debroy (research lead, published author) to the House Committee on Education and the Workforce ([testimony PDF](https://edworkforce.house.gov/uploadedfiles/debroy_testimony.pdf)).
- Peer-reviewed/working-paper research by Blair, Debroy and Heck ([NBER w28991](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28991)) and Heck et al. ([NBER w33220](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33220)). Note: these share authors with the organization, so they are not fully independent.

## 3. When
- Blair, Debroy & Heck: NBER July 2021; published *Economics of Education Review* vol. 108, 2025.
- Testimony: 2023-06-22.
- *State of the Paper Ceiling*: 2025 and 2026 editions (pages viewed 2026-11-05; exact release dates not shown).
- NBER w33220: December 2024.

## 4. What it says
- More than 70 million US workers are STARs, over half the workforce ([testimony](https://edworkforce.house.gov/uploadedfiles/debroy_testimony.pdf)).
- Blair, Debroy & Heck proxy skills by the requirements of a worker's current job. STARs and degree holders move similarly between jobs with matching skills, but STARs face more friction moving up into higher-wage, higher-skill jobs, and the gap persists in tight labor markets ([NBER w28991](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28991)).
- The testimony attributes the barrier to degree screens, manager misperceptions and professional networks; degree requirements were more common in state and local government postings (60%) than federal (49%) or private sector (36%) ([testimony](https://edworkforce.house.gov/uploadedfiles/debroy_testimony.pdf)).
- About 7.5 million job opportunities once open to STARs became degree-gated between 2000 and 2020 ([press release](https://www.opportunityatwork.org/about/press-release/new-research-uncovers-untapped-pathways-to-opportunity-for-millions-of-u-s-workers); [testimony](https://edworkforce.house.gov/uploadedfiles/debroy_testimony.pdf)).
- Signs of change reported by the organization: 38% of employers recognize the campaign; 83% of those say they are more likely to hire skilled non-degree workers than 2–3 years earlier; coalition members say they opened over 500,000 jobs to STARs since 2022 by removing requirements ([press release](https://www.opportunityatwork.org/about/press-release/new-research-uncovers-untapped-pathways-to-opportunity-for-millions-of-u-s-workers)).
- Share of employers that changed degree requirements: 27% in the 2025 edition ([SOPC 2025](https://www.opportunityatwork.org/impact/sopc2)); 37% in the 2026 edition ([SOPC 2026](https://www.opportunityatwork.org/impact/sopc)).
- STARs' upward mobility nationally: 40% nationally vs. 59% inside partner networks (edition reporting 2023 figures) ([SOPC 2025](https://www.opportunityatwork.org/impact/sopc2)); 39% nationally vs. 52% inside partner networks (edition reporting 2024 figures) ([SOPC 2026](https://www.opportunityatwork.org/impact/sopc)).
- State-level commitments in 25 states reduced degree requirements in state postings and raised public awareness of the "paper ceiling" term; hires were not measured ([NBER w33220](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33220)).
- Only 15% of STARs identify with the term ([press release](https://www.opportunityatwork.org/about/press-release/new-research-uncovers-untapped-pathways-to-opportunity-for-millions-of-u-s-workers)).

## Failure modes seen
- **Awareness without movement:** Employer awareness and requirement changes rose between editions while the national STAR mobility rate went from 40% to 39% ([SOPC 2025](https://www.opportunityatwork.org/impact/sopc2); [SOPC 2026](https://www.opportunityatwork.org/impact/sopc)).
- **Label the people don't use:** A category built to make workers visible to employers is claimed by 15% of the workers it names ([press release](https://www.opportunityatwork.org/about/press-release/new-research-uncovers-untapped-pathways-to-opportunity-for-millions-of-u-s-workers)).
- **Stuck at the step up:** Lateral moves look similar to degree holders'; the friction appears at the move into higher-skill work ([NBER w28991](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28991)).
- **Network effect, not record effect:** Mobility was much higher inside partner networks than in the labor market at large ([SOPC 2025](https://www.opportunityatwork.org/impact/sopc2)).

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- Mobility was higher inside employer networks that had committed to read non-degree evidence; is "being seen" mainly a property of the record or of the relationship with the reader?
- If most people named STARs don't call themselves that, how do participants in The Labs describe their own standing, and does it match what an outside reader would look for?
- The friction is at the step up, not sideways; which Labs thresholds (workshop to Cycle, Cycle to maintainer or facilitator) look like a step up to an outside reader?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- Most figures come from the advocate organization itself; the methodology for mobility rates and survey sampling was not visible on the pages read. Some page figures are internally inconsistent (e.g., 5.1M vs 5.3M; 70M vs 75M STARs).
- The partner-network gap is not causal: partner employers may select into the network because they already hire STARs.
- Rival explanation for flat national mobility: labor-market cooling in 2024 would reduce mobility for all workers regardless of employer attitudes toward degrees.
- Who could confirm: the full SOPC methodology appendix; independent analysts using CPS or Lightcast data.
