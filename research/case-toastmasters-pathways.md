# Case: Toastmasters International, replacing printed manuals with the Pathways online learning system

**Kind:** failure-mode candidate (platform-mediated learning path imposed across a volunteer-run club network)
**Bears on:** interfaces, cycle, facilitators, pre-cycle
**Logged:** 2026-11-05 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
Toastmasters, a member-run network of public-speaking clubs, replaced its long-standing printed education manuals with "Pathways": 11 paths of five levels each, tracked in an online learning system called Base Camp, rolled out region by region from 2017 with the old program ending 30 June 2020.

## 2. Where it came from
A nonprofit with a world headquarters, regions, districts and local clubs; club officers (notably the Vice President Education) and volunteer "Pathways Guides" support members. This file draws on Toastmasters CEO reports (2018, 2022), its magazine (2018–2026), a member blog (2018) and an online petition.

## 3. When
- Rollout: Region 2 in September 2017, remaining regions by about June 2018 ([CEO report, Mar 2018](https://ccdn.toastmasters.org/medias/files/reports/ceo-reports/2018/toastmasters-international-ceo-report-march-2018--web.pdf)).
- Two-year transition; traditional program ended 30 June 2020, award deadline not extended ([Toastmasters magazine, Jan 2020](https://toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2020/jan/countdown-to-a-new-education-era)).
- Sources dated 2018–2026.

## 4. What it says

**Pre (getting started)**
- Base Camp is a customized instance of a commercial learning management system from Cornerstone OnDemand. A member-blogger who served as a Pathways Guide reported that many members struggled to get started because they could not navigate the platform, and that leftover defaults such as "No Due Date" labels confused people ([Pathways with Duane, 2018](https://tmpathwayswithduane.blogspot.com/2018/11/pathways-on-learning-management-system.html)).
- Headquarters reported that members praised the content but asked for a better online component; it formed a team whose first project was redesigning the online guide ("The Navigator") for mobile ([Toastmasters magazine, Feb 2019](https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2019/feb/sidebars/an-improved-navigation-system)).
- By January 2020, all 11 paths were unlocked for browsing, Base Camp was reachable through member profiles with a new onboarding process, and content was in eight languages ([Toastmasters magazine, Jan 2020](https://toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2020/jan/countdown-to-a-new-education-era)).

**During (rollout and use)**
- At January 2018 leader training, leaders from districts that had launched supported Pathways; leaders from districts that had not yet launched showed what the CEO report called fear, worry and doubt ([CEO report, Mar 2018](https://ccdn.toastmasters.org/medias/files/reports/ceo-reports/2018/toastmasters-international-ceo-report-march-2018--web.pdf)).
- As of 8 March 2018: 2,184 Level 1 completions, falling to 78 at Level 5 ([CEO report, Mar 2018](https://ccdn.toastmasters.org/medias/files/reports/ceo-reports/2018/toastmasters-international-ceo-report-march-2018--web.pdf)). Early in rollout, so not a completion rate.
- A district officer wrote that he initially left the room when Pathways came up, cited website navigation problems, and changed his view after becoming a Pathways Guide ([Toastmasters magazine, Jun 2018](https://toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2018/june2018/my-turn)).
- A 2026 member article described the digital system as directionless at first, stepping away for about two years, and finding Base Camp showing 91% complete after Level 5 because a reflection step was missing; club members' check-ins and a volunteer mentor got her through ([Toastmasters magazine, Apr 2026](https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2026/april/pathways-pitfalls-and-perseverance)).
- Headquarters' 2019–2020 report said over 86% of surveyed members were satisfied with Pathways projects, and members chose a path and finished a first project in under half the previous year's time ([Toastmasters magazine, Nov 2020](https://toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2020/nov/annual-report-on-education-program)).
- Base Camp interface enhancements were still underway in early 2022, with completion expected in 2023 ([CEO report, Mar 2022](https://ccdn.toastmasters.org/medias/files/reports/ceo-reports/2022/ceo-report-march-2022.pdf)).

**Membership around the change**
- Membership was 375,581 at 30 September 2017 (flat year on year); in July–December 2017 payments fell 3.0% in regions 1–10, suspended clubs rose 33.6% and new clubs fell 11.2% ([CEO report, Mar 2018](https://ccdn.toastmasters.org/medias/files/reports/ceo-reports/2018/toastmasters-international-ceo-report-march-2018--web.pdf)).
- By 30 September 2021 membership was 314,761; headquarters said it had fallen about 20% since the pandemic began and attributed this to COVID-19 and the organization's in-person identity ([CEO report, Mar 2022](https://ccdn.toastmasters.org/medias/files/reports/ceo-reports/2022/ceo-report-march-2022.pdf)).
- An online petition (44 signatures when viewed) objected to a 75% rise in the price of an additional path, from $20 to $35, saying members were not consulted and some club leaders were not told ([petition, undated](https://www.petitions.com/keep_toastmasters_accessible_reconsider_the_75_pathways_cost_increase)). Unverified against a Toastmasters source.

**Facilitators**
- Club and district volunteers (Pathways Guides, VP Education, mentors) became the help desk for the platform; the member blog's main advice was to ask one of them for a demonstration ([Pathways with Duane, 2018](https://tmpathwayswithduane.blogspot.com/2018/11/pathways-on-learning-management-system.html)).

## Failure modes seen
- **Platform as the barrier, not the content:** Members called Pathways confusing; a Guide attributed nearly all of it to navigating the learning system ([Pathways with Duane, 2018](https://tmpathwayswithduane.blogspot.com/2018/11/pathways-on-learning-management-system.html)).
- **Off-the-shelf defaults leak through:** Generic LMS labels like due dates contradicted how clubs work ([Pathways with Duane, 2018](https://tmpathwayswithduane.blogspot.com/2018/11/pathways-on-learning-management-system.html)).
- **Pre-launch dread:** Districts not yet live showed fear and doubt while live districts were positive ([CEO report, Mar 2018](https://ccdn.toastmasters.org/medias/files/reports/ceo-reports/2018/toastmasters-international-ceo-report-march-2018--web.pdf)).
- **Invisible last step:** A member finished all five levels and the system still showed 91% because of a reflection task ([Toastmasters magazine, Apr 2026](https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2026/april/pathways-pitfalls-and-perseverance)).
- **Volunteers absorb support load:** Club officers and Guides became the de facto onboarding layer for the software ([Pathways with Duane, 2018](https://tmpathwayswithduane.blogspot.com/2018/11/pathways-on-learning-management-system.html)).
- **Long fix horizon:** Interface improvements were still pending about five years after launch ([CEO report, Mar 2022](https://ccdn.toastmasters.org/medias/files/reports/ceo-reports/2022/ceo-report-march-2022.pdf)).

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- When the route through a program lives in software, who explains the software, and is that counted as part of anyone's role?
- What does a person see when they are "almost done" but a required step is hidden?
- How would we tell whether people leaving during a platform change are leaving because of the platform or because of something else happening at the same time?
- Who is consulted before the cost or shape of a path changes, and how do club-level leaders hear about it?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- No source found gives member attrition attributable to Pathways; Toastmasters' own reports emphasize satisfaction and blame the later decline on the pandemic. Complaints here come from a handful of member accounts and a small petition.
- **Rival explanation:** The 2020–2021 membership fall coincides with the pandemic closing in-person clubs, which headquarters names as the cause; Pathways may have had little net effect.
- Could confirm: Toastmasters Business Research and Analysis team (author of the annual education report); district education directors; club VP Education officers.
