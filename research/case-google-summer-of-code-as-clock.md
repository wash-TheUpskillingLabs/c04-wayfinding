# Case: Google Summer of Code as a global clock that established projects onboard newcomers through

**Kind:** paradigmatic (a shared annual calendar used by hundreds of independent organizations)
**Bears on:** pre-cycle, cycle, facilitators, activation-mobilization
**Product part:** cycle | facilitators | pre-cycle
**Logged:** 2026-11-06 (desk research, sources below). Retention after the program is covered in `cases-online/case-gsoc-outreachy-retention.md`; this file covers calendar mechanics only.

## 1. What it is
Google runs one annual calendar (org applications in winter, contributor applications in spring, community bonding, then a coding period) and pays stipends; several hundred open source organizations each apply to be a slot-holder and supply their own mentors and project ideas. The 2022 edition loosened who could join and how long a project could run.

## 2. Where it came from
Google Open Source's program, running since 2005. Sources: Google's November 2021 announcement of the 2022 changes; the program email to mentors and org admins forwarded to a public list; Google's mentor guide; an org-side explainer (Haskell.org); the CHAOSS community handbook on mentor duties; an I-Programmer summary of Google's 2022 statistics.

## 3. When
- Program: 2005 to present. Changes announced November 10, 2021, for the 2022 edition.
- Sources: November 2021 (announcement and email), February 2022 (Haskell.org), December 2022 (I-Programmer), mentor guide and CHAOSS handbook undated.

## 4. What it says

**The calendar**
- Proposal and selection take about four weeks, then three weeks of community bonding, then a standard twelve-week coding period, extendable to 22 weeks from 2022; a midterm evaluation decides whether the contributor continues and is paid. [Google mentor guide](https://google.github.io/gsocguides/mentor/the-quick-guide.html)
- For 2023, organization applications ran January 23 to February 7 and contributor applications opened March 20. [I-Programmer](https://www.i-programmer.info/news/136-open-source/15968-success-for-google-summer-ofcode.html)

**Organization application and obligations (2022)**
- Orgs must state which kind of contributor they want (students, experienced developers, newcomers) wherever applicants look, state difficulty and expected experience, offer medium and/or large projects, and focus on new contributors; GSoC "is not a way to pay existing contributors." Orgs requesting only large projects may get fewer slots. Org stipend: $500 per contributor. [GSoC 2022 email to mentors, forwarded](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAN40gSsfMYmVHndjHN6CCSb06n=ST1eoX-5CorL8oHZVzQqymw@mail.gmail.com/T/)
- Haskell.org applies each year as an org; project owners submit ideas by pull request to a GitHub repository or talk to organizers first. [Haskell.org, February 2022](https://summer.haskell.org/news/2022-02-01-gsoc-changes-2022.html)

**Mentor commitment**
- Google's own email sets no mentor hour requirement; mentors agree scope with contributors and may re-scope together. [GSoC 2022 email](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAN40gSsfMYmVHndjHN6CCSb06n=ST1eoX-5CorL8oHZVzQqymw@mail.gmail.com/T/)
- CHAOSS asks mentors for "at least 5-6 hours" a week, weekly meetings, and to review proposals; mentees are expected to join the community before acceptance and to stay involved after. [CHAOSS handbook](https://handbook.chaoss.community/community-handbook/mentorships/mentorship-roles-and-responsibilities)
- Google's guide says it is better to give up a slot than pick a weak contributor, and to fail early, since poor midterm performers rarely finish. [Google mentor guide](https://google.github.io/gsocguides/mentor/the-quick-guide.html)

**The 2022 changes**
- Eligibility widened from students to any open source newcomer aged 18+; two project sizes (about 175 and 350 hours); deadline extendable to 22 weeks instead of a fixed June–August; reasons given were that not everyone can give a full summer and pandemic-era feedback asked for flexibility. To date: 18,000+ students from 112 countries, 17,000+ mentors from 746 organizations. [Google Open Source Blog, November 10, 2021](https://opensource.googleblog.com/2021/11/expanding-google-summer-of-code-in-2022.html)
- Result in 2022: 1,054 contributors completed with 198 organizations and 2,000+ mentors from 76 countries; 10.4% of accepted contributors were non-students; 71% kept the 12-week option, 19% ran 13–18 weeks, 9% ran 19–22 weeks; 53% large and 47% medium projects. Self-reported: 98% plan to keep working with their org, 84% would consider mentoring. [I-Programmer, December 2022](https://www.i-programmer.info/news/136-open-source/15968-success-for-google-summer-ofcode.html)

## Failure modes seen
- **Fixed summer excludes non-students.** The 2022 change was justified by people who could not devote a full summer. [Google, 2021](https://opensource.googleblog.com/2021/11/expanding-google-summer-of-code-in-2022.html)
- **Intent outruns behavior.** 98% "plan to continue" in 2022 versus the ~16% measured in earlier cohorts (see retention case). [I-Programmer](https://www.i-programmer.info/news/136-open-source/15968-success-for-google-summer-ofcode.html)
- **Slot pressure on orgs.** Orgs asking only for large projects risk fewer slots; the program polices the use of its clock. [GSoC 2022 email](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAN40gSsfMYmVHndjHN6CCSb06n=ST1eoX-5CorL8oHZVzQqymw@mail.gmail.com/T/)

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- GSoC makes the host org declare, in public, who it wants and how hard the work is before the clock starts. What does a lab declare before a Cycle, and where?
- Google's calendar has a named "community bonding" stretch between acceptance and work. What is the Labs' equivalent between sign-up and week one?
- Mentor hours are set by each org, not by Google. Does The Labs set facilitator hours per kit, or leave them to each lab?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- The official "how it works" page could not be opened (permission not granted); timeline and rules come from Google's blog, a forwarded program email and the mentor guide.
- 2022 outcome percentages are Google's self-reported survey figures as relayed by I-Programmer.
- GSoC assumes what The Labs cannot: stipends (contributor and $500 per org), a single central funder, and established projects with existing code for newcomers to join.
- **Rival explanation:** the flexibility changes may have been less about inclusion than about keeping completion numbers up after pandemic-era drops; the 2022 completion count (1,054) is not compared with prior years in the sources opened here.
- Who could confirm: Google Open Source program office; org admins at long-running participant orgs.
