# Case: Boy Scouts of America / Scouting America, merit badges as a self-chosen modular catalog feeding a rank ladder

**Kind:** paradigmatic (with documented failure modes)
**Bears on:** cycle, post-cycle, facilitators, activation-mobilization
**Product part:** kits | facilitators | post-between
**Logged:** 2026-11-06 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
A century-old catalog of over a hundred merit badges, each a written requirement list that a youth completes with a volunteer "counselor" who is an adult expert in that subject; a subset is mandatory for the Eagle rank, the rest elective. The catalog sits beside a linear rank ladder (Scout to Eagle).

## 2. Where it came from
Boy Scouts of America, renamed Scouting America in 2025. Sources: the BSA's "Reporting Merit Badge Counseling Concerns" form (Guide to Advancement section 11.1.0.0), Scouting magazine's 2019 explainer on group instruction, the BSA-affiliated blog Bryan on Scouting's Eagle-rate analysis (2015), and an Associated Press report on membership (2025).

## 3. When
Merit badges date from the 1910s; the Eagle percentage series runs 1912–2014. Scouting magazine article 25 October 2019. AP membership report 6 February 2025. Concerns form 2019 printing.

## 4. What it says

**How the catalog is routed.** A Scout chooses a badge, gets a unit leader's sign-off (the "blue card"), and works with a registered, council-approved counselor; the counselor must hold current Youth Protection Training. Group instruction is allowed, but "each Scout must actually and personally complete each requirement before the counselor signs off" ([Scouting magazine, 2019](https://scoutingmagazine.org/2019/10/getting-schooled-on-rules-for-merit-badge-classes-fairs-and-universities/)). The Guide to Advancement index places Scout choice of counselor at 7.0.0.3, counselor qualifications at 7.0.1.1–7.0.1.4, and training at 7.0.1.5–7.0.1.6 ([Guide to Advancement section 11 and index](https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/pdf/gta-section-11.pdf)). The number of badges required for Eagle and the size of the Eagle-required list are not in the pages opened here (unverified; the scouting.org Eagle page was blocked).

**Group instruction and "mills."** Scouting magazine says group classes are sometimes preferred but warns organizers to prevent shortcuts that inflate badge counts; smaller, patrol-sized groups are preferred, there is no set class limit, and Scouts may leave with a "partial" to finish one-to-one ([Scouting magazine, 2019](https://scoutingmagazine.org/2019/10/getting-schooled-on-rules-for-merit-badge-classes-fairs-and-universities/)). Reader comments on that article say summer-camp classes often skip individual testing and that multi-badge "merit badge colleges" produce Eagle-required badges too quickly; these are comments, not the magazine's finding ([Scouting magazine, 2019](https://scoutingmagazine.org/2019/10/getting-schooled-on-rules-for-merit-badge-classes-fairs-and-universities/)). The BSA publishes a formal complaint form whose checklist includes: counselor not registered; counselor not approved by the council advancement committee; counselor did not ensure each Scout personally completed requirements; requirements signed off that could not have been completed; Scout signed off for a badge but did not attend the event; an outside business profited from presenting badges ([Form 512-800, Reporting Merit Badge Counseling Concerns](https://www.scouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/33088-11-1-0-0.pdf)). Interpretation: the organization has institutionalised the expectation that the volunteer-expert layer will sometimes fail, and routes complaints to a council committee.

**Attrition at the top of the ladder.** In 2014, 51,820 Scouts earned Eagle out of 861,898 eligible male youth members, 6.01%, slightly below 2013's 6.02%, which the blog calls a record; the lifetime rate since 1912 is 2.01%, and the 2000 figure was 3.44% ([Bryan on Scouting, 30 March 2015](https://onscouting.org/2015/03/30/what-percentage-of-boy-scouts-become-eagle-scouts/)). The figures are credited to a BSA statistician and calculated by the author.

**Membership decline.** As of February 2025 the organization had just over 1 million members, up less than 2% (about 16,000) on the prior year after participation "plummeted during the COVID-19 pandemic"; it filed for bankruptcy in 2020 amid abuse claims, with more than 82,000 claims and a $2.4 billion plan effective 2023; over 196,000 girls took part in the prior year and more than 8,000 girls had earned Eagle ([AP via WSLS, 6 February 2025](https://www.wsls.com/news/national/2025/02/06/boy-scouts-see-a-small-membership-uptick-after-rebrand-to-scouting-america/)). The 2014 eligible-youth figure of 861,898 from the blog versus "just over 1 million" total members in 2025 are not like-for-like (the 2025 figure includes Cub Scouts and adults).

## Failure modes seen
- **Badge mill.** The BSA's own complaint form lists counselors signing off requirements not personally completed and Scouts credited for events they did not attend ([Form 512-800](https://www.scouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/33088-11-1-0-0.pdf)).
- **Group delivery erodes the one-to-one kit.** Scouting magazine had to restate that group classes must still test each Scout individually ([Scouting magazine, 2019](https://scoutingmagazine.org/2019/10/getting-schooled-on-rules-for-merit-badge-classes-fairs-and-universities/)).
- **Few reach the summit.** About 6% of eligible Scouts earned Eagle in 2013–14, the best years on record ([Bryan on Scouting](https://onscouting.org/2015/03/30/what-percentage-of-boy-scouts-become-eagle-scouts/)).
- **Volunteer-expert supply as the choke point.** Scouting magazine lists limited counselor access as one of the reasons group instruction gets used ([Scouting magazine, 2019](https://scoutingmagazine.org/2019/10/getting-schooled-on-rules-for-merit-badge-classes-fairs-and-universities/)).
- **Institutional shock outside the catalog.** Bankruptcy and the pandemic drove membership down regardless of program design ([AP, 2025](https://www.wsls.com/news/national/2025/02/06/boy-scouts-see-a-small-membership-uptick-after-rebrand-to-scouting-america/)).

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- Scouting assumes a youth, a parent, a weekly troop meeting and an age limit of 18 that forces the route to be finished. What plays the role of the clock and the sign-off adult when none of those exist?
- The elective catalog is wide, but the Eagle-required list is the actual route most people follow. Does The Labs want a required spine, and what happens to the kits that are not on it?
- The BSA built a complaint form for its volunteer experts. What is the equivalent accountability for a facilitator who runs a kit badly, and who hears it?
- Six percent reach Eagle in the best year. Is a 6% completion rate for the Cycle a failure or the design?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
The "badge mill" evidence is the organization's own complaint checklist plus reader comments; no published count of upheld complaints was found. The Eagle percentage is a blog calculation from BSA-supplied numbers, not an audited statistic, and the denominator mixes programs. Rival explanation for the ~6% Eagle rate: most youth age out or leave Scouting for ordinary adolescent reasons before the ladder matters, so the rate measures retention of teenagers, not the difficulty of the catalog. Scouting America's national advancement committee could confirm. Blocked: scouting.org Eagle requirements page (permission not granted), so the required-badge count is unverified.
