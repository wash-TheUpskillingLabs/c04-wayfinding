# Record: the problem owner's hypotheses (Nov 6)

**Bears on:** pre-cycle, cycle, post-cycle, facilitators, interfaces
**Logged:** 2026-11-06. Dorst's step 1: the problem owner's account, in their words, as something to test. Not a frame, not a decision.

## 1. What it is
Design positions stated by one of The Labs' initial designers and strategic advisors, in a working session on Nov 5–6, 2026, after reading the archaeology library. They extend the organizers' account in #4.

## 2. Where it came from
One member of the steering committee. One account, from the organizer's side.

## 3. When
Nov 5–6, 2026, during C04's archaeology week.

## 4. What it says
- **The Cycle is currently the point of entry, and it should be something built up to.** There is no pre-cycle experience. There is no well-mapped post-cycle or between-cycle experience.
- **The Cycle should be seen as an incubation sprint and an end-to-end capstone.** It is where new things get made and where someone practices the whole loop once.
- **Sustained projects should have slower-burn programs built around them.** The Labs' job is not to sustain those projects. Its job is to partner with them so they can onboard new contributors and use their real work as learning experiences. Existing examples include ventures and civic tech projects that came out of past Cycles.
- **That implies a member-project mechanic:** a visible set of partner projects, a way to match people to them, and a handoff on each side.
- **The product is turnkey, modular kits on OLOS** for upskilling in a Cycle and out of one, plus the Cycle itself, plus the pre- and post-cycle experience that is not yet outlined. OLOS's own app should be the first and primary head and the source of truth; other channels read from it.
- **The best case for a participant** is becoming a true fan of the practice inside their own workplace and rising there. The wayfinding part of that is the route out into a workplace and back.
- **The widespread complaint** is friction at these joints. This is taken as given inside The Labs; the field is where the understanding needs validating.

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions that would test each hypothesis:*
- When past participants' Cycles ended, did they want to keep going on the project, keep going with the people, or stop? (The alumni survey hints at "the people" but did not ask.)
- Which existing projects from past Cycles would sign up as member projects now, and what would they want from The Labs in return?
- Has any past participant already joined a maintained project informally, and how did they find it?
- Has anyone carried a Labs practice into their workplace and come back? What did the route look like?
- Would a slower program around a project hold the people the sprint cannot (the alumnus who asked for sideline roles)?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
One organizer's view, formed partly by reading this library, so it is not independent of it. Rival explanation: the friction people report at the joints may be capacity (time, jobs, money) wearing the costume of wayfinding; interviews should ask what else was going on in people's lives when they got lost.
