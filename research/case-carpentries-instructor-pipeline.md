# Case: The Carpentries, a volunteer instructor pipeline with a post-training "checkout"

**Kind:** paradigmatic (best-documented volunteer trainer pipeline with published attrition data)
**Bears on:** facilitators, post-cycle, activation-mobilization, interfaces
**Logged:** 2026-11-05 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
The Carpentries (Software Carpentry, Data Carpentry, Library Carpentry) run short coding and data workshops taught by volunteer instructors. People become instructors by attending a two-day instructor training and then completing "checkout," a set of follow-up tasks, before they are badged.

## 2. Where it came from
A nonprofit community with a small core team, volunteer Trainers, lesson Maintainers and Instructors. This file draws on the organization's own blog posts and assessment reports (2013–2023), its Instructor Handbook, and a local survey by the University of Oslo Carpentry community (2019).

## 3. When
- Instructor training cohorts from 2012–2013 onward; three-part checkout in place since 2015 ([Carpentries, 2018](https://carpentries.org/blog/2018/02/checkout-update/)).
- Open application process from July 2016 ([Carpentries, 2018](https://carpentries.org/blog/2018/08/programmatic-update/)).
- Checkout review 2022; new checkout effective 14 August 2023 ([Carpentries, 2023](https://carpentries.org/blog/2023/08/checkout_changes/)).
- Sources dated 2013–2023 plus the current handbook.

## 4. What it says

**Pre (before training)**
- Since July 2016 anyone could apply openly; people sponsored by member organizations come through a separate route the core team does not select ([Carpentries, Aug 2018](https://carpentries.org/blog/2018/08/programmatic-update/)).

**During (training and checkout)**
- Early online cohorts (2012–2013): 171 enrolled across six rounds, 67 completed (39%); round completion ranged 18% to 55% ([Wilson, 2013](https://third-bit.com/2013/12/19/instructor-stats/)).
- Checkout from 2015 had three parts: a lesson contribution on GitHub, an instructor discussion session, and a short teaching demonstration, estimated at 8–10 hours ([Carpentries, Feb 2018](https://carpentries.org/blog/2018/02/checkout-update/)).
- A 2016 survey of about two dozen newly qualified instructors reported a median of roughly 10 hours to complete checkout, with a maximum of 32 (units not stated in the post) ([Carpentries, Jul 2016](https://carpentries.org/blog/2016/07/completion-times/)).
- May 2016 data: overall checkout completion about 55% for sessions at least 90 days old; in-person 56%, online 51%; per-event completion ranged from 22% to 100% ([Carpentries, May 2016](https://carpentries.org/blog/2016/05/instructor-metrics/)).
- August 2018: 56.6% of people who attend instructor training become certified ([Carpentries, Aug 2018](https://carpentries.org/blog/2018/08/programmatic-update/)).
- In 2018, a rule that demos be run by a Trainer from a different place (to avoid conflicts of interest) was dropped because it disadvantaged regions with few Trainers; the vote was 22–1 with one abstention ([Carpentries, Feb 2018](https://carpentries.org/blog/2018/02/checkout-update/)).
- A 2022 review, the first systematic one since 2018, aimed to remove accessibility barriers ([Carpentries, Sep 2022](https://carpentries.org/blog/2022/09/checkout-project)). The resulting 2023 changes: the demo shrank to one episode; a required Welcome Session was added; the GitHub lesson contribution was replaced by a menu of "Get Involved" options (helping at a workshop, attending a community event, contributing to a repository). The post says many trainees found the GitHub step a barrier and the team found no clear evidence it achieved its aims ([Carpentries, Aug 2023](https://carpentries.org/blog/2023/08/checkout_changes/)).
- Current rule: complete checkout within 90 days of training; extensions possible up to a year after the deadline ([Instructor Handbook](https://docs.carpentries.org/handbooks/instructors.html)).

**Post (after badging)**
- Of 673 instructors trained as of May 2016, 222 (33%) had not taught; among those trained over a year earlier, 18.5% had not taught. Median time from training to first workshop was 98 days. 204 had taught before being certified ([Carpentries, May 2016](https://carpentries.org/blog/2016/05/instructor-metrics/)).
- Instructors are expected to answer workshop hosts within two working days, teach at least once a year to vote, and can become "Experienced Instructors" after three workshops. There is no formal offboarding; continued non-participation "may affect" active status ([Instructor Handbook](https://docs.carpentries.org/handbooks/instructors.html)).
- A 2019 Oslo survey (20 responses from a 68-person list) found a few certified instructors who did not know how to set up a workshop website or run a workshop locally, and two who said they lacked local support ([UiO Carpentry, 2019](https://uio-carpentry.github.io/about/reports/report_instructor-status-survey_2019-11-07)).

**Facilitators / roles**
- Checkout steps were owned by different volunteer groups: Maintainers (lesson contributions), a Mentoring Subcommittee (discussions), and Trainers (demos) ([Carpentries, Feb 2018](https://carpentries.org/blog/2018/02/checkout-update/)). Separate handbooks exist for Maintainers, Instructor Trainers and Community Session Hosts ([Instructor Handbook](https://docs.carpentries.org/handbooks/instructors.html)).
- Tracking ran through AMY, the organization's database; after 2023 trainees self-report "Get Involved" activities in AMY rather than being logged automatically ([Carpentries, Aug 2023](https://carpentries.org/blog/2023/08/checkout_changes/)).

## Failure modes seen
- **Half-finish after training:** Roughly 45% of people who attend training never complete checkout ([Carpentries, Aug 2018](https://carpentries.org/blog/2018/08/programmatic-update/)).
- **Gate that tests the wrong skill:** The GitHub contribution step blocked many trainees and showed no clear evidence of meeting its purpose, and was removed in 2023 ([Carpentries, Aug 2023](https://carpentries.org/blog/2023/08/checkout_changes/)).
- **Badged but idle:** A third of trained instructors had not taught in the May 2016 snapshot ([Carpentries, May 2016](https://carpentries.org/blog/2016/05/instructor-metrics/)).
- **Uneven cohort outcomes:** Per-event checkout completion ran from 22% to 100%, which the author read as a sign some cohorts need more follow-up ([Carpentries, May 2016](https://carpentries.org/blog/2016/05/instructor-metrics/)).
- **Geographic bottleneck in gatekeepers:** The demo rule disadvantaged regions with few Trainers ([Carpentries, Feb 2018](https://carpentries.org/blog/2018/02/checkout-update/)).
- **Silent lapse:** No offboarding; inactivity is not formally recorded ([Instructor Handbook](https://docs.carpentries.org/handbooks/instructors.html)).

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- Which steps between "attended training" and "taught for the first time" are where people actually stop, and who can see that?
- When a gate is owned by a different volunteer group than the one running training, who notices when it has become a barrier?
- What counts as "active" for a facilitator or trainer, and is lapsing recorded or just assumed?
- Does a menu of ways to get involved help people find a route, or blur what the next step is?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- Most numbers are internal and from 2013–2018; no published post-2023 completion rate was found to show whether the new checkout changed completion.
- The 2016 instructor-metrics post is a snapshot; recently trained people inflate the "not yet taught" share.
- **Rival explanation:** Low completion may reflect the training's use as general professional development (people attend to learn teaching practice with no plan to teach Carpentries workshops), not checkout friction; member-organization trainees were excluded from the 2018 analysis, which may also shift rates.
- Could confirm: The Carpentries Instructor Training team or Trainers Leadership; AMY data; the full 2018 programmatic report on Zenodo ([record](https://zenodo.org/records/1404428)).
