# Case: Alabama Talent Triad, a state-run skills registry, job-description generator and learner wallet

**Kind:** maximum variation (state government as builder, against nonprofit- and standards-led cases)
**Bears on:** pre-cycle, post-cycle, facilitators, interfaces
**Field claims:** 2, 3, 4
**Evidence:** supports 3; supports 2 in reverse (employers asked for evidence of time and cost savings before using the system); complicates 4 (no public usage numbers found, and employers were reluctant to validate skills for fear of poaching; [C-BEN, 2023](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/CBEN-23-014-Employer-Engagement-USA-v5.pdf))
**Logged:** 2026-11-05 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
The Alabama Talent Triad is a statewide, state-led platform with three parts: a credential registry (with the competencies each credential builds), a skills-based job description generator and employer portal, and a learning and employment record wallet for jobseekers and students, with automated matching. It was built with EBSCO.

## 2. Where it came from
- The Governor's office and Alabama's workforce agencies, with EBSCO and the Competency-Based Education Network (C-BEN); a public-private partnership of 19 state agencies plus employers and providers.
- Sources: C-BEN employer brief (2023, Walmart-funded, hosted by Aspen); Work Shift reporting (2023/2024); Alabama State Department of Education memo (2024); NAWB toolkit spotlight (2026); Alabama Reporter (2026).

## 3. When
- 2018: state "Success Plus" goal of 500,000 more credentialed workers by 2025.
- March 3, 2023: employer convening; April 2023: pilot; December 6, 2023: statewide launch.
- September 2024: schools told to collect parental consent so students can join from eighth grade.
- February 2026: national innovation recognition from Apolitical.

## 4. What it says
- About 40 employers and workforce professionals attended the March 2023 convening; most employers had not heard of the platform. The pilot launched April 2023, with a goal of enrolling at least 10,000 candidates by the end of 2023. ([C-BEN, 2023](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/CBEN-23-014-Employer-Engagement-USA-v5.pdf))
- Employers named barriers: reluctance to act as skills validators because it exposes workforce strategy, fear that documented skills make poaching easier, a steep learning curve for skills-based job descriptions, need for integration with HR systems such as Workday, unclear scope, weak small-business representation, privacy, and a demand for evidence of return on investment. ([C-BEN, 2023](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/CBEN-23-014-Employer-Engagement-USA-v5.pdf))
- Work Shift reported the state's 500,000-worker goal and that officials had spoken with about two dozen states, but gave no usage figures. It noted that LER pilots had not yet shown why people would want a wallet, and that dental offices said certificates were enough for a role while their postings still asked for associate degrees. ([Work Shift (Paul Fain), August 2023, updated May 2024](https://workshift.org/?p=7861))
- The Governor launched the Talent Triad on December 6, 2023; a September 2024 state memo tells schools to collect parental opt-in so students can join from eighth grade. ([Alabama State Department of Education, September 2024](https://alabamaachieves.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/StateSuperIn_Memos_20240910_FY24-3057-Alabama-Talent-Triad-System_v1.0.pdf))
- A 2026 NAWB spotlight names awareness as the main ongoing barrier; says about 3.5 full-time staff built it against a need of 7–8; reports that early committee-written competencies read like job descriptions and had to be rewritten to be demonstrable and testable; and names leadership turnover and cross-state recognition as problems. It reports no usage numbers. ([NAWB, 2026](https://www.nawb.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/NAWB-Skills-Economy-Toolkit-Spotlight-Alabama.pdf))
- In February 2026 Apolitical named the Talent Triad Alabama's entry in its "50 States, 50 Breakthroughs" program; the coverage gives no usage numbers. ([Alabama Reporter, February 2026](https://www.alreporter.com/2026/02/18/alabamas-talent-triad-wins-national-award-for-workforce-innovation/))
- Alabama has published over 1,530 credentials to the national Credential Registry. ([Credential Engine, 2025](https://credentialengine.org/all-resources/2025-counting-credentials/))

## Failure modes seen
- **The validator won't validate:** Employers hesitated to certify workers' skills because a legible worker is easier for competitors to hire away ([C-BEN, 2023](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/CBEN-23-014-Employer-Engagement-USA-v5.pdf)).
- **Nobody knows it exists:** Most employers at the 2023 convening had not heard of it, and in 2026 awareness is still named as the main barrier ([C-BEN, 2023](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/CBEN-23-014-Employer-Engagement-USA-v5.pdf); [NAWB, 2026](https://www.nawb.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/NAWB-Skills-Economy-Toolkit-Spotlight-Alabama.pdf)).
- **The legibility system is itself illegible:** No public source found reports enrollment, matches or hires against the 10,000-candidate or 500,000-worker goals ([Work Shift, 2024](https://workshift.org/?p=7861); [NAWB, 2026](https://www.nawb.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/NAWB-Skills-Economy-Toolkit-Spotlight-Alabama.pdf)).
- **Under-staffed infrastructure:** About 3.5 staff did work that the state estimates needs 7–8 ([NAWB, 2026](https://www.nawb.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/NAWB-Skills-Economy-Toolkit-Spotlight-Alabama.pdf)).
- **Stated skills vs posted requirements:** Employers said a certificate sufficed but kept degree requirements in postings ([Work Shift, 2024](https://workshift.org/?p=7861)).
- **Turnover resets:** New leaders and partners had to be brought up to speed from scratch ([NAWB, 2026](https://www.nawb.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/NAWB-Skills-Economy-Toolkit-Spotlight-Alabama.pdf)).

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- If an employer gains nothing (or loses something) by vouching for someone's skills, who in The Labs' world is in a position to vouch?
- Can people tell whether their record is being read? What would they need to see?
- Alabama wrote competencies by committee and had to rewrite them; who writes the "what you can now do" language for a Cycle, and how is it tested?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- We did not find published counts of users, employers, matches or hires. That may mean the data exists but is not public. The Apolitical case study was behind a login and could not be read.
- Most sources are from partners or favorable outlets (C-BEN, EBSCO, NAWB, award coverage); the only critical voices are in Work Shift.
- **Rival explanation:** Alabama's very low unemployment (2.2% in May 2023, per the C-BEN brief) meant employers were hiring nearly anyone available, so a skills-matching tool had little to add; low uptake would then reflect the labor market, not the design.
- Could confirm: Alabama Governor's Office of Education and Workforce Transformation, Alabama Department of Workforce, EBSCO, C-BEN, and AlabamaWorks career centers.
