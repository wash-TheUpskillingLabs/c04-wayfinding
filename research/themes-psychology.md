# The five needs, and the research behind their names

Companion to the post [Five Human Needs Running Through the Field](/blog/field-themes/) and its data, `content/themes/themes.json`. Dorst's step 5 found five themes in the field. This note records why each one carries the name it does: each is a human need that psychology has studied, defined and measured, so the pod can build on what's known instead of inventing terms.

The needs came first, from the field and the three voices inside The Labs. The research came second, to name them. Reading individual-level psychology into what organizations publish is an inference; the names say what to look for, not what anyone here has told us they feel.

| Need | In a person's words | Construct | What the research found | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mattering | “I want someone to see what I can do, and for it to count.” | Mattering | The sense that others notice you, think you important and rely on you; two halves, feeling valued and adding value. Predicted less depression in four samples of teenagers after controlling for self-esteem. | Rosenberg & McCullough 1981; Elliott, Kao & Grant 2004; Prilleltensky 2020 |
| Coherence | “I need this to make sense: where I am, what’s next, and that I can handle it.” | Sense of coherence | Three parts: comprehensibility, manageability, meaningfulness. Built from interviews with people who came through severe hardship; across hundreds of studies, related to better mental health and quality of life. | Antonovsky 1979, 1987; Eriksson & Lindström 2006, 2007 |
| Not Alone | “I don’t want to face the hard part by myself.” | Social support (buffering); social baseline theory | Support protects most under stress. Holding a familiar person’s hand reduces the brain’s threat response; the brain treats company as its baseline and being alone as extra work. | Cohen & Wills 1985; Coan, Schaefer & Davidson 2006; Coan & Sbarra 2015 |
| Belonging | “I want a place, and people, that are still mine when this ends.” | The need to belong | A fundamental motivation needing frequent, pleasant contact with the same people within a bond that lasts; people form bonds readily and resist ending them. At transitions, a one-hour exercise normalizing belonging worries raised grades for years and later career satisfaction. | Baumeister & Leary 1995; Walton & Cohen 2007, 2011; Brady et al. 2020 |
| Believing I Can | “I want to believe I can become someone new, and that I’m allowed to.” | Self-efficacy; possible selves | Belief in your capability for a specific task, built most by mastery, then by watching similar others, then by credible encouragement. Job-search programs worked only when they built motivation, self-efficacy included, as well as skills. Career changers try on possible selves, often through role models. | Bandura 1977, 1997; Liu, Huang & Wang 2014; Markus & Nurius 1986; Ibarra 1999, 2003 |

## Two frameworks that agree

- **Self-determination theory** (Deci & Ryan 2000; Ryan & Deci 2017): universal needs for autonomy, competence and relatedness. Not Alone and Belonging are relatedness; Believing I Can is competence, with autonomy in “allowed to”; Coherence is what SDT calls structure, the support that makes competence possible.
- **Jahoda's latent functions of work** (Jahoda 1981, 1982; Paul et al. 2023 meta-analysis): besides pay, work gives time structure, social contact, collective purpose, status and activity; people out of work report less of all five, and each predicts mental health. Time structure is Coherence; contact is Not Alone; collective purpose is Mattering and Belonging; status is Mattering. Activity has no line of its own. Hypothesis for Wayfinding, untested: between Cycles, members may lose these the way people between jobs do.

## How the names were chosen

- A name had to be a need a person feels, said in plain words, and a construct with a measured evidence base.
- Where the construct's name is plain English (mattering, coherence, belonging), it is the name. Where it isn't (social support, self-efficacy), the name is the plain-language definition: Not Alone, Believing I Can.
- Earlier names were dropped for being metaphors or institutional: Being Recognized, Knowing Where You Are, Someone Beside You, Lasting Past the End, Becoming Someone New; then Being Seen, Solid Ground, A Place to Come Back To.
