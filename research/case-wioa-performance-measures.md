# Case: Federal workforce system (WIA/WIOA), counting people after they exit

**Kind:** failure (paradigmatic)
**Bears on:** pre-cycle, post-cycle, facilitators
**Field claims:** 2, 3, 4
**Evidence:** supports 2 (federal law requires every state to report what happened to participants after exit, with sanctions attached; [DOL](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/performance/performance-indicators), [GAO-02-275](https://www.gao.gov/products/GAO-02-275)); supports 3 (studies of JTPA and WIA document creaming, timing games and weak links between the measures and real effects; [Barnow & Smith 2004](https://users.ssc.wisc.edu/~jasmith43/PAPERS/Barnow%20and%20Smith%20(2004)%20PFM%20Performance%20Management%20of%20US%20Job%20Training%20Programs.pdf)); complicates 4 (the record is read and acted on, but the reading is about the program's numbers, and those numbers are "weakly, and sometimes perversely" related to long-run impact; [Heckman, Heinrich & Smith 2002](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nbr:nberwo:9002))
**Logged:** 2026-11-05 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
The U.S. public workforce system (Workforce Investment Act of 1998, replaced by the Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act of 2014) judges states and local boards on a short list of outcomes measured after a participant leaves the program. It is the longest-running U.S. attempt to make "what happened to people" legible to a funder at scale.

## 2. Where it came from
The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) Employment and Training Administration defines the indicators. The case draws on DOL's indicator page and its 2020 statistical model documentation; a 2002 Government Accountability Office (GAO) report on WIA measures; a 2004 review of evidence by Burt Barnow and Jeffrey Smith; a 2002 study by James Heckman, Carolyn Heinrich and Jeffrey Smith of the earlier JTPA system; and 2015 comments from the Center for Law and Social Policy (CLASP) on draft WIOA reporting rules.

## 3. When
- Performance standards date back to JTPA (1980s–1998); WIA passed in 1998; WIOA in 2014.
- Sources: GAO 2002; Heckman et al. 2002; Barnow & Smith 2004; CLASP September 2015; DOL statistical model documentation February 2020; DOL indicator page (undated, current).

## 4. What it says
- WIOA's primary indicators are: employment rate in the 2nd and 4th quarter after exit; median earnings in the 2nd quarter after exit; credential attainment during participation or within a year of exit; measurable skill gains during the program year; and effectiveness in serving employers (e.g. retention with the same employer). [DOL, Performance Indicators](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/performance/performance-indicators)
- Under WIA, missing negotiated performance levels could bring financial sanctions; GAO warned that, because of this, states might choose to serve only the job seekers most likely to succeed. GAO also found the data were neither comparable across states nor timely, and no measure covered the one-stop system as a whole even though at least 17 programs delivered services through it. [GAO-02-275](https://www.gao.gov/products/GAO-02-275)
- Barnow & Smith's review reports that under JTPA, sites delayed formal enrollment until a job was secured, or served people without enrolling them; DOL changed rules in 1992 in response. Under WIA, they cite GAO (2002) reporting that local areas registered few participants out of concern for meeting performance levels, and a DOL report finding WIA registrations in one region at about half of JTPA levels. [Barnow & Smith 2004](https://users.ssc.wisc.edu/~jasmith43/PAPERS/Barnow%20and%20Smith%20(2004)%20PFM%20Performance%20Management%20of%20US%20Job%20Training%20Programs.pdf)
- The same review summarizes work by Courty and Marschke showing JTPA sites timed participants' official "termination" (exit) to flatter the measures: employed people were exited at once, non-employed people were held until the end of a follow-up window, and exits were bunched around the program year end. Those authors estimate the manipulation reduced program impacts. [Barnow & Smith 2004](https://users.ssc.wisc.edu/~jasmith43/PAPERS/Barnow%20and%20Smith%20(2004)%20PFM%20Performance%20Management%20of%20US%20Job%20Training%20Programs.pdf)
- Creaming evidence is mixed: a Tennessee study estimated JTPA's placement rate would have been 61.6% instead of 70.7% had eligible people enrolled at random; but a Corpus Christi study found staff selected *against* likely-successful applicants, which the authors attributed to a "social worker mentality." Barnow & Smith also note WIA standards were not adjusted for participant characteristics, which they argue strengthened the incentive to enroll the job-ready. [Barnow & Smith 2004](https://users.ssc.wisc.edu/~jasmith43/PAPERS/Barnow%20and%20Smith%20(2004)%20PFM%20Performance%20Management%20of%20US%20Job%20Training%20Programs.pdf)
- Across seven studies tabulated by Barnow & Smith (1980–2002), short-term outcome measures had little or no consistent relationship to measured long-run program impacts. [Barnow & Smith 2004](https://users.ssc.wisc.edu/~jasmith43/PAPERS/Barnow%20and%20Smith%20(2004)%20PFM%20Performance%20Management%20of%20US%20Job%20Training%20Programs.pdf); [Heckman, Heinrich & Smith 2002](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nbr:nberwo:9002)
- Heckman, Heinrich & Smith found training centers did respond to the standards, but that efficiency gains or losses from cream skimming were small. [NBER w9002](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nbr:nberwo:9002)
- WIOA adds a statistical adjustment model: DOL estimates expected performance for each state, adjusting for participant characteristics (education, prior wages, disability, homelessness, offender status, long-term unemployment and others) and local economic conditions, described as factors outside a state's control. It is used in negotiating targets and assessing results. [DOL, Development of PY 2020–2021 WIOA Models, Feb 2020](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/ETA/Performance/pdfs/Model-Selection-PY-20-21.html)
- In 2015 comments, CLASP argued that restricting the credential measure's denominator to people enrolled in training would discourage programs from enrolling lower-skilled people, that measurable skill gains counted seat time rather than competency, and that loose definitions would let states produce non-comparable data. [CLASP comments, Sept 2015](https://www.clasp.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/CLASP-Comments-on-the-Workforce-Innovation-and-Opportunity-Act-WIOA-Data-Reporting-Templates.pdf)

## Failure modes seen
- **Creaming at the door:** GAO warned that sanctions could lead states to serve only the people most likely to succeed. [GAO-02-275](https://www.gao.gov/products/GAO-02-275)
- **Not registering people:** local WIA areas registered few participants so that people served informally would not count against them. [Barnow & Smith 2004](https://users.ssc.wisc.edu/~jasmith43/PAPERS/Barnow%20and%20Smith%20(2004)%20PFM%20Performance%20Management%20of%20US%20Job%20Training%20Programs.pdf)
- **Gaming the exit date:** JTPA sites chose when to record a participant's exit to maximize measured employment. [Barnow & Smith 2004](https://users.ssc.wisc.edu/~jasmith43/PAPERS/Barnow%20and%20Smith%20(2004)%20PFM%20Performance%20Management%20of%20US%20Job%20Training%20Programs.pdf)
- **Measure-impact gap:** the short-term numbers that funding depended on did not track long-run effects on earnings. [NBER w9002](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nbr:nberwo:9002)
- **Incomparable records:** state data were not comparable or timely, limiting what a reader could conclude. [GAO-02-275](https://www.gao.gov/products/GAO-02-275)

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- When the moment of "exit" is what triggers the count, who decides when someone has exited, and what happens to people who never formally entered?
- What would a reader of The Labs' record (a library, a board) be able to infer about people who arrived but were never registered for anything?
- If a measure rewards those who start closest to the finish line, what does a "threshold" look like to people who start furthest away?
- Is the thing the field reads (employment in quarter 2) the same thing a participant would want to be seen for?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- Most hard evidence on gaming is from JTPA; Barnow & Smith say there was little direct evidence on WIA's effect on impacts. We found no verified post-2014 study of creaming under WIOA's adjusted measures in this pass.
- **Rival explanation:** differences between who enrolls and who is eligible may come from self-selection (people not knowing about the program) rather than staff picking winners; Heckman & Smith (2004), cited in Barnow & Smith, found part of the gap comes from this.
- Could confirm: DOL ETA performance office; state workforce boards' performance staff; GAO's education/workforce team.
