# Case: Recurse Center, a self-directed retreat with a "Never Graduate" alumni community

**Kind:** extreme (no curriculum, no teachers; alumni stay members) plus one documented failure (RC Start)
**Bears on:** pre-cycle, cycle, post-cycle, activation-mobilization, interfaces
**Logged:** 2026-11-05 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
A free, self-directed programming retreat in New York (and online since 2020) where admitted people spend 6 or 12 weeks working on whatever they choose, alongside overlapping batches, then become alumni with continuing access to the space, chat and events.

## 2. Where it came from
Started in 2010 as a recruiting company (Hackruiter), opened as Hacker School in July 2011, renamed Recurse Center (RC) in 2015; funded mainly by recruiting fees from partner companies, self-sufficient on that income from 2014 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recurse_Center)). This file draws on RC's User's Manual and blog posts (2018–2021), a 2015 participant blog by a published researcher, and Wikipedia.

## 3. When
- 2011 opening; 2015 rename; RC Start mentorship program January–September 2016; remote from March 2020; physical space reopened 2023 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recurse_Center); [RC, 2018](https://www.recurse.com/blog/140-why-we-stopped-rc-start); [RC, 2020](https://www.recurse.com/blog/153-what-weve-learned-from-running-rc-remotely-and-an-update-for-future-batches)).
- Sources dated 2015–2021 plus the current manual.

## 4. What it says

**Pre**
- Before a batch, admitted people introduce themselves on Zulip (RC's chat) and complete a directory profile; first-day welcome talks covering the self-directives and social rules are mandatory, and missing them likely means moving to a later batch ([RC Manual](https://www.recurse.com/manual)).
- Returning alumni reapply in writing without an interview; anyone can recommend an applicant ([RC Manual](https://www.recurse.com/manual)).
- RC does not publish admission rates ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recurse_Center)).

**During**
- Three self-directives (work at the edge of your abilities; build your volitional muscles; learn generously) and four social rules (no feigning surprise, no well-actuallys, no back-seat driving, no subtle -isms) ([RC Manual](https://www.recurse.com/manual)).
- Each person picks an accountability method: daily check-ins, Zulip check-in threads, office hours, presentations, blogging ([RC Manual](https://www.recurse.com/manual)).
- Batches of roughly 15–30 overlap, with a new batch every six weeks; there is no curriculum or exam ([Kleppmann, 2015](https://martin.kleppmann.com/2015/10/11/recurse-center-joy-of-learning.html)).
- Faculty enforce the code of conduct, answer questions, watch participation and can ask someone to end a batch early ([RC Manual](https://www.recurse.com/manual)). RC did not publish a code of conduct until June 2017 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recurse_Center)).
- Remote shift: decided 11 March 2020, space closed 13 March, new batch onboarded remotely from 30 March. "Virtual RC" mapped Zoom rooms to physical areas with a map showing who was where; three daily check-in calls were added. The first remote batch had 60 people. Staff said they had found no good replacement for walking up to someone to ask what they were working on ([RC, 2020](https://www.recurse.com/blog/153-what-weve-learned-from-running-rc-remotely-and-an-update-for-future-batches)).

**Post ("Never Graduate")**
- At the end of a batch people become alumni and keep access to the directory, events calendar, Zulip, jobs chat and the Hub; they can host events, present and program on site, and apply for further batches (record: four full batches) ([RC Manual](https://www.recurse.com/manual)).
- From 2020 all alumni could join and host events remotely ([RC, 2020](https://www.recurse.com/blog/153-what-weve-learned-from-running-rc-remotely-and-an-update-for-future-batches)).
- A 2015 resident observed that alumni often remained involved long after their batch ([Kleppmann, 2015](https://martin.kleppmann.com/2015/10/11/recurse-center-joy-of-learning.html)).
- Alumni and community donated over $300,000 during the pandemic; RC counted over 2,000 alumni and seven staff in 2021 ([RC, 2021](https://www.recurse.com/blog/159)).

**RC Start (documented failure)**
- A free one-on-one mentoring program for beginner programmers: 167 people matched with alumni mentors between January and September 2016, 350+ mentor hours, 15 later attended a retreat ([RC, 2018](https://www.recurse.com/blog/140-why-we-stopped-rc-start)).
- Problems named: applicants were more experienced and narrower in their asks than expected, making matching hard; the pool was less diverse than retreat applicants; participants could not join RC's community spaces and built their own Slack; one-mentor pairs broke when the mentor was unavailable or mismatched. RC's leadership called it a strategic mistake because it did not strengthen the community and was not community-driven ([RC, 2018](https://www.recurse.com/blog/140-why-we-stopped-rc-start)).

**Funding shocks**
- When recruiting dried up in 2020, RC cut monthly spending by over 20% and suspended its need-based living grants (up to $7,000) indefinitely ([RC, 2020](https://www.recurse.com/blog/153-what-weve-learned-from-running-rc-remotely-and-an-update-for-future-batches)).

## Failure modes seen
- **Side door without a hallway:** RC Start participants were matched to members but barred from member spaces, and set up a parallel Slack ([RC, 2018](https://www.recurse.com/blog/140-why-we-stopped-rc-start)).
- **Single point of support:** One-to-one mentor pairs left people stranded when the match failed ([RC, 2018](https://www.recurse.com/blog/140-why-we-stopped-rc-start)).
- **Lost serendipity online:** Remote RC replicated rooms and check-ins but not casual walk-up contact ([RC, 2020](https://www.recurse.com/blog/153-what-weve-learned-from-running-rc-remotely-and-an-update-for-future-batches)).
- **Revenue tied to hiring cycles:** The free retreat's funding fell with the hiring market in 2020, ending living grants ([RC, 2020](https://www.recurse.com/blog/153-what-weve-learned-from-running-rc-remotely-and-an-update-for-future-batches)).

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- What does it mean, concretely, for someone's status not to end when their intensive period ends: which doors stay open, and which are only open during it?
- Where are the mandatory moments (e.g., the first-day talks) and what does missing one cost the person?
- When a program serves people who are not yet members, are they inside the shared spaces or alongside them?
- When people work on self-chosen projects, what makes their progress visible to peers without a curriculum?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- RC publishes little outcome data: no admission rate, no alumni-activity rate. Accounts are mostly RC's own or participants' blogs.
- **Rival explanation:** Alumni engagement may owe more to selection (admitting people already motivated and self-directed) and to the recruiting link (alumni have job-market reasons to stay connected) than to the "Never Graduate" design.
- Could confirm: RC faculty; alumni; RC's Zulip activity data (private by RC's own rules).
