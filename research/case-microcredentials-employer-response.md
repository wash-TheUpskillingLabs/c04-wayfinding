# Case: Certificates, microcredentials and online credentials, do employers call back?

**Kind:** maximum variation (null, negative and positive results side by side)
**Bears on:** post-cycle, interfaces
**Field claims:** 4, 3
**Evidence:** mixed; contradicts 4 for added certificates on résumés in a US audit (no callback effect: [Gaulke et al. 2019](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/1992)); supports 4 for low-baseline workers who made credentials visible on LinkedIn (6% more new jobs: [Athey & Palikot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00247)); shows provider reputation can cut the other way ([Deming et al. 2016](https://lkatz.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/value-postsecondary-credentials-labor-market-experimental-study)).
**Logged:** 2026-11-05 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
A set of field and audit experiments testing whether adding a short credential (certificate, online course certificate, for-profit credential) changes how employers respond, plus one large employer survey on microcredentials.

## 2. Where it came from
- Gaulke, Cassidy and Namingit (Kansas State University), correspondence study, *Labour Economics* 2019 ([AEA RCT Registry](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/1992)).
- Deming, Yuchtman, Abulafi, Goldin and Katz, audit study, *American Economic Review* 2016 ([Katz faculty page](https://lkatz.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/value-postsecondary-credentials-labor-market-experimental-study); [NBER WP 20528](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20528.pdf)).
- Athey and Palikot (Stanford), randomized encouragement experiment with an online learning platform, arXiv preprint ([arXiv 2405.00247](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00247)).
- Northeastern University College of Professional Studies, survey of 1,250 US hiring managers ([Northeastern CPS](https://cps.northeastern.edu/news/the-evolution-of-hiring-what-managers-know-about-think-about-and-are-doing-with-microcredentials/)).

## 3. When
- Gaulke et al.: data collection Dec 2016–Jun 2019; published 2019.
- Deming et al.: published 2016 (NBER working paper 2014).
- Athey & Palikot: first posted 2024-04-30, revised 2025-11-05.
- Northeastern survey: published 2024-07-22.

## 4. What it says
- **Null:** Fictitious résumés sent to real postings, one randomly given a post-baccalaureate certificate. No statistically significant callback difference; the 95% confidence interval ran from −1.44 to +0.50 percentage points against an 11.6% baseline callback rate ([AEA Registry, linked paper](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/1992)).
- **Negative by provider:** A business bachelor's from a for-profit online school got 22% fewer callbacks than one from a non-selective public school; a for-profit health certificate got 57% fewer callbacks than a community-college certificate for jobs that did not require a certificate. Where a certificate plus license was required, provider made no difference ([Deming et al. 2016](https://lkatz.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/value-postsecondary-credentials-labor-market-experimental-study)).
- **Positive when made visible, for weaker profiles:** Over 800,000 online-certificate earners without degrees in developing countries were randomized; the treatment made it easier to post the certificate on LinkedIn and sent reminders. Sharing raised new employment 6% (1.0 percentage point), 9% for jobs related to the certificate ([Athey & Palikot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00247)).
- In the same study, gains were 12% for the least employable third and negligible for the top third; in a résumé-scoring exercise, credentials lifted weak résumés 15–20 points on a 100-point scale and strong ones near zero ([Athey & Palikot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00247)).
- **Survey:** Most of 1,250 hiring managers said microcredentials signal discipline or a learning mindset; managers who held a microcredential themselves were about 200% more likely to hire candidates with only microcredentials ([Northeastern CPS](https://cps.northeastern.edu/news/the-evolution-of-hiring-what-managers-know-about-think-about-and-are-doing-with-microcredentials/)). This is stated attitude, not observed behavior.
- **Unverified:** A registry entry for an occupational-certification audit (1,500 postings, China, 2018) claims advanced certifications raised callbacks 42% and intermediate ones showed weak or no gains, with gains concentrated among applicants already strong on education ([AEA Registry 17143](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/17143)). Results are the authors' abstract only; we did not locate a paper.

## Failure modes seen
- **Credential nobody weighs:** An added post-baccalaureate certificate produced no detectable callback change ([Gaulke et al.](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/1992)).
- **Issuer reputation overrides content:** Same credential type, worse callbacks when the issuer was a for-profit provider ([Deming et al. 2016](https://lkatz.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/value-postsecondary-credentials-labor-market-experimental-study)).
- **Earned but not shown:** The positive effect came from a nudge to display a credential the learner already held, implying many earned credentials sit unseen ([Athey & Palikot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00247)).
- **Say–do gap:** Survey enthusiasm is self-report; the audit evidence above is weaker or null ([Northeastern CPS](https://cps.northeastern.edu/news/the-evolution-of-hiring-what-managers-know-about-think-about-and-are-doing-with-microcredentials/)).

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- The largest documented effect came from making an existing record visible where employers already look, for people with thin résumés; what does that imply about where a record lives versus what it says?
- If the issuer's reputation shapes how a credential is read, who is the issuer of a record that comes out of a library-based volunteer network, in an employer's eyes?
- Where license or certificate is formally required, provider stopped mattering; are there any outside parties for whom a Labs record would be formally required rather than optional?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- No rigorous US audit of open digital badges (e.g., Open Badges/Credly-style) specifically was found in this pass; the evidence here is certificates and online-course credentials.
- Athey & Palikot is a preprint, developing-country sample, one platform; Gaulke et al. tests post-bac certificates on degree holders, not workers without degrees.
- Rival explanation for the LinkedIn effect: the nudge may have also prompted more active job search, so visibility to employers may not be the only channel.
- Who could confirm: the authors; badge platforms' own data on employer verification clicks (not located).
