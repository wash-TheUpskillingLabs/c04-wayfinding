# Parked: an example frame (ski trail ratings)

*Parked Oct 28 by the steering committee. **This is an example of what a frame looks like, not the frame this pod should use.** Frames come from the pod's own themes on Nov 7. Test this against the evidence, borrow from it, or throw it out.*

## The hunch it grew from

Wayfinding may be about the **liminal space between modules**: the thresholds people cross between a workshop, a project night, a Cycle, a pod, a team, the end of a Cycle and what comes after. (Scaffolding would be about the modules themselves.) To choose a route, people need to see where they've been, where they are and where each path leads. That makes **legibility** the point, with reporting as the map.

## The frame

*If we treat this problem as if it were a problem of marking ski trails, then…*

- **Signs sit at the junctions.** You read a trail rating at the top of a run, which is exactly the threshold between modules.
- **Ratings are relative to the mountain.** A blue at one resort is a black at another. The rating says how a run compares to the others here, not against some absolute standard. That's one way adaptive and legible could coexist.
- **You choose by skill and by mood.** The same skier takes a green to warm up and a black after lunch. Routes aren't a ladder.
- **The trail map is the reporting.** It shows the whole mountain at once, so you can plan a route, not just the next run.
- **Beyond double black is backcountry.** Unmarked and unpatrolled, and you go with a guide and gear.

A rough mapping, to argue with:

| Rating | At The Labs |
|---|---|
| Green | A workshop |
| Blue | A project night |
| Black | A Build Cycle |
| Double black | Builder in residence, pod maintainer |
| Backcountry | Off the map: spinning out a project, maintaining OLOS, starting a lab |

## How it would scale to the network

- **Each region is a mountain.** The rating system is shared and each mountain rates its own runs. The national body holds the vocabulary; each region applies it.
- **Totals are the stats board at the base lodge.** Runs by rating, which are open this week, how many people took each, where people turned back. Counts by branch and region, published only above a privacy floor, rolling up across regions.
- **Case studies are run reports.** One person's route across the mountain: where they started, which thresholds they crossed, where it got steep, what got them through. No names. Collected across regions, they show which thresholds lose people everywhere and which only lose them in one place.
- **Ratings come from traffic.** A run isn't black because someone declared it. It's black because the totals and run reports show that's where people need more skill or a guide. Ratings get revised each season from what people actually did.

## What would test it

- Ski ratings work because the mountain mostly holds still. The Labs changes every cohort. Does a relative, revisable rating survive that?
- Do people at The Labs actually experience the thresholds as the hard part? The interviews will say.
- The trail-rating system itself is worth logging as a `Case:`: a shared standard, applied locally, relative by design.
