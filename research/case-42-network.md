# Case: 42 network, a teacherless peer-learning school with a selection bootcamp and deadline-based expulsion

**Kind:** extreme (no teachers, peer evaluation, hard time limits)
**Bears on:** pre-cycle, cycle, post-cycle
**Logged:** 2026-11-05 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
42 is a tuition-free coding school founded in Paris, now a network of campuses, with no teachers or lectures: students work through gamified projects, are graded by peers and an automated checker, and are admitted only after a four-week trial called the "Piscine."

## 2. Where it came from
Founded in Paris and expanded internationally (founding year not verified in an opened source). This file draws on an AFP report (2019), a peer-reviewed ethnographic study at the Florence campus (2024), and a campus FAQ (42 Heilbronn). Source coverage is thinner than for other cases; marked "optional" in the stream.

## 3. When
Sources dated 2019–2026 (2019 Paris figures; 2024 network figures).

## 4. What it says

**Pre (selection)**
- Paris received about 40,000 applications a year for about 1,000 places; about 3,000 reached the Piscine ([AFP via The Local, 2019](https://www.thelocal.fr/20190819/students-battle-for-a-place-at-frances-free-computer-coding-college)).
- The Piscine lasts four weeks of 10–16 hour days of projects and exams; some candidates sleep on site ([AFP via The Local, 2019](https://www.thelocal.fr/20190819/students-battle-for-a-place-at-frances-free-computer-coding-college)).
- A current campus lists four steps: register, solve two online logic games (2–3 hours), attend an online introduction (1 hour), then the Piscine; the only requirement is being 18 or older ([42 Heilbronn FAQ](https://www.42heilbronn.de/en/faq/)).
- Students often needed time to adapt to inductive, no-instruction learning, especially during the Piscine ([Pollini et al., 2024](https://robertoverdecchia.github.io/papers/LEARNER_2024.pdf)).

**During**
- The network reported 54 campuses in 31 countries and over 21,000 students in 2024; the Florence campus had about 10 staff and 217 active students ([Pollini et al., 2024](https://robertoverdecchia.github.io/papers/LEARNER_2024.pdf)).
- Peer reviews are paid for with "evaluation points"; researchers observed rigor in about 10 reviews but also leniency near deadlines, offset by written guidelines, staff checks and an automated grader ([Pollini et al., 2024](https://robertoverdecchia.github.io/papers/LEARNER_2024.pdf)).
- When projects got hard, students sought peers unprompted. The campus acted as a social hub, but recreation sometimes displaced study and slowed progress ([Pollini et al., 2024](https://robertoverdecchia.github.io/papers/LEARNER_2024.pdf)).
- Students must complete projects to push back a deadline; reaching it means expulsion and two years' loss of access ([Pollini et al., 2024](https://robertoverdecchia.github.io/papers/LEARNER_2024.pdf)).
- The Paris course had 21 levels and took about three years on average ([AFP via The Local, 2019](https://www.thelocal.fr/20190819/students-battle-for-a-place-at-frances-free-computer-coding-college)).

**Post**
- Many students were hired before finishing; the school reported 100% employment of graduates. No dropout or graduation rates were given ([AFP via The Local, 2019](https://www.thelocal.fr/20190819/students-battle-for-a-place-at-frances-free-computer-coding-college)).
- Partner companies offer internships, direct hiring and some scholarships ([42 Heilbronn FAQ](https://www.42heilbronn.de/en/faq/)).

**Facilitators**
- No teachers; a small staff, peers as evaluators and an automated checker carry the teaching and assessment load ([Pollini et al., 2024](https://robertoverdecchia.github.io/papers/LEARNER_2024.pdf)).
- The Paris campus faced a 2017 press report of harassment and misogyny (several students were punished) and a 2018 privacy-regulator finding of excessive video surveillance ([AFP via The Local, 2019](https://www.thelocal.fr/20190819/students-battle-for-a-place-at-frances-free-computer-coding-college)).

## Failure modes seen
- **Selection as the onboarding:** Only a small share of applicants reach the four-week trial, which is itself the main orientation ([AFP via The Local, 2019](https://www.thelocal.fr/20190819/students-battle-for-a-place-at-frances-free-computer-coding-college)).
- **Peer grading softens under time pressure:** Reviewers were lenient near deadlines ([Pollini et al., 2024](https://robertoverdecchia.github.io/papers/LEARNER_2024.pdf)).
- **Social hub crowds out work:** Recreation sometimes slowed progress ([Pollini et al., 2024](https://robertoverdecchia.github.io/papers/LEARNER_2024.pdf)).
- **Hard clock exit:** Missing the rolling deadline means expulsion and a two-year lockout ([Pollini et al., 2024](https://robertoverdecchia.github.io/papers/LEARNER_2024.pdf)).
- **Culture without staff oversight:** Harassment reports at Paris in 2017 ([AFP via The Local, 2019](https://www.thelocal.fr/20190819/students-battle-for-a-place-at-frances-free-computer-coding-college)).
- **Exit to work before finishing:** Many leave mid-course for jobs; counted as success or attrition depending on the measure ([AFP via The Local, 2019](https://www.thelocal.fr/20190819/students-battle-for-a-place-at-frances-free-computer-coding-college)).

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- What does a person learn about whether a demanding, self-directed route fits them before they commit to it?
- When peers are the evaluators, what keeps standards steady when everyone is short on time?
- Is leaving early for a job, a project or a life change recorded as an exit, an outcome, or nothing?
- What happens when the community space is mostly social: is that the activation, or a drift away from the work?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- No published dropout, completion or graduation rates were found. The ethnography is preliminary, one campus, two researcher-participants. The AFP piece relies on the school's own employment claim.
- **Rival explanation:** Outcomes may reflect extreme selection (40,000 to 1,000) more than the peer-learning model itself.
- Could confirm: 42 network or campus staff; French Ministry or regional funders; the Florence research team's later publications.
