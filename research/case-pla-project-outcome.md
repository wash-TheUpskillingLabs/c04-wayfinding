# Case: Public Library Association's Project Outcome, a standard outcome survey for library programs

**Kind:** paradigmatic (and an ending: platform sunset in 2025)
**Bears on:** cycle, post-cycle, facilitators, interfaces
**Field claims:** 2, 3, 4
**Evidence:** complicates 2 (libraries most often used results to validate their own programs, not to report outward; [PLA mid-year memo 2016](https://projectoutcome.org/ckeditor_assets/attachments/360/mid-year_results_summary_data_collected_dec_2015.pdf)); supports 3 (positive-skewed answers, low follow-up response, and a hosted platform retired for cost after ten years; [Davis & Plagman 2016](https://www.libraryassessment.org/wp-content/uploads/bm~doc/94-davis-2016.pdf), [VPL 2018](https://www.vpl.ca/sites/default/files/ProjectOutcomeEvaluationUpdate.pdf), [ALA 2025](https://www.ala.org/news/2025/08/pla-celebrates-project-outcome-success-transitions-resources-free-ongoing-use)); partly supports 4 at institutional level (IMLS aligned state grant reporting with Project Outcome questions; [IMLS 2017](https://www.imls.gov/sites/default/files/outputsoutcomesbuildingcapacity.pdf)), but no documented case was found of a funder acting on an individual library's results
**Logged:** 2026-11-05 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
A free toolkit of short, standardized patron surveys (four outcome questions each) that public libraries hand out at the end of a program, plus a follow-up survey weeks later, with a shared online portal for benchmarking. It is measured at the level of the program, not the person over time.

## 2. Where it came from
Built by the Public Library Association (PLA, a division of the American Library Association) with its Performance Measurement Task Force, funded initially by the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation. Sources: PLA's first-year report and mid-year evaluation memo; a 2016 Library Assessment Conference paper by a PLA staff author and a library administrator; a Vancouver Public Library board report (2018); an Oregon State Library explanation of survey design (2015); ALA's 2025 sunset announcement; ICPSR dataset record (2024).

## 3. When
- Launched 2015; online platform sunset announced August 19, 2025.
- Sources dated December 2015 to August 2025.

## 4. What it says
- Surveys cover seven (later nine) service areas, e.g. job skills, digital learning, economic development, early childhood literacy, education/lifelong learning, civic engagement, summer reading. Immediate surveys ask patrons to agree or disagree with changes in knowledge, confidence, application and awareness. [Davis & Plagman 2016](https://www.libraryassessment.org/wp-content/uploads/bm~doc/94-davis-2016.pdf); [ALA 2025](https://www.ala.org/news/2025/08/pla-celebrates-project-outcome-success-transitions-resources-free-ongoing-use)
- The surveys were kept to four questions on purpose so they would not overwhelm staff or patrons, trading depth for comparability across service areas. [Oregon State Library, Dec 2015](https://omls.oregon.gov/pipermail/pl-directors/2015-December/000507.html)
- First year: more than 225 library systems implemented surveys; over 17,000 responses across 774 programs; more than 1,000 libraries registered. [Project Outcome first-year report](https://www.projectoutcome.org/annual-report)
- Over about ten years: more than 2,600 libraries, more than 38,000 surveys, almost 500,000 patron responses. In August 2025 PLA said it was sunsetting the online platform "to contain costs while sustaining free access," moving templates to local use in Google and Microsoft formats. [ALA press release, Aug 19, 2025](https://www.ala.org/news/2025/08/pla-celebrates-project-outcome-success-transitions-resources-free-ongoing-use)
- Early analysis found about 89–90% of respondents agreed or strongly agreed (a ceiling effect); response rates started low; a few records showed more surveys than reported attendance. Follow-up surveys only launched in June 2016. [Davis & Plagman 2016](https://www.libraryassessment.org/wp-content/uploads/bm~doc/94-davis-2016.pdf)
- In a mid-year survey of 213 registered staff (Dec 2015), the most common benefit reported was validating program quality (48% of those who reviewed results); the most common challenge was getting patrons to respond (38%). Smaller libraries were less likely to have used the web resources (64% vs 81%). [PLA mid-year memo, Feb 2016](https://projectoutcome.org/ckeditor_assets/attachments/360/mid-year_results_summary_data_collected_dec_2015.pdf)
- Vancouver Public Library ran immediate and three-month follow-up surveys on a babytime program: 53 of 75 parents (75%) answered the first; 13 (17%) answered the follow-up. The library noted possible positive bias, extra staff time for collecting contact details and consent, and English-only forms. [VPL board report, Sept 2018](https://www.vpl.ca/sites/default/files/ProjectOutcomeEvaluationUpdate.pdf)
- Project Outcome data (2015–2023) were later linked to IMLS Public Libraries Survey records in a research dataset, described as a convenience sample. [ICPSR 38653, 2024](https://www.icpsr.umich.edu/web/ICPSR/studies/38653)

## Failure modes seen
- **Everyone agrees:** about nine in ten respondents chose agree/strongly agree, limiting what the scores could distinguish. [Davis & Plagman 2016](https://www.libraryassessment.org/wp-content/uploads/bm~doc/94-davis-2016.pdf)
- **Follow-up falls off a cliff:** the three-month survey drew 17% versus 75% at the session. [VPL 2018](https://www.vpl.ca/sites/default/files/ProjectOutcomeEvaluationUpdate.pdf)
- **Consent and contact as a cost:** following people over time required collecting personal details, which added staff work. [VPL 2018](https://www.vpl.ca/sites/default/files/ProjectOutcomeEvaluationUpdate.pdf)
- **Platform outlives its funding:** the hosted portal was retired after a decade to contain costs. [ALA 2025](https://www.ala.org/news/2025/08/pla-celebrates-project-outcome-success-transitions-resources-free-ongoing-use)
- **Small libraries lag:** smaller libraries used the resources less. [PLA mid-year memo 2016](https://projectoutcome.org/ckeditor_assets/attachments/360/mid-year_results_summary_data_collected_dec_2015.pdf)

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- Project Outcome describes programs, not people. What does a participant see of themselves in such a record?
- The follow-up survey is where "what happened next" lives, and it is where response collapsed. Which channel and which moment would a person actually answer from?
- Who was the main reader of these results: the library itself, its funders, or IMLS?
- What happens to a community's record when the platform that holds it is retired?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- We could not find published data on how many libraries shared results with funders, or on any funding decision traced to Project Outcome results. The Davis & Plagman paper says libraries reported using results in grant applications, but gives no count.
- First-year figures come from PLA's own reports.
- **Rival explanation:** the platform sunset may reflect the end of the original foundation grant and PLA's budget, not any judgment that the surveys failed; the announcement frames it as cost containment.
- Could confirm: PLA staff; state library data coordinators; IMLS Office of Research and Evaluation.
