# Player: Greater Washington Partnership — Capital CoLAB (network)

**Circle:** field
**Scope:** regional
**Role in the problem:** standard-setter | employer | partner project
**Logged:** 2026-11-06

## Who they are, and where people meet them
A CEO-led civic alliance for the Baltimore–Washington–Richmond corridor. Its Capital CoLAB, launched June 2018, links universities and employers (19 named at launch, including American, Georgetown, George Mason, Howard, Johns Hopkins, UMD, USM, plus Capital One, EY, JPMorgan Chase, Northrop Grumman, MedStar) ([UMD Right Now, 22 Jun 2018](https://umdrightnow.umd.edu/umd-joins-greater-washington-partnerships-capital-colab)). A Labs participant would most likely meet it indirectly, as a university student earning a CoLAB credential or as an employee of a member firm.

## What they bring
- **Social / political:** a standing table of university presidents and large employers across three states ([UMD Right Now](https://umdrightnow.umd.edu/umd-joins-greater-washington-partnerships-capital-colab)).
- **Cultural (standards):** "regionwide credentials" in digital technology, co-designed by businesses and universities and recognized by member employers when hiring, with student pathways built with the Business-Higher Education Forum ([UMD Right Now](https://umdrightnow.umd.edu/umd-joins-greater-washington-partnerships-capital-colab)).
- **Financial:** a 2021 Digital Tech Credential scholarship for minorities and women ([GWP release, Jul 2021](https://greaterwashingtonpartnership.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/Greater-Washington-Partnership-Capital-CoLAB-Announces-New-Digital-Tech-Credential-Scholarship-for-Minorities-and-Women-.pdf), search result; amount **unverified**).

## What they want
The 2018 release says the CoLAB seeks to "position the region as a leading global hub for innovation," through workforce development, technology leadership and regional profile ([UMD Right Now](https://umdrightnow.umd.edu/umd-joins-greater-washington-partnerships-capital-colab)).

## What they've tried about this problem, and how it went
- **Employer-recognized credential layered on degree programs** (2018–). Number of students credentialed: not on pages fetched — **unverified**.
- Entry point is enrolled undergraduates; no published route for mid-career or non-degree adults found in this pass.

## How they see the problem
Talent pipeline: raise the number and quality of digital-tech graduates, with employers signalling demand through a shared credential (2018 release). Inference: a supply-side, credential frame aimed at students, not at displaced mid-career workers.

## Open questions for the pod
- Has CoLAB considered recognizing non-university evidence of skill (portfolios, shipped projects)?
- How many credentials have been issued, and did member employers actually hire on them?
