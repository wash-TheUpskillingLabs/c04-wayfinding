# Player: Jobs for the Future, JFF (institution)

**Circle:** field
**Scope:** national
**Role in the problem:** policy, standard-setter, funder (via JFFLabs), partner project
**Logged:** 2026-11-06

## Who they are, and where people meet them
A 40-year-old national intermediary with a 2033 "North Star" of 75 million people facing barriers in quality jobs; it runs a Center for Artificial Intelligence & the Future of Work and JFFLabs ([policy priorities post, Oct 1 2025](https://www.jff.org/blog/a-future-that-works-jffs-policy-priorities-for-an-ai-ready-workforce/); [JFFLabs AI page](https://jff.org/work/jff-labs/jfflabs-incubation/jfflabs-artificial-intelligence/)). People meet JFF through its Horizons conference, its AI-Ready Workforce Framework (presented to state community college systems, e.g. [NC Community Colleges event](https://www.nccommunitycolleges.edu/events/jffs-ai-ready-workforce-framework/)), and its employer partnerships (e.g. University of Phoenix, June 2025).

## What they bring
- Political/cultural: a detailed federal AI-workforce agenda. Budget not verified in sources opened.
- Human: research claims it circulates: workplace AI use rose from 8% to 35% between 2023 and 2024; more than half of workers say they are unprepared to use AI; many leading AI-literacy curricula are written at a grade-11 reading level ([Oct 2025 post](https://www.jff.org/blog/a-future-that-works-jffs-policy-priorities-for-an-ai-ready-workforce/)).

## What they want
Five federal asks (Oct 2025): AI Workforce Centers of Excellence under WIOA, "anchored by community colleges or nonprofit consortia"; a Digital Transformation Fund for state career-navigation platforms and labor-market data; employer training tax credits (Investing in American Workers Act); codify the Youth Apprenticeship Readiness Grant; expand RESEA with digital and AI literacy. Roadmap: "AI for Economic Opportunity and Advancement: A Call to Action" ([same post](https://www.jff.org/blog/a-future-that-works-jffs-policy-priorities-for-an-ai-ready-workforce/)).

## What they've tried about this problem, and how it went
- Launched the Center for AI & the Future of Work (press release on jff.org, [link](https://www.jff.org/newsroom/press-releases/jobs-future-launches-new-center-artificial-intelligence-future-work/)); date not verified from the page.
- The reading-level finding on AI curricula is the one published evidence item that bears on on-ramps: it says existing AI-literacy material is too hard for many of the people it is meant for ([Oct 2025](https://www.jff.org/blog/a-future-that-works-jffs-policy-priorities-for-an-ai-ready-workforce/)).
- No outcomes published for the proposed centers; they are proposals.

## How they see the problem
Frame: AI readiness is a systems problem to be fixed by federal structure (regional centers, data platforms, tax credits) and by community colleges. Inference: "career navigation platforms" are imagined as state-run systems, not community-run ones; libraries and volunteers are absent from the pages opened.

## Open questions for the pod
- Would JFF's proposed regional AI Workforce Centers treat a library-based network as a "nonprofit consortium" anchor or as a referral source?
- What reading level does The Labs' own material sit at, against JFF's grade-11 finding?
