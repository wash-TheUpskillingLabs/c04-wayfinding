**Circle:** field

*Brief; see the existing case file `field-employers/case-opportunity-at-work-stars.md` for the STARs evidence.*

## Who they are, and where people meet them
A DC-based nonprofit that coined "STARs" (workers Skilled Through Alternative Routes) and runs the Tear the Paper Ceiling campaign with the Ad Council ([Opportunity@Work](https://opportunityatwork.org/)). People meet it through employer pledges, state executive orders dropping degree requirements, and its annual "State of the Paper Ceiling" report and event.

## What they bring
- Social/political: a 77-member coalition; $100M+ in donated advertising; 37% employer awareness of the campaign ([Three Years In, early 2025](https://blog.opportunityatwork.org/three-years-in-tracking-the-state-of-the-paper-ceiling-f24450273266)).
- Nearly 600,000 public-sector jobs committed to be open to STARs by executive order or pending legislation as of Q1 2025 ([same](https://blog.opportunityatwork.org/three-years-in-tracking-the-state-of-the-paper-ceiling-f24450273266)).

## What they want
A labor market where "all talent can shine" (homepage tagline) and a roadmap to get more STARs into higher-wage jobs by 2030 ([same](https://blog.opportunityatwork.org/three-years-in-tracking-the-state-of-the-paper-ceiling-f24450273266)).

## What they've tried about this problem, and how it went
- Coalition employers opened 273,779 jobs to STARs in 2024; 63% of postings for historically degree-requiring roles in its network are open to STARs vs 57% market-wide; median wage gain for upwardly mobile STARs in the network was $13,200 in 2023 ([same](https://blog.opportunityatwork.org/three-years-in-tracking-the-state-of-the-paper-ceiling-f24450273266)).
- Only 15% of STARs call themselves a STAR, which bears on identity and self-recognition at the threshold ([same](https://blog.opportunityatwork.org/three-years-in-tracking-the-state-of-the-paper-ceiling-f24450273266)).
- Among aware employers, one in three has removed a degree requirement from at least one job description; the gap between posting changes and actual hiring is documented separately by Burning Glass Institute (see that file).

## How they see the problem
Frame: the barrier is the degree screen on the employer side, not the worker's skill. Inference: the post-program route it offers is a changed job posting, not a changed worker record.
