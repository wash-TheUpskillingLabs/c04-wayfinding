# Player: NSF TechAccess: AI-Ready America (program)

**Circle:** field
**Scope:** national
**Role in the problem:** funder, policy
**Logged:** 2026-11-06

## Who they are, and where people meet them
A multi-agency program (NSF lead; USDA NIFA, DOL ETA, SBA) that funds up to 56 State/Territory Coordination Hubs, one National Coordination Lead and a series of Catalyst Awards; solicitation NSF 26-508 ([NSF solicitation](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/techaccess-ai-ready-america/nsf26-508/solicitation)). A Labs participant or organizer would meet it through whichever institution wins the DC, Maryland or Virginia hub, since hubs are told to act as backbone organizations that connect existing programs rather than deliver training.

## What they bring
- Financial: anticipated $168M–$224M; each hub gets $1M per year for three years, possibly four. Three rounds: LOIs due June 16, 2026, Dec 15, 2026, June 1, 2027; full proposals July 16, 2026, Jan 15, 2027, July 1, 2027 ([solicitation](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/techaccess-ai-ready-america/nsf26-508/solicitation)). Secondary coverage dates the launch to around April 2026 ([Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/hrtech/news/us-agencies-commit-224-m-to-ai-ready-america-workforce-initiative-716849f2-e15c-44ff-99b7-521fcfaeb15e)).
- Built/social: hubs must maintain a public inventory of state AI resources (training, infrastructure, support) and use existing partners such as Cooperative Extension, American Job Centers and Small Business Development Centers ([solicitation](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/techaccess-ai-ready-america/nsf26-508/solicitation)).

## What they want
To "coordinate AI readiness and accelerate deployment" beyond K-16 into businesses, public-serving organizations and individuals; hubs write a statewide AI-readiness plan, align credentials, expand experiential learning including apprenticeships, and report progress to the national lead ([solicitation](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/techaccess-ai-ready-america/nsf26-508/solicitation)).

## What they've tried about this problem, and how it went
New; no awards found yet. Notable design choices: hubs "should not deliver most training directly"; a public resource-navigation inventory is a required deliverable; cost sharing is prohibited; one proposal per institution ([solicitation](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/techaccess-ai-ready-america/nsf26-508/solicitation)).

## How they see the problem
Frame: the problem is coordination and navigation across existing providers, state by state. The required inventory is a wayfinding artifact at state scale. Inference: libraries are not named as partners in the solicitation summary; eligibility runs through NSF PAPPG categories, which typically include nonprofits but not unaffiliated individuals.

## Open questions for the pod
- Who is bidding for the DC, Maryland and Virginia hubs, and would a volunteer network be listed in their resource inventory?
- How will a hub decide what counts as an "AI resource" worth listing?
