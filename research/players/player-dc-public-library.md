# Player: DC Public Library (institution)

**Circle:** field
**Scope:** regional
**Role in the problem:** host | referrer | partner project
**Logged:** 2026-11-06

## Who they are, and where people meet them
The District's public library system; its central branch is the Martin Luther King Jr. Memorial Library at 901 G St NW, which houses "The Labs at DC Public Library" (Fabrication Lab, Studio Lab, Memory Lab) on the A-Level ([The Labs at DCPL](https://dclibrary.org/labsatdcpl)). A person at The Labs meets DCPL as the room they are standing in: the AI Upskilling Cohort pilot met weekly, in person, at MLK Library from August to October 2025, with the library providing meeting space, staff facilitation and job-seeker support ([DCPL news, 14 Jul 2025](https://www.dclibrary.org/news/future-proof-your-career-dc-public-library-launches-ai-upskilling-cohort); [DCPL event listing, 2 Sep 2025](https://dclibrary.libnet.info/event/14522100)).

## What they bring
- **Built:** Fab Lab (900 sq ft) and Studio Lab (850 sq ft) opened at MLK in 2015 at a project cost of $490,000, with a $20,000 Google gift to the DC Public Library Foundation ([Mayor's office, 29 May 2015](https://mayor.dc.gov/release/mayor-bowser-launches-new-innovation-labs-mlk-memorial-library)). The Digital Commons at MLK has about 80 PCs and Macs, 3D printers, and the Dream Lab, a ~1,800 sq ft free co-working space ([Urban Libraries Council](https://www.urbanlibraries.org/innovations/dcpl-digital-commons)).
- **Human / social:** Digital Commons ran 50–70 classes a month averaging ~10 attendees (roughly 500–700 people trained per month) and hosted DCTech meetups drawing over 500 attendees, per the ULC entry (year not stated) ([ULC](https://www.urbanlibraries.org/innovations/dcpl-digital-commons)). A named "digital inclusion coordinator" role exists on staff ([DCPL news](https://www.dclibrary.org/news/future-proof-your-career-dc-public-library-launches-ai-upskilling-cohort)).
- **Political:** The Labs at DCPL is supported in part by the Institute of Museum and Library Services ([The Labs at DCPL](https://dclibrary.org/labsatdcpl)).

## What they want
The 2015 launch framed the labs as moving the library "from passive centers of learning to active destinations for creativity and innovation" ([Mayor's office, 2015](https://mayor.dc.gov/release/mayor-bowser-launches-new-innovation-labs-mlk-memorial-library)). The 2025 cohort announcement states that the library will track engagement and completion and survey participants to refine the model ([DCPL news, 2025](https://www.dclibrary.org/news/future-proof-your-career-dc-public-library-launches-ai-upskilling-cohort)).

## What they've tried about this problem, and how it went
- **On-ramp by appointment:** All Labs spaces require an orientation ("Machine Intro" or "DIY Session"), a signed release, government ID and an active DC library card; some programs have limited capacity and no waitlist ([The Labs at DCPL](https://dclibrary.org/labsatdcpl)).
- **Tiered classes:** Digital Commons classes are labelled basic / intermediate / advanced (Online Application Assistance through Python) ([ULC](https://www.urbanlibraries.org/innovations/dcpl-digital-commons)).
- **AI Upskilling Cohort (2025):** 12-week pilot with Levy; 50 participants selected from 200 applications, over 60% with advanced degrees, average 15+ years' experience; one month in, participants generated 50+ problem spaces narrowed to 12 and formed 6 pods; no job guarantee; employers invited to an October showcase ([Technical.ly, 12 Sep 2025](https://technical.ly/civic-news/ai-upskilling-cohort-dc-workforce/); [DCPL event listing](https://dclibrary.libnet.info/event/14522100)). Outcomes after October 2025 are not published on the pages fetched.

## How they see the problem
Published framing is access and creativity ("anyone can be a maker") in 2015 and, by 2025, AI literacy as "the next essential skill" for job seekers including displaced federal workers ([2015 release](https://mayor.dc.gov/release/mayor-bowser-launches-new-innovation-labs-mlk-memorial-library); [2025 news](https://www.dclibrary.org/news/future-proof-your-career-dc-public-library-launches-ai-upskilling-cohort)). Inference: the library frames itself as host and convener, with program design supplied by partners.
