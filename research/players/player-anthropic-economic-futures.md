# Player: Anthropic Economic Futures Program (program)

**Circle:** field
**Scope:** national
**Role in the problem:** funder (research), policy
**Logged:** 2026-11-06

## Who they are, and where people meet them
An AI developer's program, announced June 27, 2025, to study and respond to AI's economic effects: research grants, policy forums and an expanded Anthropic Economic Index ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/introducing-the-anthropic-economic-futures-program)). People meet it as researchers (rapid grants "up to $50,000" with API credits), as authors of policy proposals for symposia in Washington, DC (Sept 2025) and London (Nov 2025), or as readers of the Economic Index.

## What they bring
- Financial: research awards up to $50K on a rolling basis; API credits and partnerships for independent research institutions ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/introducing-the-anthropic-economic-futures-program)). Later coverage reports larger commitments ($200M–$350M figures appear in secondary blogs, unverified; [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/27/as-job-losses-loom-anthropic-launches-program-to-track-ais-economic-fallout) covers the launch).
- Cultural: longitudinal data on AI usage by task, published regularly.

## What they want
Evidence-based policy on AI's labor-market effects; the components are research, policy and measurement ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/introducing-the-anthropic-economic-futures-program)).

## What they've tried about this problem, and how it went
- No upskilling or training program is described; a related link mentions worker retraining but is not detailed ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/introducing-the-anthropic-economic-futures-program)). Outcomes of the symposia were not found in sources opened.

## How they see the problem
Frame: a measurement and policy problem (what AI is doing to tasks, productivity and wages) rather than a delivery problem. Inference: a regional network would be visible to it as a research site or a policy-proposal author, not as a grantee for programs.
