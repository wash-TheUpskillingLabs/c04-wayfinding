# Player: Byte Back (program — closed 2024)

**Circle:** field
**Scope:** regional
**Role in the problem:** peer program (former) | failure case
**Logged:** 2026-11-06

## Who they are, and where people meet them
A DC nonprofit that ran no-cost digital-literacy classes (Microsoft Office basics through CompTIA A+) for 27 years, in English and Spanish, in person and online, expanding to Baltimore in 2019; it ceased programming at the end of July 2024 without publishing a reason ([Technical.ly, 29 Jul 2024](https://technical.ly/professional-development/byte-back-closing-digital-literacy-nonprofit-dc/)). Nobody meets it now; its students were offered places at Computer CORE in Alexandria, a much smaller nonprofit with about 600 adults enrolled a year ([Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/professional-development/byte-back-closing-digital-literacy-nonprofit-dc/)).

## What they bring
- **Then:** ~7,000 people served by its 25th anniversary in 2022; 2022 revenue $3.6 million, 98% contributions, ~$3 million assets ([Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/professional-development/byte-back-closing-digital-literacy-nonprofit-dc/)).
- **Now:** a published evaluation. A study of almost 300 participants found employment rose from 14% to 40% within two months of a 24-hour course and daily computer use doubled ([RAND commentary, 1 Aug 2025](https://www.rand.org/pubs/commentary/2025/08/this-digital-skills-training-tripled-job-rates-why.html)).

## What they want
Not applicable. RAND's authors argue basic digital-skills training is "a pragmatic investment in workforce readiness, not charity" and that for participants "that first job is a foothold, not a finish line" ([RAND](https://www.rand.org/pubs/commentary/2025/08/this-digital-skills-training-tripled-job-rates-why.html)).

## What they've tried about this problem, and how it went
- **Short course, large effect, no survival:** RAND attributes closure to inability to attract sustained funding despite results ([RAND](https://www.rand.org/pubs/commentary/2025/08/this-digital-skills-training-tripled-job-rates-why.html)). The board declined to explain ([Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/professional-development/byte-back-closing-digital-literacy-nonprofit-dc/)).
- **Confidence, not access:** RAND reports many participants lacked confidence rather than computers ([RAND](https://www.rand.org/pubs/commentary/2025/08/this-digital-skills-training-tripled-job-rates-why.html)).
- Rival explanation: the 2024 article notes DC's $100 million BEAD award (May 2024) and Byte Back's digital-navigator role, suggesting public digital-equity money was shifting to infrastructure and navigators rather than classroom nonprofits — inference from juxtaposition, not stated by either source.

## How they see the problem
Digital confidence as the gate to employment; a first job as a foothold (RAND). Field condition: the region's longest-running digital-literacy on-ramp disappeared one year before the federal layoffs.

## Open questions for the pod
- Where did Byte Back's instructors and volunteers go, and are they a facilitator pool?
- Which funders dropped Byte Back, and are they the same ones the Labs would approach?
