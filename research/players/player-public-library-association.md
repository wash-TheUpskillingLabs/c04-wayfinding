# Player: Public Library Association / American Library Association (network)

**Circle:** field
**Scope:** national
**Role in the problem:** host (via member libraries), standard-setter, funder (small incentives)
**Logged:** 2026-11-06

## Who they are, and where people meet them
PLA is the public-library division of ALA, founded 1944, with nearly 9,000 members ([PLA release, Dec 23 2025](https://www.ala.org/news/2025/12/pla-launches-transformative-technology-task-force-focus-artificial-intelligence)). DC Public Library staff meet it through its conference, its digital-literacy incentives, DigitalLearn and its new AI task force. For The Labs it is the professional body of its hosts.

## What they bring
- Financial (small, targeted): Digital Literacy Workshop Incentives supported by AT&T, up to $10,000 per library; in Dec 2024 PLA funded more than 100 libraries as part of a $2.7M effort ([ALA release, Dec 10 2024](https://www.ala.org/news/2024/12/pla-provides-funding-more-100-public-libraries-part-27-million-effort-boost-digital); [incentive page](https://www.ala.org/pla/initiatives/digitalliteracy/incentive)). A Digital Navigator Workshop pilot incentive also exists ([page](https://www.ala.org/pla/initiatives/digitalliteracy/incentive/nav-overview)).
- Human/cultural: a Transformative Technology Task Force (9 members, began Nov 2025, two-year AI focus) that will recommend priority training topics for staff and users; AI ranked in the top five professional-development priorities in a 2025 survey ([PLA release](https://www.ala.org/news/2025/12/pla-launches-transformative-technology-task-force-focus-artificial-intelligence)).
- Partnership: "Building AI Skills and Agency Through Libraries" with Microsoft Elevate, announced Oct 22, 2026: free AI training and webinars for library workers and patrons, a community of practice, and case studies ([Route Fifty, Oct 26 2026](https://www.route-fifty.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/10/microsoft-help-public-libraries-build-ai-literacy-communities/416817/)).

## What they want
The task-force chair's stated goal is AI access and knowledge for all library staff so they can help the public, with a focus on "literacy and access rather than adoption of specific tools" ([Route Fifty](https://www.route-fifty.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/10/microsoft-help-public-libraries-build-ai-literacy-communities/416817/)).

## What they've tried about this problem, and how it went
- Digital Literacy Workshop Incentives: small grants tied to running workshops from PLA's DigitalLearn curriculum; a cohort of libraries shared NSC's 92% finding as motivation ([ALA Connect](https://connect.ala.org/pla/discussion/fw-new-report-92-of-jobs-require-digital-skills)). Published outcome data not found in sources opened.
- PLA 2026 conference (Apr 1–3, Minneapolis) carried five AI programs including AI book clubs for older adults and building AI agents for program engagement ([PLA release](https://www.ala.org/news/2025/12/pla-launches-transformative-technology-task-force-focus-artificial-intelligence)).
- Its Project Outcome measurement tool is covered in the existing case `field-funders/case-pla-project-outcome.md`.

## How they see the problem
Frame: staff capacity first, then patron programs; AI as a literacy topic delivered in workshops, like earlier digital-literacy work. Inference: a multi-week cohort or a post-program project route is not part of the workshop-incentive model.
