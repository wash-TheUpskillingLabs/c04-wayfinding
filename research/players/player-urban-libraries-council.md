# Player: Urban Libraries Council, ULC (network)

**Circle:** field
**Scope:** national
**Role in the problem:** policy, standard-setter, host (via members)
**Logged:** 2026-11-06

## Who they are, and where people meet them
A membership body of 189 large public library systems in the US and Canada (40 states), whose US members serve more than 86 million residents ([Why Libraries Matter, Apr 2025](https://urbanlibraries.org/files/ULC-Why-Libraries-Matter_April-2025.pdf)). DC Public Library's leadership meets it through its forum, innovation awards and policy work; a participant at The Labs would not meet it directly.

## What they bring
- Political: it lobbies for libraries in WIOA reauthorization; its CEO Brooks Rainwater argued for the House provision making libraries eligible as one-stop workforce centers (House passed the Stronger Workforce for America Act 378–26) ([The Well News op-ed](https://thewellnews.com/opinions/wioa-reauthorization-must-recognize-libraries-workforce-development-potential)).
- Cultural: an AI initiative since January 2019 and an AI leadership brief (Sept 2019) ([ULC release](https://www.urbanlibraries.org/newsroom/new-ulc-leadership-brief-calls-on-public-libraries-to-lead-on-ai)); research and data on demand for free public spaces, cited in 2026 coverage ([Route Fifty](https://www.route-fifty.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/10/microsoft-help-public-libraries-build-ai-literacy-communities/416817/)).
- Budget not verified.

## What they want
Federal support for WIOA reauthorization, small business and entrepreneurship, skills development, workforce training and home broadband; libraries positioned as workforce and economic-development hubs ([Why Libraries Matter](https://urbanlibraries.org/files/ULC-Why-Libraries-Matter_April-2025.pdf); [op-ed](https://thewellnews.com/opinions/wioa-reauthorization-must-recognize-libraries-workforce-development-potential)).

## What they've tried about this problem, and how it went
- Documented member examples: Las Vegas-Clark County's EmployNV Career Hubs at five branches since 2017, with "thousands" enrolled in training; Kansas City libraries' decade of entrepreneur training. The op-ed concedes most libraries lack dedicated funding for workforce programs ([op-ed](https://thewellnews.com/opinions/wioa-reauthorization-must-recognize-libraries-workforce-development-potential)).
- Its 2019 AI brief framed the library's "teaching role" as preparing the public for AI ([ULC release](https://www.urbanlibraries.org/newsroom/new-ulc-leadership-brief-calls-on-public-libraries-to-lead-on-ai)). No outcome data found.
- Its research chief has said libraries can use demand for AI help to make the case for more local funding ([Route Fifty](https://www.route-fifty.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/10/microsoft-help-public-libraries-build-ai-literacy-communities/416817/)).

## How they see the problem
Frame: the library as an under-recognized, under-funded workforce institution; the fix is formal eligibility in federal workforce law. Inference: the actor in its frame is the library system, not the people who run programs inside it.
