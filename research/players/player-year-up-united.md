# Player: Year Up United (institution)

**Circle:** field
**Scope:** national
**Role in the problem:** peer program, employer partner
**Logged:** 2026-11-06

## Who they are, and where people meet them
A tuition-free program for 18–29-year-olds, founded 2000 in Boston with 22 students, now "52,000+ students supported" across 20+ campuses, with four Career Pathways (Business, Customer Experience, IT, Software) delivered as Train, Experience (corporate internship), Access (coaching, YUPRO Placement) ([yearup.org](https://www.yearup.org); [AIR PROPEL](https://air.org/PROPEL/growing-and-diversifying-year-model)). People meet it as applicants, interns at corporate partners, or employers. Its National Capital Region site was not confirmed from pages opened.

## What they bring
- Human: 72% of graduates employed or enrolled within four months; $55,000 average starting salary; 92% alumni satisfaction ([yearup.org](https://www.yearup.org)).
- Cultural: the program with the strongest randomized evidence in the field (the PACE study; outcomes page at yearup.org/research/pace not opened here, so figures are not cited).

## What they want
A skills-first approach adopted by companies, and expanded economic mobility ([25th anniversary page](https://www.yearup.org/about/stories/25thAnniversary)).

## What they've tried about this problem, and how it went
- Pre-cycle: its "time-intensive assessment processes have made efficient placement difficult and limited their ability to serve a broader population," so it is co-developing entry assessment tools with AIR's PROPEL Center ([AIR](https://air.org/PROPEL/growing-and-diversifying-year-model)). This is a documented on-ramp bottleneck.
- Post-cycle: YUPRO Placement and alumni engagement (2015–2019 timeline) ([25th anniversary page](https://www.yearup.org/about/stories/25thAnniversary)).
- AI: not mentioned on pages opened.

## How they see the problem
Frame: selection, intensive training, a corporate internship as the bridge, a job as the end. Inference: breadth is limited by the cost of its own front door, which it is now trying to reduce with assessment tools.

## Open questions for the pod
- What share of applicants are not admitted, and is there any referral out?
- What do the new entry assessments measure, and could a library-based project serve as evidence?
