# Player: Climb Hire (institution)

**Circle:** field
**Scope:** national (California and Colorado footprint)
**Role in the problem:** peer program
**Logged:** 2026-11-06

## Who they are, and where people meet them
A seven-year-old workforce nonprofit, founded by Nitzan Pelman, built on a social-capital curriculum: "Climbers" get 200 hours of training, career navigation, pod-based learning and a professional network; it serves "hundreds of Climbers" with connections in California and Colorado ([2025 Annual Report, CEO letter](https://online.flippingbook.com/view/14595812/3/)). People meet it as applicants or through its network events; it has no Greater Washington footprint found.

## What they bring
- Social: the model's distinguishing asset is the network itself; alumni pay it forward (described in interviews, e.g. [Climb Hire podcast page](https://climbhire.co/?p=626), not opened).
- Financial: a $6M grant reported in Dec 2023 ([NorCal Public Media](https://norcalpublicmedia.org/2023122694103/news-feed/nonprofit-gets-6-million-grant-to-help-low-wage-workers-climb-higher), not opened). Budget not stated on the page opened.

## What they want
Equity-focused mobility for low-wage workers through skills plus relationships ([CEO letter](https://online.flippingbook.com/view/14595812/3/)).

## What they've tried about this problem, and how it went
- Pods as the unit of learning and a network as the post-program structure; placement and wage numbers appear in later report pages that were not opened (unverified).
- AI training not mentioned in the CEO letter.

## How they see the problem
Frame: the missing ingredient is social capital, not skill; the route after the program is the network. This is the closest national analogue to The Labs' pods and Share phase, at a much smaller scale.

- What does a pod look like after the 200 hours end?
