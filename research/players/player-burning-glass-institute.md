# Player: Burning Glass Institute (institution)

**Circle:** field
**Scope:** national
**Role in the problem:** standard-setter (research)
**Logged:** 2026-11-06

## Who they are, and where people meet them
A labor-market research nonprofit led by Matt Sigelman, working from job-posting and career-history data. People meet it through its reports, which are the common reference for "is skills-based hiring real" and "is AI eating entry-level jobs" ([research index](https://www.burningglassinstitute.org/research/tag/Talent+Strategy)).

## What they bring
- Human/cultural: a published research line including "Skills-Based Hiring: The Long Road from Pronouncements to Practice," "The Emerging Degree Reset," "No Country for Young Grads," "Talent Disrupted," "The Expertise Upheaval," and a "Doppler Radar for AI" tracker that reports junior software developer demand down 10% ([research index](https://www.burningglassinstitute.org/research/tag/Talent+Strategy)).
- A Skills-First Workforce Initiative with Fortune 500 employers that built a shared skills language covering 30 roles and the skills of over 35 million workers ([same](https://www.burningglassinstitute.org/research/tag/Talent+Strategy)).
- Co-publishes the American Opportunity Index (corporate scorecard of worker advancement) with the Schultz Family Foundation and HBS ([same](https://www.burningglassinstitute.org/research/tag/Talent+Strategy)).
- Budget not verified.

## What they want
Not stated as a mission on the page opened; the titles frame its interest as making hiring practice match skills-first pronouncements and tracking AI's effect on the early-career ladder.

## What they've tried about this problem, and how it went
- Research only; no programs. Its finding that skills-based hiring announcements outrun practice is the standard caution against treating a dropped degree requirement as a route (report title in [index](https://www.burningglassinstitute.org/research/tag/Talent+Strategy); findings detailed in the existing case `field-employers/case-skills-based-hiring-practice.md`).
- Its early-career work is widely re-reported as "AI is taking entry-level jobs" (e.g. [UC San Diego career blog, Oct 2025](https://unex-careerhub.ucsd.edu/blog/2025/10/06/is-ai-killing-the-early-career-job-market-parsing-the-data-from-three-high-profile-studies/)); the Institute's own framing is about structural forces, not a single cause.

## How they see the problem
Frame: the labor market is legible through postings and career histories; the "ladder" is the unit, and AI is removing its lower rungs. Inference: community-based learning is invisible in its data unless it produces a posting-visible credential or a job change.

## Open questions for the pod
- Which skills in the 30-role shared language overlap with what a Build Cycle produces?
- Does any BGI dataset capture informal, volunteer or project-based learning?
