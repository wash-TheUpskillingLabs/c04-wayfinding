# Theory: Calm technology, information scent, and designing across channels

**Bears on:** interfaces, activation-mobilization, pre-cycle, post-cycle
**Logged:** 2026-11-05

This strand is about how technology can keep people informed without seizing their attention, and how people decide which path to follow when looking for information. It runs from Xerox PARC in the 1990s (calm technology; information foraging) through a 2006 critique of the calm vision to practitioner restatements and early work on information architecture that spans many channels.

## Works

### Mark Weiser and John Seely Brown, *Designing Calm Technology* (1995)
- **Link:** [calmtech.com archive of the paper](https://calmtech.com/papers/designing-calm-technology), dated 21 Dec 1995, Xerox PARC.
- **Core idea:** Technology can sit at the edge of attention and move to the centre only when needed. Information kept at the periphery is absorbed without effort, so people stay informed without overload; being able to pull a peripheral concern into focus and act on it is what makes the experience calm. Examples: a string that twitches with network traffic; glass panels between offices and hallways.
- **Vocabulary worth borrowing:** *periphery* (what you sense without focusing); *center* (what you are attending to); *peripheral reach* (how much detail can live in the background); *locatedness* (feeling oriented in your surroundings).
- **Touches:** Describes how a person can be aware of activity around them (others working late, network busy) without being told, which bears on staying "activated" between peaks.
- **Critiques and limits:** Yvonne Rogers (2006) argued the calm-computing agenda's results did not match Weiser's vision and proposed designing for "engaging rather than calming people" ([Rogers, UbiComp 2006, Open Research Online](https://oro.open.ac.uk/5253)). The authors themselves called their thinking incomplete ([calmtech.com](https://calmtech.com/papers/designing-calm-technology)).

### Amber Case, *Calm Technology* (O'Reilly; principles published 2015)
- **Link:** [Principles of Calm Technology, caseorganic.com](https://www.caseorganic.com/post/principles-of-calm-technology); [Chapter 2 excerpt, O'Reilly, 21 Mar 2016](https://oreilly.com/content/principles-calm-technology-we-are-not-bad-at-technology-technology-is-bad-at-us).
- **Core idea:** Eight design principles: demand as little attention as possible; inform and calm; use the periphery; amplify the best of people and machines; communicate without necessarily speaking; fail gracefully to a usable state; use the minimum technology needed; respect social norms and introduce change gradually. The O'Reilly excerpt collects failures such as a car that kept announcing "your door is ajar", networked smoke alarms that echoed and could not be silenced, and a lighting bug that locked lights at full brightness.
- **Vocabulary worth borrowing:** *fail gracefully* (fall back to a usable state); *minimum technology needed* (the least that solves the problem); *communicate without speech* (a tone, light or vibration instead of words).
- **Touches:** States conditions for alerts and status signals that reach someone outside a session (a reminder, a calendar ping), and for what happens when a channel breaks.
- **Critiques and limits:** No published critique located in this pass. The principles are a practitioner synthesis; the excerpt's examples are anecdotes, not studies.

### Peter Pirolli and Stuart Card, *Information Foraging* (1999)
- **Link:** [PhilPapers record: *Psychological Review* 106(4), 643–675, 1999](https://philpapers.org/rec/PIRIF); background on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_foraging).
- **Core idea:** Developed at PARC from optimal-foraging theory in ecology, it models people looking for information as foragers deciding which "patch" to work and when to leave. People judge from cues how likely a path is to pay off, follow it, and switch when the signal stops getting stronger.
- **Vocabulary worth borrowing:** *information scent* (cues that predict what's down a path); *patch* (a source worth working, such as a site or a channel); *informavore* (a person as information-seeker).
- **Touches:** Describes why someone abandons a path (a link, a channel, a thread) when its cues stop matching what they want; applies to a person choosing between a branch workshop listing, a Slack channel and an email digest.
- **Critiques and limits:** Wikipedia notes models estimate relevance from word statistics in large text collections and were later stretched to social settings; no direct critique opened in this pass ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_foraging)).

### Resmini and Rosati, *Pervasive Information Architecture: Designing Cross-Channel User Experiences* (Morgan Kaufmann, 2011)
- **Link:** [Review notice, I Programmer, 18 Oct 2011](https://www.i-programmer.info/book-watch-archive/3065-pervasive-information-architecture-designing-cross-channel-user-experiences-morgan-kaufmann.html); [publisher listing via Barnes & Noble](https://www.barnesandnoble.com/w/pervasive-information-architecture-andrea-resmini/1147770154).
- **Core idea:** Extends information architecture (organising and labelling for navigation) to experiences that span several channels and mix physical and digital touchpoints. Detailed heuristics from the book were not verified in this pass and are left out.
- **Vocabulary worth borrowing:** *cross-channel* (one journey crossing several media, as distinct from the same content copied to each).
- **Touches:** Speaks to a single participant journey that crosses a library branch, chat, email and a web app.
- **Critiques and limits:** None located in this pass. Only secondary listings were opened; treat the summary as thin.

## Questions for the pod
- Weiser and Brown say calm comes from being able to pull a background concern into focus and act on it. For a person between Cycles, what in The Labs is in their periphery right now, and what would they have to do to pull it into focus?
- Rogers argued for engaging rather than calming. The Labs wants both "activated" (low-key, ongoing) and "mobilized" (a collective push). Did any case in this stream show one system doing both without the loud mode drowning the quiet one?
- Information scent is judged per path. When the same next step is announced in a branch, in chat and by email, does the scent stay the same, or does each channel's wording send people different ways?
