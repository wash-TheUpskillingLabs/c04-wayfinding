# Case: Hack for LA (with Civic Tech DC and the 2019 Brigade "new person" breakout), how newcomers find a project in a volunteer civic-tech group

**Kind:** paradigmatic (with a documented failure-and-redesign inside it)
**Bears on:** pre-cycle, cycle, facilitators, interfaces, activation-mobilization
**Logged:** 2026-11-05 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
Hack for LA is a Los Angeles volunteer civic-tech group (a former Code for America Brigade, listed as founded 2013) that runs a structured onboarding pipeline before volunteers are placed on a project team. Two other sources show the same threshold elsewhere: Brigade leaders' 2019 discussion of newcomers, and Civic Tech DC's account of how people find work.

## 2. Where it came from
Hack for LA's own public documentation (GitHub wiki pages, a project-history page for its CivicTechJobs board, and its Meetup onboarding event page); the Civic Tech Field Guide listing; a Code for America Network forum report from a 2019 Brigade Day breakout on "New Person Engagement"; and an undated organizer write-up from Civic Tech DC (formerly Code for DC).

## 3. When
- Hack for LA founded 2013 ([Civic Tech Field Guide](https://civictech.guide/projects/hack-for-la)).
- Onboarding phases: pre-2020 (in person), 2020 to mid-2021 (Zoom, team-run), mid-2021 onward (central onboarding + communities of practice).
- Onboarding event page viewed for a 29 Apr 2024 session.
- Brigade breakout: 29 May 2019, continuing a discussion from October 2018.
- Civic Tech DC write-up: undated (after the 2023 rename).

## 4. What it says
**Hack for LA: three phases of the newcomer path** ([CivicTechJobs history](https://hackforla.github.io/CivicTechJobs/misc/history))
- Before COVID, volunteers were recruited in person at onboarding nights.
- From 2020 to mid-2021, open roles were posted on the website and newcomers could join weekly Zoom onboarding; each team handled its own onboarding, which was inconsistent. The page reports that some projects received many volunteers without the needed skill level and links this to high turnover and product-manager burnout. Its phrase: "some projects got too many volunteers but not the right skill level."
- From mid-2021, all newcomers complete central onboarding, then join a "community of practice" (CoP) by discipline. Open roles left the main website; each CoP has its own opportunity board, CoP leads coach volunteers on fit, and listings show whether a project is ready to take on volunteers. A dedicated job board (CivicTechJobs) was approved to let volunteers search for roles matching their goals.

**Hack for LA: what onboarding asks of a newcomer** ([Meetup event page, Apr 2024](https://www.meetup.com/codeforla/events/300190014/))
- Part 1 is a Zoom webinar on how the group works, its tools, requirements and CoPs. Part 2 is self-paced with help: GitHub account and two-factor authentication, code of conduct, Slack, an onboarding form, a Slack self-introduction, and a placement step.
- Sessions run 1.5 to 3 hours and can be split in two. Engineers and UX researchers take self-tests for placement; other disciplines are placed through a separate process; some are matched during the session, others choose among teams with openings.

**Hack for LA: joining one team after placement** ([Website team onboarding wiki](https://hackforla.github.io/website-wiki/pending-audit/onboarding/joining-the-hackforla-org-website-team))
- A newcomer to the website team confirms they can attend meeting times, introduces themselves in a team Slack channel, messages a product manager, accepts a shared drive invite, adds themselves to a roster spreadsheet, gets GitHub access from a discipline lead, reads an introduction and a Kanban guide, and then follows role-specific steps. That is roughly nine steps across four tools before first work.

**Brigades generally (2019)** ([CfA Network forum, Brigade Day breakout, 29 May 2019](https://discourse.codeforamerica.org/t/brigade-day-breakout-new-person-engagement/516))
- 35 Brigade participants listed newcomer problems: meetings open with introductions then split into project work that suits regulars; newcomers perceive cliques; no clear direction on how to contribute; too few projects to offer; first-timers push to change project direction; onboarding takes long and many first-timers never return.
- Top proposed fixes (by votes, not tested results): written self-start documentation plus scheduled member help (11); a low-commitment "honeypot" project such as a learning group (11); follow-up surveys of first-timers (9); boards listing technical and non-technical tasks (9).

**Civic Tech DC** ([Making of a Civic Tech Community, undated](https://helengarden.netlify.app/making-of-civic-tech))
- The organizer write-up says knowledge has mostly passed from organizers to newcomers informally, face to face. It describes two orientations among newcomers: people who want a team and a defined problem, and people who want to act on their own ideas; an informal incubator was meant to help the second group turn a pitch into a plan with milestones.

## Failure modes seen
- **Firehose to the wrong team.** Posting open roles publicly in 2020–21 sent many under-skilled volunteers to some projects, linked to turnover and product-manager burnout. ([CivicTechJobs history](https://hackforla.github.io/CivicTechJobs/misc/history))
- **Every team onboards differently.** Team-run onboarding in 2020–21 was inconsistent across the organization. ([CivicTechJobs history](https://hackforla.github.io/CivicTechJobs/misc/history))
- **Regulars' meeting, newcomers' confusion.** Project nights opened with introductions and then split into work that suited returning members, leaving first-timers without direction. ([CfA forum, 2019](https://discourse.codeforamerica.org/t/brigade-day-breakout-new-person-engagement/516))
- **Not enough doors.** Some Brigades had too few projects to offer new attendees. ([CfA forum, 2019](https://discourse.codeforamerica.org/t/brigade-day-breakout-new-person-engagement/516))
- **Onboarding cost with no return.** Onboarding drained project-night time and many first-timers never came back. ([CfA forum, 2019](https://discourse.codeforamerica.org/t/brigade-day-breakout-new-person-engagement/516))
- **Tool-stack threshold.** A newcomer completes GitHub, two-factor, Slack, a form, a roster and a drive before placement and team work. ([Meetup page](https://www.meetup.com/codeforla/events/300190014/); [team wiki](https://hackforla.github.io/website-wiki/pending-audit/onboarding/joining-the-hackforla-org-website-team)) Whether this causes drop-off is not documented.

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- No public figures on how many people start onboarding, finish it, get placed, or are still active after 3 or 6 months.
- The 2021 redesign's effects are not reported; the job board's adoption is unknown.
- The 2019 breakout lists proposals, not tested results.
- The Civic Tech DC write-up is undated and its author is not named.
- **Rival explanation:** the 2020–21 mismatch and turnover may have come mostly from the pandemic surge of remote volunteers with free time (and later return to work), not from how roles were posted.
- Who could confirm: Hack for LA's organization team and CoP leads (via its public GitHub/Slack); Civic Tech DC organizers.
