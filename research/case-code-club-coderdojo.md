# Case: Code Club and CoderDojo (Raspberry Pi Foundation), free project kits run by volunteers in schools and libraries

**Kind:** paradigmatic (with documented failures at the club level)
**Bears on:** pre-cycle, facilitators, activation-mobilization, interfaces
**Product part:** kits | facilitators
**Logged:** 2026-11-06 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
Two volunteer-run networks of free after-school coding clubs for children, each built around a catalog of free step-by-step projects that a non-expert adult can run. Code Club (UK, 2012) and CoderDojo (Ireland, 2011) were both absorbed by the Raspberry Pi Foundation (2015 and 2017).

## 2. Where it came from
Code Club was a UK charity that merged into the Raspberry Pi Foundation in November 2015; CoderDojo is a Dublin-based charity that joined in 2017. Sources: TechCrunch's merger report, the Foundation's 2017 annual review, an NFER randomised trial of Code Club (2015–16), a Durham University quasi-experimental evaluation (2023–24), the Foundation's 2025 Code Club annual survey, and a Foundation blog post on the CoderDojo Girls Initiative.

## 3. When
CoderDojo began 2011; Code Club 2012; Code Club merger November 2015; CoderDojo merger announced 26 May 2017. NFER trial June 2015–September 2016. Durham evaluation October 2023–April 2024; summary report October 2024. Annual survey sent March 2025.

## 4. What it says

**The kit.** Code Club's materials are free, step-by-step projects in Scratch, HTML/CSS and Python, plus "Notes for Club Leaders"; the projects make games, animations, websites and applications. In the NFER trial, club leaders rated the projects as the single most important success factor, with about four-fifths calling them "very important" ([NFER RCT report](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/media/jtyds5xm/code01.pdf)). The intended model was one term each of Scratch, HTML/CSS and Python ([NFER](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/media/jtyds5xm/code01.pdf)). In 2025, 99% of 543 responding clubs used Scratch, 57% Python, 40% HTML/CSS, and 1,416 clubs said they mostly or always use the Foundation's projects or "pathways" site ([Code Club annual survey 2025](https://static.raspberrypi.org/files/about/Code_Club_annual_survey_report_2025.pdf)). CoderDojo's equivalent is "Sushi Cards" (short printed project cards, e.g. for wearables and App Inventor) and three "Mini-Sushi" taster projects in Scratch, HTML and App Inventor ([Raspberry Pi blog, CoderDojo Girls Initiative](https://www.raspberrypi.org/blog/coderdojo-girls-initiative/)). The Durham evaluators observed learners mostly working on individual projects and browsing the projects website ([Durham summary report](https://static.raspberrypi.org/files/about/Code_Club_Summary_Report_Durham-Oct_2024.pdf)).

**Merger and scale.** At the 2015 Code Club merger there were about 3,150 UK clubs; by May 2017 almost 5,900 UK clubs and about 10,000 worldwide. CoderDojo had more than 1,250 Dojos in 69 countries, and the stated target was 5,000 Dojos by end of 2020 ([TechCrunch, 26 May 2017](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/26/raspberry-pi-foundation-and-coderdojo-to-code-club-together)). CoderDojo kept its own brand and charity status and stayed "platform-neutral" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/26/raspberry-pi-foundation-and-coderdojo-to-code-club-together)). The Foundation's 2017 review reports Code Club grew by 2,964 clubs to over 10,000 and CoderDojo by 41% to 1,556 active Dojos ([Raspberry Pi 2017 annual review](https://www.raspberrypi.org/blog/annual-review-2017/)).

**Counts later.** The 2025 survey counts 7,494 Code Clubs in 102 countries that "have run in the last two years," with 1,001 new clubs since January 2025 and 38% in low- and middle-income countries ([Code Club annual survey 2025](https://static.raspberrypi.org/files/about/Code_Club_annual_survey_report_2025.pdf)). Interpretation: the 2017 "over 10,000" and the 2025 "7,494 run in the last two years" are not the same measure, but together they suggest that a large share of registered clubs stop running; the Foundation's documents reviewed here give no closure figure. Whether the CoderDojo target of 5,000 Dojos by 2020 was met is not covered by the sources opened here (unverified).

**What the RCT found.** 688 Year 5 pupils in 35 schools were recruited; 317 pupils from 21 schools reached primary analysis. No significant effect on the Bebras computational-thinking test (both arms improved about 16 points); a significant gain on a coding-skills quiz (+1.49 points) and in confidence making things with code; no effect on future interest in coding ([NFER](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/media/jtyds5xm/code01.pdf)). Four schools withdrew during the year, mainly for staffing problems in running the club; one club paused for several weeks while a new leader was found; schools that declined before randomisation cited no staff member or volunteer, too little pupil interest, and lack of parental buy-in ([NFER](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/media/jtyds5xm/code01.pdf)). Of 22 leaders, half said the club ran as intended and half did not; almost all covered Scratch, 17 covered HTML/CSS 1, 12 covered Python 1 and only 3 reached Python 2 ([NFER](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/media/jtyds5xm/code01.pdf)). Though designed to be volunteer-led, most trial clubs were run by teachers; 10 of 14 respondents ran the club alone and only one had an outside volunteer ([NFER](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/media/jtyds5xm/code01.pdf)).

**The 2024 evaluation.** A difference-in-differences study (not randomised): 412 learners at pre-survey, 274 at post-survey; 57 members and 166 non-members in the main analysis. Members gained more on a coding quiz (effect size 0.24) and on confidence to learn independently (0.49); the effect on belonging was 0.1 ([Durham summary](https://static.raspberrypi.org/files/about/Code_Club_Summary_Report_Durham-Oct_2024.pdf)). Leaders were not all teachers and depended on school staff to be present; some reported difficulty when a school could not assign staff ([Durham summary](https://static.raspberrypi.org/files/about/Code_Club_Summary_Report_Durham-Oct_2024.pdf)).

**Who the volunteers are.** The 2025 survey reached 1,689 clubs and 775 mentors, and the authors note that answers came from 8% to 26% of clubs depending on question ([Code Club annual survey 2025](https://static.raspberrypi.org/files/about/Code_Club_annual_survey_report_2025.pdf)). CoderDojo reported 29% of attendees were girls in 2017, with a 40% target ([Raspberry Pi blog](https://www.raspberrypi.org/blog/coderdojo-girls-initiative/)).

## Failure modes seen
- **Club dies with its one adult.** Four of the trial schools withdrew mainly over staffing, and one club stopped for weeks until a new leader was found ([NFER](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/media/jtyds5xm/code01.pdf)).
- **Kit run short.** Half of leaders did not run the three-term model; only 3 of 22 clubs reached the second Python unit, citing time, leader knowledge, software trouble and pupils finding Python hard ([NFER](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/media/jtyds5xm/code01.pdf)).
- **"Volunteer-led" in design, teacher-led in practice.** Most trial clubs were run by a teacher alone; one outside volunteer across 14 respondents ([NFER](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/media/jtyds5xm/code01.pdf)).
- **Host institution as a hidden dependency.** Leaders could not run sessions when the school assigned no staff member ([Durham summary](https://static.raspberrypi.org/files/about/Code_Club_Summary_Report_Durham-Oct_2024.pdf)).
- **Registered-versus-active gap.** 10,000+ clubs in 2017 versus 7,494 that ran in the two years to 2025; no published closure count ([2017 review](https://www.raspberrypi.org/blog/annual-review-2017/); [2025 survey](https://static.raspberrypi.org/files/about/Code_Club_annual_survey_report_2025.pdf)).
- **Skills without routes.** The RCT found coding skill and confidence gains but no change in interest in coding careers ([NFER](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/media/jtyds5xm/code01.pdf)).

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- The projects were the thing leaders valued most, yet the clubs that died died for lack of an adult, not a project. What does a kit for The Labs have to contain about the *person*, not the activity?
- Code Club's kit assumes a school building, a term calendar, a captive age group and a staff member on site. Which of those does a branch library replace, and which does it not?
- Leaders stopped at the hard module (Python). Where is the equivalent "Python" in a Cycle, and who is allowed to skip it?
- What would The Labs count: registered labs, labs that met this year, or labs that shipped something?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
We cannot see closure data; the Foundation publishes growth and survey figures, not an active/inactive ledger. Rival explanation for the gap between 10,000+ and 7,494: counting rules changed (the 2025 figure explicitly limits to clubs that ran in two years, and the pandemic sits between them), so the gap may measure the metric, not attrition. Rival explanation for the RCT's null computational-thinking result: the trial ran during assessments that ate club time and most clubs never reached the later units, so it may test a half-delivered kit rather than the kit. The Foundation's research team and the NFER/Durham authors could confirm. Blocked or not opened: none for this case.
