# Case: Learning and Employment Records (US), portable digital skill records piloted with employers

**Kind:** paradigmatic
**Bears on:** post-cycle, interfaces, activation-mobilization
**Field claims:** 2, 3, 4
**Evidence:** supports 3; complicates 2; contradicts 4 (in a 2024 survey of 2,049 jobseekers, 19% had ever been asked by an employer for a digital credential, and the 2020 federal pilot paper reports no measured hiring outcomes; [JFF, 2024](https://www.jff.org/blog/jobseekers-want-digital-credentials-for-skill-sharing-are-employers-ready/), [AWPAB, 2020](https://www.commerce.gov/sites/default/files/2020-09/LERwhitepaper09222020.pdf))
**Logged:** 2026-11-05 (desk research, sources below)

## 1. What it is
Learning and Employment Records (LERs) are digital, verifiable records of what a person has learned and done (in school, at work, in training, in the military), held by the person, often in a "wallet", and shared with employers. Since 2018 a coalition of federal advisory bodies, the US Chamber of Commerce Foundation's T3 Innovation Network, Jobs for the Future (JFF), standards bodies and foundations has piloted them.

## 2. Where it came from
- The American Workforce Policy Advisory Board (AWPAB), a White House advisory board, published the main progress paper through its Digital Infrastructure Working Group.
- The T3 Innovation Network (US Chamber of Commerce Foundation) ran the LER Resource Hub and a pilot directory.
- JFF ran demonstrations and commissioned a jobseeker survey from Morning Consult.
- The Aspen Institute's UpSkill America ran qualitative employer interviews.
- Sources: AWPAB white paper (2020); US Chamber Foundation blog (2020); JFF blog and road map (2024); Aspen/UpSkill America employer research (2024, 2025); LER Accelerator white paper (2025).

## 3. When
- 2020: AWPAB white paper (September 2020) and launch of the T3 LER Resource Hub (July 2020).
- February 2024: JFF/Morning Consult jobseeker survey, published April and August 2024.
- March 2024: Aspen interviews with 12 companies, published October 2024.
- 2024: JFF three-site demonstration, road map published September 2024.
- February 2025: LER Accelerator white paper.
- 2025: Aspen/Walmart.org blog on next steps, including a summer 2025 veterans demonstration led by JFF.

## 4. What it says
- The AWPAB paper describes three flagship pilots: IBM (cybersecurity pathway, linking Western Governors University (WGU) and Central New Mexico Community College credentials to an IBM network, with learners using a National Student Clearinghouse wallet), Walmart (sharing verified WGU credentials with Walmart, Workday and LinkedIn) and Salesforce (a nurse-hiring talent network with a health system). Each is reported as a narrative profile of one learner, with no measured results. ([AWPAB, 2020](https://www.commerce.gov/sites/default/files/2020-09/LERwhitepaper09222020.pdf))
- The same paper counts 21 pilots under test (the three above plus 18 in the T3 pilot directory). It concludes the technology is viable and says early adopters face significant business risk; it calls for common standards, investment and federated governance. COVID-19 made it harder to recruit hospital partners and nurses for the Salesforce pilot. ([AWPAB, 2020](https://www.commerce.gov/sites/default/files/2020-09/LERwhitepaper09222020.pdf))
- The T3 Innovation Network reported more than 500 organizations working on the data and technology infrastructure. Its LER Hub announcement gives no adoption metrics. ([US Chamber Foundation, 2020](https://uschamberfoundation.org/blog/post/introducing-ler-hub-next-phase-development-learning-and-employment-records))
- The JFF/Morning Consult survey (2,049 jobseekers, February 2024) found that about 24% had used a digital credential in an application, 65% said they would likely use one after it was explained, and only 19% had ever been asked for one by an employer. 15% were unfamiliar and not interested; 80% of that group had no postsecondary degree. ([JFF, 2024](https://www.jff.org/blog/jobseekers-want-digital-credentials-for-skill-sharing-are-employers-ready/); summary also at [JFF report page](https://www.jff.org/idea/exploring-the-role-of-digital-wallets-and-digital-credentials-in-a-changing-job-market/))
- JFF's three-site demonstration (transitioning service members, women entrepreneurs, biotech entry-level jobseekers) found that all three sites needed more time than budgeted. Barriers named: hiring managers did not know how to read credential metadata, decision-makers could not tell verifiable credentials from badges, credentials certified program completion rather than specific skills, and partners lacked technical capacity. ([JFF, 2024](https://jff.org/realizing-the-potential-of-learning-and-employment-records-as-verifiable-credentials-a-road-map-for-successful-demonstrations))
- In interviews with 12 companies (March 2024), employers saw credentials as a positive sign but few had systematic ways to use them in hiring. They could not easily link a credential to a skill set and then to a job, and one participant called the landscape a "complex web of mess." ([Aspen/WGU press release via Yahoo Finance, 2024](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/employers-view-digital-credentials-positively-193200559.html))
- Aspen's employer-engagement report says significant obstacles separate current LER design from broad employer adoption, including how employers are communicated with. ([Aspen Institute, n.d.](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/publications/employer-engagement-in-learning-and-employment-records/))
- The LER Accelerator (higher-education associations convened through AACRAO) names the lack of a shared vocabulary among educators, learners and employers as the main barrier. It gives no LER issuance or adoption counts. ([LER Accelerator, Feb 2025](https://www.aacrao.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/LER-Accelerator.Unlocking-Potential-1.pdf))
- A 2025 Aspen/Walmart.org piece says there is still no simple, standardized way to prove skills learned in class, on the job or in training, and that pilots are surfacing user "friction points." ([Aspen Institute, 2025](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/blog-posts/how-do-we-get-there/))

## Failure modes seen
- **Supply without demand:** Jobseekers were willing, but only 19% had ever been asked by an employer for a digital credential ([JFF, 2024](https://www.jff.org/blog/jobseekers-want-digital-credentials-for-skill-sharing-are-employers-ready/)).
- **Unreadable record:** Hiring managers did not understand verifiable credentials or how to read their metadata ([JFF, 2024](https://jff.org/realizing-the-potential-of-learning-and-employment-records-as-verifiable-credentials-a-road-map-for-successful-demonstrations)).
- **Completion, not capability:** Credentials certified program completion rather than skills, which is too coarse for employers ([JFF, 2024](https://jff.org/realizing-the-potential-of-learning-and-employment-records-as-verifiable-credentials-a-road-map-for-successful-demonstrations)).
- **Pilot evidence as anecdote:** The flagship pilots are reported as single-learner stories with no measured outcomes ([AWPAB, 2020](https://www.commerce.gov/sites/default/files/2020-09/LERwhitepaper09222020.pdf)).
- **Perpetual pilot:** Five years after the 21-pilot count, 2025 writing still describes demonstrations and standards work, not broad use ([Aspen Institute, 2025](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/blog-posts/how-do-we-get-there/)).
- **Uneven reach:** The people least interested were mostly those without degrees and on lower incomes, the group LERs were meant to help ([JFF, 2024](https://www.jff.org/blog/jobseekers-want-digital-credentials-for-skill-sharing-are-employers-ready/)).

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- If the record holder is willing and the reader is not asking, who in The Labs' world (branch, workforce board, employer) actually asks for a record, and when?
- Does a record of *completing* a Cycle tell an outside reader anything they can use, or only a record of what was built and what skills it took?
- What would count as evidence that a reader acted on a record, rather than a story about one person?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
- We cannot see whether any pilot employer changed hiring because of LERs; the published material is narrative or qualitative. Most sources come from LER advocates and funders.
- No source gives a count of LERs issued or used nationally.
- **Rival explanation:** Low uptake may reflect a tight-then-loose labor market and the slow replacement cycle of applicant tracking systems (procurement and HR integration timing), not a flaw in the record concept. The 2020 paper itself cites COVID-19 and fast-changing technology as causes.
- Could confirm: JFF, T3 Innovation Network staff, UpSkill America, HR Open Standards, and the employers named in the 2020 pilots.
