# Record: Post-Cycle alumni survey, C01–C03 (Sep–Oct 2026)

**Bears on:** pre-cycle, cycle, post-cycle, facilitators, interfaces
**Logged:** 2026-11-06, from the survey's response sheet (internal Drive). Counts and paraphrase only. Roles, not names.

## 1. What it is
The Labs' "Post Upskilling Labs Survey," sent to alumni of Build Cycles C01 (Health Care), C02 (Energy and Climate) and C03 (Civics). Twelve responses between Sep 28 and Oct 30, 2026. Two form versions; both ask the same open questions: what you hoped to gain, what was most valuable, what you'd change, and anything else.

## 2. Where it came from
The organization's own survey, run by the organizing team. Response sheet in the shared Drive.

## 3. When
Responses Sep 28 to Oct 30, 2026, about Cycles that ran Q1 to Q3 2026.

## 4. What it says

**Who answered.** 12 alumni: 7 from C03, 3 from C02, 2 from C01. 11 of 12 joined a project team.

**Ties.** Lasting relationships made through The Labs: 3–4 people (6 respondents), 5+ (3), 1–2 (2), none (1, the person who never joined a team). Still in touch with their team: at least every other week (4), at least every other month (3), no (4).

**Would recommend (0–10):** 4, 5, 5, 7, 8, 8, 8, 9, 10, 10, 10, 10. Median 8.

**"One thing you would change."** Coded by the thresholds this pod owns. Nine of twelve answers are about wayfinding; the other three are about AI over-reliance, time, and "NA."

| Threshold | Count | What people said (paraphrased) |
|---|---|---|
| Arriving / joining | 2 | Onboarding was tricky and joining a project was not obvious; jumping into projects felt overwhelming (the one respondent who never joined a team). Improve "what to expect." |
| Knowing what's next, week to week | 3 | Needs more structure and a common goal; more guidance about what to work on in a given week; participants at times "did not really understand what to do next" and moderators should step in to get teams unstuck. |
| Forming a team / starting a project | 2 | A clearer roadmap from individual work into team pods, with roles and expectations; direct guidance at the start of projects on tools and setup, because one person's early setup didn't fit the team that formed later and course-correcting took a long time even with Labs experts. |
| Staying with the team | 1 | People constantly moving in and out of the project; no way to get people to commit. |
| Different routes for different people | 1 | Map out different pathways so people can watch from the sidelines or play different roles depending on learning style and availability. |
| Finding materials | 1 | Content from past Cycles in one central place; in C01 links came from individual trainers, not a central location, and it felt ad hoc. |

**"Most valuable."** Nearly all answers are about people: learning with peers on a focused project, a supportive and safe space, community across sectors and career levels, working with others of varying technical skill. Two name specific skills (vibe coding, app building). Nobody names a workshop by itself.

**Post-cycle routes mentioned.** Several describe new jobs, contracts or job searches; one is building a venture started in the Cycle and hopes to keep building with their Labs group. One says the most transferable skill was working with people of mixed technical ability on projects where AI was the supporting tool.

## Failure modes seen
- **Lost at the joint, not inside the room.** The three threshold clusters with the most answers are joining, week-to-week "what's next," and the move from individual work into a team.
- **One route for everyone.** A respondent asked for routes that let people watch from the sidelines or play different roles.
- **Scattered record.** Materials came from individual trainers, not one place.
- **Churn without commitment.** People moving in and out of teams with nothing holding them.

## 5. What it tells us about the problem
*Left for the pod. Questions to answer:*
- The organizers' account (#4) and this survey name the same thresholds. Do current C04 participants name them too, or has C04 already changed them?
- "Most valuable" is almost entirely people. What would it mean to make the route visible as *who* you go with, not only *what* comes next?
- Nine of twelve answers are about wayfinding. Is that because the Cycle is well designed inside and weak at the joints, or because the survey question invites process complaints?
- Four of eleven team members are no longer in touch with their team. What did the end of the Cycle look like for them?

## 6. Hearsay and limits
Twelve self-selected respondents out of several hundred people reached across three Cycles; a $50 drawing was offered. Respondents skew toward people who finished and joined teams (11 of 12). Rival explanation: people who answer alumni surveys are the ones who stayed engaged, and the ones who got lost at the first threshold never answered at all, which would mean the arriving/joining problem is undercounted here. The organizing team could confirm response rates and who was invited.
