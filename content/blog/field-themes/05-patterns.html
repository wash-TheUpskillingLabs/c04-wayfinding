Dorst’s sixth step looks for patterns across the themes. Five stood out, and each is a gap between how organizations see people and how people feel.

People are counted one at a time, but what they value is each other. Every number the field publishes is per person: a credential, a wage, a placement, a person trained. What alumni valued most was the people they built with, and the problem owner found that asking for individual contributions cut across a team’s sense of itself.4, 6 The Labs makes its value in teams; the field can only see it person by person.

Organizations draw the map from above; a person needs it from where they’re standing. Inventories, platforms and guides show everything that exists. Nobody draws “you are here,” and that is what our alumni asked for.

What everyone is short of is a person’s time. The field tries to stretch it with tools and training. The evidence keeps saying a crossing works when a person is there: reminder texts helped students get to college when a counselor answered the replies, and statewide campaigns that dropped the advising lost the effect; Wikipedia newcomers invited to its Teahouse stayed, while those met by bots left.9, 10

Places close; people keep each other anyway. Programs and grants end on a schedule. The groups that outlived their hub kept a recurring room and a name, and the relationships outlived the program.

At every door, a person is asking three questions. Will they see what I can do? Is anyone here for me? Do I belong here? The last question has been tested. In a one-hour exercise during the first year of college, students read that worries about belonging are common at first and fade with time. It raised Black students’ grades through senior year, and years later they reported more satisfaction with their careers.19 Year Up says its own assessment limits who it can serve. UDC asks for documents and a test at the door. Arlington’s lab asks only for a library card and a signed agreement.3 Inside, “not ready” has nowhere to go.6 These doors are the thresholds this pod owns.
