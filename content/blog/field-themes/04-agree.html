The five needs came from the field, not from a textbook. But two well-tested frameworks land in nearly the same place, which makes us more confident the needs are real.

Self-determination theory holds that everyone needs autonomy, competence and relatedness, and decades of studies across cultures back it.25 Not Alone and Belonging are relatedness. Believing I Can is competence, with autonomy in the words “allowed to.” Coherence is what these researchers call structure: the clear expectations and guidance that make competence possible.

Marie Jahoda studied an Austrian village whose only big employer closed in the Great Depression, and asked what work gives people besides pay. Her answer was five things: a structure to the day, regular contact with people, a sense of being useful to others, being valued, and regular activity. A 2023 meta-analysis found that people out of work report less of all five, and that each one predicts mental health on its own.24 Four line up with ours: structure is Coherence, contact is Not Alone, being useful to others is Mattering and Belonging, and being valued is Mattering. Only activity has no line of its own.

Jahoda’s work matters for Wayfinding. Between jobs, people lose all of these at once. Between Cycles, our members may lose them too: the rhythm, the people, the shared project. That’s a comparison, not a finding; we haven’t asked members what they lose when a Cycle ends. It’s worth asking.
