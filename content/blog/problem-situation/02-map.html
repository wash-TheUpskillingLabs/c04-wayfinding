Over a few weeks we collected {{total}} things people have said about this. Twelve past participants answered a survey. The folks who run The Labs told us their side. So did the steering committee and the problem owner. And we read up on 54 other programs that have wrestled with the same gap, which is why a few of the marks on the map are hollow: those are borrowed from somewhere else.

Then we sorted every statement by asking five ordinary questions. What gets in the way? Who’s involved? Where does it happen? Why? And what has actually worked, somewhere else? Each question is drawn as a line, the way the Metro draws its routes: red for where people stop, blue for what has worked elsewhere, dark for the rest. Statements that say similar things gather around a station, and the bigger a station’s name, the more people said it. Where two circles overlap, the overlap means something: a station sitting there answers both questions at once, the way an interchange sits on two Metro lines. It’s a big picture. On a phone, scroll sideways; it really wants a large screen.
