The first thing you notice is the biggest cluster in the “what” circle. We called it lost between the parts, and it holds more statements than anything else on the map. So no, it isn’t just you. It’s the most common thing people told us.

Around it sit a few that look different but rhyme. Many people are looking for the next step to be spelled out. There’s too much to keep track of. And on a team of five, the building often falls to one person while the others help in other ways.

The overlaps are where the map starts to think. Where what meets why, a complaint and its cause turn out to be the same fact: the building falls to a few because effort drops in groups when nobody can tell who did what; losing the vote means losing your team. Where why meets how solved, the cause and the fix are the same fact seen from two sides: messages only work from a known person, and the programs that put a person at the first contact are the ones that kept people. Those are the places where somebody has already found the lever.

The gaps between circles matter as much. A why with no how next to it is a problem nobody has solved for us yet: the big picture nobody has written down, a Cycle built only on volunteer hours, a Cycle that practices skills but doesn’t teach them. That empty space is the pod’s own ground.

The “how solved” circle plays by a stricter rule than the other four. Nothing in it is a wish, a plan or something we tried. Every mark there is a move one of the 54 programs made that it can show worked, with the number attached: a mentor for every few trainees cut dropout from a quarter to 2%; a text before the interview made people 55% likelier to turn up; a 23-page guide seeded 3,800 groups in seven weeks. Our own ideas stay out of that circle until they’ve earned a number of their own.

The second thing you notice is what’s missing. Almost nothing on the map comes from someone who came once and didn’t come back. Nothing comes from employers. Those are the people we most need to hear from, and we’d rather show that gap than paper over it.

One thing to keep in mind as you look: a cluster is big because more people said it, not because it matters more. The map helps us see. It doesn’t decide for us.
