The outer outline is The Upskilling Labs. For now the regional association is part of it, not a separate organization: national runs the region, and a steering committee has begun the region’s self-organization toward a chartered regional organization of its own.

People

Someone hears about us and comes to a first session. If it’s worth it, they come back, become a member, and join a Cycle. They finish with a team and a record of what they built. Some stay on as volunteers: they teach the sessions, and they take a team’s question when it’s stuck. That’s how the region got every mentor and trainer it has, and it happened without anyone designing it. The plan is to make that path deliberate, and to add one more line out of the Cycle: to employers who can read the record.

Work and code

OLOS, the Upskilling Labs app, is built and maintained by national. The region runs its own fork of it, kept current by the regional association and the steering committee, so it can change things to fit how the region works. Prototypes and merged changes from the Cycle go into the region’s fork; whatever holds up goes back to national as pull requests, so the next region starts from it. Kits and modules are planned to work the same way: written once in the commons, run in many rooms.

Knowledge

National gives the practice and the Cycle’s design. The region gives the Cycle problems worth twelve weeks; the Cycle gives back finds, paradoxes and what each team learned. Sessions give members a tool or a way of working. Mentors give help once a team is stuck, and not before; a team uses its own and its pod’s resources first. Alumni tell us what happened next. One line is planned and matters more than it looks: every Cycle participant teaches a public workshop on a skill they used, which is how what one person learned reaches the next room.

Money and support

Grants, contracts and partnerships move through The Upskilling Labs: funders and partners agree terms with national, not with the region. Most of what keeps the region going day to day is time and rooms: four to six volunteer hours a week from each person in a Cycle, and libraries that open their doors. The region plans to raise money of its own from sustaining regional members, for and through the Greater Washington brand. That money would still sit inside the national organization, in the region’s own sub-account, and it buys no standing: at The Labs, standing comes from what you contribute, not what you give. Employers in the room at the showcase are planned, not agreed.
