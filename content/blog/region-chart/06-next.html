Everything dashed on the map is a plan. Here’s the plain version.

Neighborhood workshops would let a small team in one part of the region host a session at their own library, using a kit. A kit is a session in a box: the plan for the evening, the materials, notes for whoever’s leading, and how to set up the room. A first session could then happen near you without us in the room. Salons are smaller evenings built around one question, where people who’ve done a Cycle talk with people who haven’t. Meetups are bigger, co-hosted with libraries, nonprofits, civic tech groups and employers. Summits are national’s to run, and the plan is to make them real ones in Washington, with panels, people from agencies and employers, and the Cycle’s showcase on the program.

The app, the kits and the learning modules are all meant to be built the way open-source software is. Anyone can contribute a fix or an idea. Reviewers check it, committers can merge it, and maintainers look after a part for the long haul. Those are rungs, and the first one is open to you now.

If you came once and left, or you’ve been through a Cycle and wondered what came next, we’d like to hear how it looked from where you stood. Your name stays off it.
