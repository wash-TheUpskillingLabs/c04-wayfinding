People first. The blue path runs from newcomer to session to member to Cycle to alumni. Two lines already run past the end of it, and nobody had drawn them: alumni who stay on teach the sessions, and they take a stuck team’s question. Mentors come in after a team has used up its own resources and its pod’s, never before. The plan makes that path deliberate, and adds one more line, to employers, who haven’t been asked yet. One solid line leaves the system on purpose: alumni carry a skill and a record out with them, into work the region never sees. It’s the only line on the map that says who, outside The Labs, is better off.

Work and code. National builds and maintains OLOS. The region runs a fork of it, which the steering committee keeps current. What the Cycle builds goes into the fork, and what holds up is offered upstream, where the Board decides what reaches the core. That makes two bars, ours and national’s, and the map draws them as two arrows. Kits and modules are planned to travel the same road, under the same ladder of contributor, reviewer, committer and maintainer.

Knowledge. National gives the practice and the Cycle’s design. The region gives the Cycle problems worth its time, and the Cycle gives back finds, paradoxes and what each team learned. Sessions hand members a tool or a way of working. The planned teach-back line is the one to watch: each participant teaches one public workshop on a skill they used, which is how what a team learned reaches the next room.

Money and support. Grants, contracts and partnerships would go through national, not through us. That line is dashed because we can’t yet confirm any are flowing. What the region has in hand is hours and rooms, meaning volunteer time and library space. The plan is sustaining regional members who give to Greater Washington, held in the region’s own account inside national. It’s dashed because terms aren’t agreed, and standing still comes from what you contribute.

The big outline is the open question here. The Upskilling Labs holds both levels for now, and the steering committee is working toward a chartered regional association. The map names the committee, not the association, because the committee is what exists. One thin line runs back up to national that nobody had drawn: a working regional instance, and what it costs to run. That’s the region’s main contribution to national this Cycle, and its case for more say later. Where the line between regional and national roles falls is being written this quarter, and a longer-term structure is being co-designed during this Cycle.
