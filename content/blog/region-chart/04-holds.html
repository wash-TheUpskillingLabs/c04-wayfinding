Zoom out from you. The big outline is The Upskilling Labs, the nonprofit, and it works on two levels. National keeps what every region shares: the brand, the Board, the charter, and the main copy of our app. Greater Washington, the region, runs things on the ground: the sessions, the Cycle, and the volunteers who make them happen.

Right now the region is part of the national organization, not a separate one. A steering committee is working toward turning it into an organization of its own, one that other cities could copy.

The app is called OLOS, and it’s what everything else runs on. National builds and looks after the main version. The region has its own fork, which is a copy it’s allowed to change, a bit like making your own copy of a shared doc so you can edit freely. When a change in our copy works, we offer it back, and the national Board decides whether it goes into the main version. That’s why the thick dark lines between those boxes run both ways.

Money moves differently. Grants and partnerships go through the national organization. Libraries lend rooms and volunteers give hours, and that’s most of what makes a session happen in any given week. We also plan to let people become sustaining members of Greater Washington, with their gifts held in the region’s own account inside national. That’s dashed because it’s a plan. A gift won’t buy a bigger role, since higher levels here come from what you contribute.
