The dashed lines are the plan. Most of it is in the two things the region runs: places to gather, and a commons of OLOS, modules and kits. Neither is running in full yet. Both are aimed at the places people leave.

Where people gather

Neighborhood workshops. A neighborhood team hosts a workshop in person, at their own library, run from a kit, so a first session can happen anywhere in the region without waiting on us. Salons are smaller: an evening around one question, where people who’ve been through a Cycle meet people who haven’t. Meetups are planned as multi-organization events from the start, co-hosted with civic tech groups, libraries, nonprofits and employers. Summits are national’s to run, and they become real DC-style summits, with panels, partners, agencies and employers in the room, and the Cycle’s showcase on the program. That’s the first answer to after: put the people who could read the record in the room where it’s shown.

The commons: the region’s OLOS, modules and kits

Kits are modular programmatic kits: a session in a box, with the agenda, materials, facilitator notes and setup, so a stranger can run it. Learning modules are what a training teaches, written once and taught by any trainer. OLOS, the modules and the kits all take an open contribution model, the way open-source projects do: anyone can be a contributor by opening an issue or a pull request; reviewers are trusted to say what’s ready; committers can merge; maintainers keep a part healthy over time and carry it upstream to national. OLOS itself, the Upskilling Labs app, is maintained by national; the region runs a fork of it, which the steering committee and the regional association keep current and send changes back from. Modules and kits will have maintainers of their own.

That’s the second answer to after: a role that keeps you in. The mentor program being designed now is the third: a bench, a way to ask, and a way to say yes. A pod’s first issue is already a contribution; a project’s merged change is already a commit. The ladder just names what people are doing and gives them the next rung.
