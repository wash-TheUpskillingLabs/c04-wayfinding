Where people gather

Neighborhood workshops. A team of neighbours hosts a workshop in person, at their own library, run from a kit, so a first session can happen anywhere in the region without waiting on us. Salons are smaller: an evening around one question, where people who’ve been through a Cycle meet people who haven’t. Meetups grow from our own nights into multi-organization events, co-hosted with civic tech groups, libraries, nonprofits and employers. Summits become real DC-style summits, with panels, partners, agencies and employers in the room, and the Cycle’s showcase on the program. That’s the first answer to after: put the people who could read the record in the room where it’s shown.

The commons: OLOS, modules and kits

Kits are modular programmatic kits: a session in a box, with the agenda, materials, facilitator notes and setup, so a stranger can run it. Learning modules are what a training teaches, written once and taught by any trainer. OLOS, the modules and the kits all take an open contribution model, the way open-source projects do: anyone can be a contributor by opening an issue or a pull request; reviewers are trusted to say what’s ready; committers can merge; maintainers keep a part healthy over time and carry it upstream to national.

That’s the second answer to after: a role that keeps you in. A pod’s first issue is already a contribution; a project’s merged change is already a commit. The ladder just names what people are doing and gives them the next rung.
