About ten text messages over a summer helped admitted students actually show up to college, in the first trials, where a counselor stood behind every message. Scaled statewide and nationally, the effect mostly vanished. The researcher’s own best guess: the advising was dropped, and students didn’t know or trust the sender.3

Wikipedia learned the same thing from the other side. Its newcomers were turned away by semi-automated tools, while newcomers invited into the Teahouse, a help space run by people, stayed at higher rates.4

The message was never the point. A notification is a very cheap way of saying “someone here knows you exist,” and it only works if that’s true.
