Read it like the Metro. The blue line is the journey, left to right, and dashed means planned and not running yet. Red rings are where people leave, the arcs above are people skipping ahead or coming back, and the outlined columns are the Cycle.

Inside the Cycle, the line works like track that several lines share. When proposals are open, they run side by side as a bundle: problems, then paradoxes, then frames, then projects. At a vote, some proposals end at a terminus bar and the chosen ones branch off. Vote 1 sends pods off on their own lines, each in its own colour, and Vote 2 sends projects off inside each pod in that pod’s colour. As on the Metro, the other pods run green and yellow beside it; blue is yours, and silver is a proposal that wasn’t chosen. The colours were picked so that people with any common kind of colour blindness can still tell every line apart, and each line also carries a letter, a position and a thin dark edge, so colour is never the only clue. Like Metro’s service discs, each pod’s line also carries a letter, so you can follow it without telling colours apart. And like a Metro line, each way out is named for where it ends: the names come from what alumni told us they went on to do. Every project comes back together at the showcase. How many pods and projects form is decided in the Cycle, not in advance, so the map draws a few and the dots say there may be more, or fewer.

The bottom row quotes the pod’s statements: A is the alumni survey of the first three Cycles, O is organizers. Where it says nothing heard yet, we haven’t asked anyone who was there.

Flags above the line are landmarks: the moments people remember, like Kickoff, Sensemaking Day and the Showcase, with this Cycle’s dates beside them. Use Where Are You? to pick your step. A red ring marks it, and only your step and the next one stay at full strength, so you can see what’s ahead without reading the whole map.
