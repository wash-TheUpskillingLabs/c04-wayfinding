Read it like the Metro. The blue line is the journey, left to right, and dashed means planned and not running yet. Red rings are where people leave, the arcs above are people skipping ahead or coming back, and the outlined columns are the Cycle.

Inside the Cycle, the line works like track that several lines share. When proposals are open, they run side by side as a bundle. The map narrows them in two ways. At a vote, some proposals end at a terminus bar and the chosen ones branch off: Vote 1 sends pods off on their own lines, each in its own colour. In sensemaking, nothing ends; lines merge onto one track, the way two Metro lines join. Inside each pod that happens twice: many paradox proposals merge into a few paradoxes, and under each paradox, many frame proposals merge into a few frames. At Vote 2 each frame is offered as a project, and the ones people register for go on to build. A few problems can become many projects this way, and the map leaves the counts open. As on the Metro, the other pods run green and yellow beside it; blue is yours, and silver is a proposal that wasn’t chosen. Each pod is a family of colour: its paradoxes branch in shades of it, one keeping the pod’s colour and one a darker or lighter shade, and each is named like a branch of a Paris RER line, A1 and A2. Frames and projects keep their paradox’s shade, so you can trace any project at the showcase back to the paradox it came from. The colours were picked so that people with any common kind of colour blindness can still tell lines apart where they run side by side, and each line also carries a letter and a position, so colour is never the only clue. Like Metro’s service discs, each pod’s line also carries a letter, so you can follow it without telling colours apart. And like a Metro line, each way out is named for where it ends: the names come from what alumni told us they went on to do. Every project comes back together at the showcase. How many pods and projects form is decided in the Cycle, not in advance, so the map draws a few and the dots say there may be more, or fewer.

The bottom row quotes the pod’s statements: A is the alumni survey of the first three Cycles, O is organizers. Where it says nothing heard yet, we haven’t asked anyone who was there.

Flags above the line are landmarks: the moments people remember, like Kickoff, Sensemaking Day and the Showcase, with this Cycle’s dates beside them. Use Where Are You? to pick your step. A red ring marks it, and only your step and the next one stay at full strength, so you can see what’s ahead without reading the whole map.
