Most people start at step 4. Today the Cycle is the front door, so the arc from hearing of us jumps straight to it, past the sessions and the prep. Organizers said it plainly: the Cycle is the point of entry. The plan is for the Cycle to be something you build up to.

Before the Cycle, there isn’t a path yet. Trainings, workshops, open labs and meetups happen in any order, and nothing helps you see where you’ve got to. So the map draws them as a loop, not a line. You can join anywhere and go round as often as you like, and there’s a suggested way round: learn a tool, work a problem with others, build together, then share what you found and meet people. That’s the Learn, Build, Share spiral, one small turn at a time, and a Cycle is several turns at once. The planned record under the loop would stamp each session, so even a messy path makes sense looking back.

The complaints pile up inside the Cycle. Nine of the seventeen quotes sit in steps 4 to 6, and nearly all of them ask the same thing: what am I meant to do now? Joining a pod, then a team, is where people lose the thread. That is the pod’s problem, stated by the people who lived it. Line the quotes up under the map and they sit at the votes, where proposals end and lines branch. People get lost where the group makes a choice, and each choice leaves more lines, not fewer: after Vote 1 you pick a pod, after Vote 2 a project inside it. That is when an alumnus asks for “a clearer roadmap from individual work into team pods.”

Two steps have no voice at all. Nobody has told us how they first heard of us, or what the showcase felt like. And the survey only reached people who stayed, so the red rings come from what others saw of people leaving, never from anyone who left.

The arcs on the right are the hopeful part. Alumni come back to teach a session or take a stuck team’s question. That loop runs today without anyone running it.
