# Wayfinding

**How does an adaptive, self-directed experience stay legible to the people in it, and to the people deciding whether to back it?**

## What this is

This is one of three Research Pods in [**Build Cycle C04**](https://github.com/wash-TheUpskillingLabs/c04), run by The Upskilling Labs of Greater Washington. A Build Cycle is six weeks in which a group of neighbors picks a hard problem, researches it properly, and builds something real in response. This Cycle, for the first time, the problem is The Labs itself. This pod took one piece of it: the question above.

We use **frame creation**, a research method from designer Kees Dorst (*Frame Innovation*, 2015). The idea is simple. Hard problems stay hard because everyone is looking at them the same way. If you study the problem carefully, you find a **paradox**: two things that are both true and pull against each other. Then you look for a **frame**: a new way of seeing the problem that makes the paradox easier to move. Only then do you build.

The first two weeks are research. On Nov 7 we write the paradox and frame the problem. The next week we test frames with rapid prototypes. On Nov 14 the room votes on project proposals. The last three weeks we build. By **Dec 8**, each approved project ships one working change to how The Labs runs, built on OLOS, used by real people, and written up.

We own what someone can tell from outside the room, or later. What happens in the room is [Scaffolding](https://github.com/wash-TheUpskillingLabs/c04-scaffolding). We read each other's repos every week.

## Why we build on OLOS

OLOS is The Labs' open-source platform: the software a lab runs on. The Upskilling App at theupskillinglabs.org is the national organization's copy of it, less than a year old. Greater Washington is setting up its own copy for this Cycle. We change ours to fit how the region works, and anything that holds up goes back to the main OLOS project as a pull request, so the next region starts from what we learned. Research The Labs, not the app. Build on OLOS, not around the app's limits.

## The nine steps, and when

Dorst's steps in his words (Dorst & Kokotovich, 2015), and what this pod does for each.

| Step | Dorst | Here | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Archaeology | "Analyzing the history of the problem owner and of the initial problem formulation." | Who first called this a problem, in what words, and what they wanted. What's been tried, here and anywhere, and how it went. What's treated as non-negotiable, listed so we can question it. | Now to Nov 7 |
| 2. Paradox | "Analyzing the problem situation: what makes this hard?" | Written as *because* statements: *X needs A, because… Y needs B, because… Both are true.* From the evidence, not Saturday's hunches. Then **set aside**. Frames come from the themes, not from staring at the paradox. | Nov 7 |
| 3. Context | "Analyzing the inner circle of stakeholders." | Two circles. Inner: who affects this problem today. Outer: who would have to be part of any solution. What each has tried, and how each sees it. | Now to Nov 7 |
| 4. Field | "Exploring the broader societal field." | Everyone around this problem who isn't in the room: libraries, employers, funders, agencies, universities, the next cohort. Their values, their pressures, how they decide. Not just who they are: what they're after. | Now to Nov 13 |
| 5. Themes | "Investigating the themes that emerge in the broader field." | The deeper motivations that keep showing up across players: what people are really trying to get, in their words. Themes are human, not technical. | Nov 7 |
| 6. Frames | "Identifying patterns in the themes to create frames." | Each builder in residence proposes one frame, written as *If we treat this problem as if it were a problem of [theme], then…*, Ideated on Nov 7, from the themes. | Nov 7 |
| 7. Futures | "Exploring the possible outcomes and value propositions for the various stakeholders." | Each frame gets a rapid prototype on GitHub, Vercel and Supabase, put in front of three real people. What they do with it decides whether the frame holds. Project proposals written from the results. | Nov 8 to 13 |
| 8. Transformation | "Investigate the change in stakeholders' strategies and practices required for implementation." | Proposals pitched and approved Nov 14. Then build the change on the region's OLOS. Release weekly to real people. Change it from what they do. | Nov 14 to Dec 5 |
| 9. Integration | "Draw lessons from the new approach and identify new opportunities within the network." | In use, someone keeps it, written up, taught back, pull request to OLOS. | Dec 8 |

Budget **4 to 6 hours of your own time a week**, not counting what your coding agent does. If a week needs more, cut scope.

Most of us do this on nights and weekends, so **weekly work is due Monday night**, after the weekend. The exceptions are **group days**, when the whole Cycle meets: work for a group day is due when the day starts.

**Open Lab Nights.** The Cycle votes on which weeknight(s) The Labs keeps open for working together. Pods can hold their weekly meeting there.

## What we're collecting now (archaeology, due at the start of Sat Nov 7)

Archaeology means finding out how this became a problem and what has already been tried. **Not just by us.** Anyone, anywhere, who has faced this problem counts. Three kinds of evidence:

| Kind | What it is | What it must include |
|---|---|---|
| **Interview** | A conversation with one person who has lived this problem, here or elsewhere. Two kinds of questions. **History:** *Tell me about the last time this went wrong. What happened? What did you try? What did you assume? Who else should I talk to?* **Motivation:** *What would a good year here look like for you? Why does that matter? And why does that matter?* Keep asking why. The history feeds archaeology. The motivation feeds themes. | Their role (not their name), when you spoke, what they said in their words, what they're after, what it tells us |
| **Case study** | One organization or program, anywhere, that tried to solve something like this | Who they are, what they tried, what happened, how we know, what we can't tell from outside |
| **Record** | Something that already exists: a document, a dataset, a past decision, a survey count | What it is, where it lives, when it's from, what it says |

**Where to look first**

- **Other programs** that show progress without freezing it: open badges, portfolio schools, community programs that report to funders. What do they show, to whom, and does it work?
- **Our own record.** Every pin, portfolio, credential and report The Labs has produced. What funders and library hosts asked for, and what we sent back.
- **How this became a problem here.** Who first said progress and belonging were hard to see, and what they were looking at.
- **The survey.** Every response behind the "seeing" theme. Count them. Quote them.

**How to turn it in**

One GitHub issue per piece of evidence. Title it `Interview:`, `Case:` or `Record:` and a few words. In the body, answer these in order:

1. **What it is.** One or two sentences.
2. **Where it came from.** The role of the person, the name of the organization, or the location of the document.
3. **When.** The date of the conversation, or the date of the thing.
4. **What it says.** Facts and quotes. Keep your opinion separate.
5. **What it tells us about the problem.** Your read, labeled as your read.
6. **Is any of this hearsay?** If so, who could confirm it.

**No names, recordings or transcripts in this repo.** Write "a former mentor," not who. Keep raw notes on your own computer. This repo is public.

## Context and field maps

Alongside archaeology, the pod maps **who is in this problem**. Dorst calls it two steps:

- **Context** is the inner circle: the players at The Labs who affect this problem today, or who would have to be part of any solution.
- **Field** is the wider world: everyone around the problem who isn't in the room. Libraries, employers, funders, agencies, universities, other programs, the next cohort.

Both maps live in one file, `research/players.md`, with a diagram of the two circles at the top. [**Build or update the maps with Claude**](https://claude.ai/code?repositories=wash-TheUpskillingLabs%2Fc04-wayfinding&prompt=I%27m+in+the+Wayfinding+pod+of+Build+Cycle+C04.+Read+README.md+first%2C+then+every+issue+and+file+in+research%2F.+Help+me+build+or+update+our+context+and+field+maps+in+research%2Fplayers.md%3A+one+entry+per+player+in+the+format+the+README+gives%2C+split+into+Context+%28inner+circle%29+and+Field+%28wider+world%29.+Ask+me+about+any+player+I+add.+Then+draw+both+circles+as+a+Mermaid+diagram+at+the+top+of+the+file.+Use+roles+and+organization+names%2C+never+individual+people%27s+names.+Then+open+a+pull+request.)

**One entry per player** (a role or an organization, never a named individual):

1. **Player and type.** For example "Library branch staff (institution)" or "A first-time upskiller (person)".
2. **Who they are, and where people meet them.**
3. **Context or field?**
4. **What they bring.** Which community capitals (natural, cultural, human, social, political, financial, built), and how much.
5. **What they want.** What they're really after, in their words if you have them. This feeds the themes.
6. **What they've tried** about this problem, and how it went.
7. **How they see the problem.** Their frame, as best you can tell.

Start with who you know, then let every interview add players: ask each person *who else should I talk to?*

| **A longer write-up** | A file in `research/` | Case studies, interview notes (no names), records with numbers. | **A rapid prototype** (after Nov 2) | A folder in `prototypes/` | [**Start a prototype with Claude**](https://claude.ai/code?repositories=wash-TheUpskillingLabs%2Fc04-wayfinding&prompt=I%27m+a+builder+in+the+Wayfinding+pod+of+Build+Cycle+C04.+Read+README.md+first.+Help+me+make+a+rapid+prototype%3A+a+quick%2C+rough+probe+to+show+people+so+we+learn+how+they+react+to+our+problem+situation.+Ask+me+what+I+want+to+learn+and+who+I%27ll+show+it+to.+Then+build+it+as+a+single+page+at+prototypes%2F%3Cshortname%3E%2Findex.html+and+add+a+short+prototypes%2F%3Cshortname%3E%2FREADME.md+saying+what+it+tests+and+what+we+learned.+Use+only+made-up+data%3A+no+real+names%2C+emails%2C+addresses+or+quotes+that+identify+anyone.+Then+open+a+pull+request.) |

**Archaeology first.** This week is for finding out how the problem came to be: interviews, case studies, records. Don't build yet. You can't probe a problem you haven't started to understand.

**Then rapid prototypes, as research tools.** Once you have your first finds (after Mon Nov 2), a rough prototype helps you go deeper: show it to someone and watch what they do. A clickable page, a fake form, a mock dashboard. Made in an hour with Claude, thrown away without regret. Each one gets a short README: what it tests, which finds it came from, who saw it, what we learned. These are probes, not solutions. The solution comes after the paradox.

Prototypes here use **made-up data only**. Once the region's OLOS instance is up, prototypes that need real accounts or a database move there.

To use the links, sign in at [claude.ai/code](https://claude.ai/code) with your Claude account and connect GitHub once.

**Project proposal format.** One issue titled `Proposal:` and the project name. Answer in order:

1. **The frame.** One sentence, in the *If we treat this as if…* form.
2. **The paradox it moves.** Copy it from the README. Say which side gives.
3. **What the prototype showed.** Who tried it (roles, not names), what they did, what surprised you.
4. **What we'll build by Dec 8.** One change to how The Labs works that a real person uses. Small enough for three weeks at 4 to 6 hours a week.
5. **Who's on it.** You, and the contributors you've recruited so far.
6. **Who keeps it in January.**
7. **What goes upstream to OLOS.**

## Projects

After Nov 14, each builder in residence leads one project and recruits a team. A project is a change to how The Labs works that a real person uses, built on the region's OLOS and sent to the main project as a pull request. Code lives in the region's OLOS fork on a branch named `c04-wayfinding-projectname`, no hyphens inside the project name (for example, `c04-wayfinding-journeymap`). This repo gets a branch of the same name holding the project's README. `main` holds the pod's research.
